\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols use on-chain wallet reputation to screen counterparties, set risk parameters and detect automated or malicious behavior. A recent graph method, the Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) of Do, Do and Nguyen (2023), ranks wallets on the graph of token transfers. Transfers record payments and not trust. Every ERC-20 token also records a more explicit act, the allowance, by which an owner authorizes a spender to move tokens on the owner's behalf. This thesis asks whether adding allowance edges to PageRank improves on the transfer-only AWP: in what the score measures, in how well it anticipates later behavior, in what it costs to compute, and when both edge types share one walk.

EndorseRank is PageRank on the directed graph of the latest non-zero ERC-20 allowances between addresses. An allowance points from the owner to the spender, so EndorseRank ranks the spenders that receive approvals, such as routers and vaults, and not the owners that grant them. The coupled PageRank (C-PR) mixes the allowance and transfer rows of each node with weight $\lambda$; $\lambda=1$ gives EndorseRank and $\lambda=0$ gives AWP. The endorsement-seeded PageRank (S-PR) restarts the transfer walk from the EndorseRank distribution. Under a quantitative, positivist design, all scores are computed for the same $n=5{,}521$ GMX~V2 trading wallets on Arbitrum One from 1~December~2025 to 31~May~2026 and compared with Kendall's $\tau$ and a paired wallet bootstrap. The main test is a temporal holdout. Scores frozen on 28~February~2026 are set against new owner--spender approvals and new transfer senders counted from March to May~2026, on the $1{,}335$ spenders that held an approval at the freeze, with each wallet's raw degree at the freeze as a baseline. The value of $\lambda$ and a first rule for C-PR were fixed on 14~September~2026, before any coupled score was computed. A second rule, for C-PR against AWP alone, was registered on 28~September~2026 together with a new window: scores frozen on 31~May~2026 and labels counted from June to August~2026, among them the liquidation-free close rate of the matched traders.

On the spring holdout the allowance edge carries information that the transfer edge lacks. EndorseRank ranks the spenders that later gain new approvals at $\tau=0.479$ (95\% CI $[0.427,0.529]$) against $0.044$ for AWP, and it also ranks new transfer senders better ($0.372$ against $0.270$); both contrasts were fixed before the run. The gain lies in the edge and not in the walk: the spender's approval count at the freeze ranks new approvals better than EndorseRank ($\tau=0.711$), and the transfer in-degree ranks new senders better than AWP ($0.472$), so AWP is a weak reference point. C-PR at $\lambda=0.5$ fails the first rule, which asked it to match EndorseRank on new approvals ($\Delta\tau=-0.184$, interval $[-0.213,-0.149]$) and to beat AWP on new senders ($+0.007$, interval $[-0.012,0.028]$). Against AWP alone it gains $0.251$ on new approvals (interval $[0.203,0.306]$) with no detectable loss on new senders. This gain was noticed only after the labels were in, so the second rule tests it on the June--August window: \freshpending{registered result}. S-PR behaves like AWP, so allowance edges help when the walk moves along them and not when they only set the restart. In the same window, AWP aligns more closely with transfer statistics ($\tau=0.534$ against $0.333$) and EndorseRank with allowance statistics ($0.355$ against $-0.030$), but EndorseRank separates only 122 of the traders from two tied values, so these figures rest on few wallets and on which wallets made any approval. Scored as isolated nodes instead of zero, the traders outside the allowance graph would turn EndorseRank's transfer coefficient negative ($-0.071$). The allowance graph has about a ninth of the transfer graph's edges, and its PageRank solve is about ten times faster ($0.40$ against $4.11$ seconds).

The thesis contributes an allowance edge for on-chain reputation, an out-of-window comparison with the transfer baseline and its degree counts, and a coupled walk tested under rules fixed before its scores were computed, with a registered replication on new data. For a protocol that needs to know which spenders owners trust, the approval count is the cheapest useful signal and EndorseRank its propagated form. Both scores come from public logs, so anyone can recompute and audit them. EndorseRank does not rank the traders who grant approvals, and in an exploratory run AWP was ahead of it on counts of their later profitable closes, while neither score ranked the rate. Under uniform teleportation, a farm of ten addresses can lift its target to 7.7 times the highest EndorseRank among the traders.
