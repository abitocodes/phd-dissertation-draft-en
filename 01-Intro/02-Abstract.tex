\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols use on-chain wallet reputation to screen counterparties and set risk parameters. Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) ranks wallets on their token transfers, but a transfer records a payment, not trust. ERC-20 tokens also record allowances, by which an owner authorizes a spender to move its tokens. This thesis asks what allowance edges add to PageRank as a reputation score.

EndorseRank is PageRank on the graph of the latest non-zero allowances, with AWP's edge weights. It ranks the spenders that receive approvals, not the owners that grant them. A coupled PageRank (C-PR) walks both edge types at once. All scores, raw degrees included, are computed for the same $5{,}521$ GMX~V2 traders on Arbitrum One and compared with Kendall's $\tau$ and paired bootstrap intervals. Scores frozen on 28~February~2026 are tested against new approvals and new transfer senders on $1{,}335$ spenders, and a June--August~2026 window was registered before its logs were extracted.

Out of window no PageRank ranks a label as well as the raw degree of the same edge type: the approval count ranks later approvals at $\tau=0.711$ against $0.394$ for EndorseRank, and the transfer in-degree likewise beats AWP on new senders. A closed form explains the gap: on this one-hop ego-network sample the allowance walk has depth one, and EndorseRank reduces to an owner-normalized in-strength, which discounts owners that approve many spenders; multi-hop propagation is untested. C-PR meets neither its first rule nor its registered rule. In pre-specified post hoc analyses of a trader outcome built from neither edge type, later liquidation avoidance, the approval count ranked traders better than the transfer count in both windows ($\Delta\tau=+0.150$ and $+0.153$, 97.5\% intervals above zero). The PageRank versions point the same way but are not resolved. Most approval-receiving traders are contract accounts, so account type is an unresolved confound, and the signal is lower on the share of profitable or non-losing closes: it ranks forced-liquidation avoidance, not trading success. EndorseRank solves about nine times faster than AWP because its graph has about nine times fewer edges.

The thesis contributes the allowance as a reputation edge, an out-of-window evaluation of every score against the counts it smooths, a closed-form account of EndorseRank on a depth-one graph, and a coupled walk tested under rules fixed in advance and reported as failed. The scores are auditable from public logs, but a ten-address farm can lift its target to 7.7 times the cohort's highest EndorseRank, so none should decide credit or rewards without Sybil controls.

\emph{Keywords---} DeFi, EndorseRank, Adaptive Weighted PageRank, coupled PageRank, on-chain reputation, ERC-20 allowance, temporal holdout, pre-registration, Arbitrum One, Kendall's tau
