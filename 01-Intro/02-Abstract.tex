\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols increasingly rely on wallet-level reputation to gate integrations, calibrate risk parameters and screen for automated or adversarial behaviour. Existing on-chain reputation methods, exemplified by the transfer-graph Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) of Do, Do and Nguyen (2023), aggregate historical token transfers. That design is costly to refresh on a rollup that emits millions of transfers per month, and transfer volume measures economic throughput rather than an explicit act of trust. The problem addressed in this thesis is whether a cheaper endorsement signal that already exists on every EVM chain, the ERC-20 allowance an owner grants to a spender, can support a reputation score that is operationally interpretable, computationally light and empirically checkable against public ledger data.

The thesis introduces EndorseRank, PageRank computed on a directed endorsement graph whose edges are the latest non-zero ERC-20 allowances between wallets, and asks whether the allowance and transfer layers can be combined to advantage. The combination is a coupled PageRank (C-PR) whose transition row at each wallet is a convex mixture of the two layers with weight $\lambda$, so that $\lambda=1$ recovers EndorseRank and $\lambda=0$ recovers AWP on a shared node set; an endorsement-seeded PageRank (S-PR), a transfer walk restarted from the EndorseRank distribution, is the ablation. Following a quantitative, positivist design, all methods are compared with a faithful re-implementation of AWP on the same matched cohort of $n=5{,}521$ Arbitrum One wallets over the window 1~December~2025 to 31~May~2026. Alignment with contemporaneous transfer, allowance and Sybil-stability statistics is measured with Kendall's $\tau$; uncertainty is quantified with a paired wallet bootstrap (400 resamples) for every coefficient and for pre-specified differences between coefficients. A temporal holdout scores wallets on events up to 28~February~2026 and tests the scores against two labels that post-date every scored event: owner--spender approvals that first appear in March--May~2026 and transfer senders that first appear in the same months, with the raw degree at the freeze date as the baseline for each. The value of $\lambda$ and the rule for what would count as an improvement were written into the versioned configuration before the coupled scores were computed. Runtime is measured under a single timing protocol (one warm-up, five timed solves), and node-count sensitivity is examined on an expanded pool of $N=99{,}080$ addresses.

Four results follow. First, EndorseRank solves PageRank in 0.40\,s against 4.11\,s for AWP on the same cohort (about 10 times faster), because its graph has 14,727 edges against 137,087; the ratio stays between 8.1 and 12.2 across staged node counts. The coupled operator costs as much as AWP, since it walks on both edge sets. Second, the two methods measure different constructs: AWP aligns more strongly with transfer proxies ($\tau=0.534$ against 0.333 for EndorseRank), whereas EndorseRank aligns with allowance proxies ($\tau=0.355$, 95\% CI 0.322--0.384) where AWP is slightly negative ($-0.030$); the inter-method agreement is $\tau=0.316$. EndorseRank's allowance alignment is not statistically higher than its own transfer alignment, so the allowance result is read as an intended-construct check, not as an external test. Third, on the holdout spender cohort ($n=1{,}335$) EndorseRank ranks wallets that later receive new approvals at $\tau=0.479$ (95\% CI 0.426--0.529) while AWP is near zero (0.044); the in-approve degree at the freeze date, however, reaches $\tau=0.711$, and the paired difference to EndorseRank lies below zero. AWP is likewise below the transfer in-degree on new senders. Neither PageRank adds out-of-window information to its own raw count on this cohort. Fourth, C-PR at $\lambda=0.5$ fails the pre-registered rule on both parts: it is worse than EndorseRank on new approvals ($\Delta\tau=-0.184$, interval $[-0.213,-0.149]$) and indistinguishable from AWP on new senders ($\Delta\tau=+0.007$, interval $[-0.012,0.028]$); S-PR reproduces AWP. Damping, top-20-token and sample-definition checks do not reverse these patterns.

The comparison is the first same-cohort evaluation of an allowance-based and a transfer-based PageRank that reports confidence intervals, difference tests, a two-label temporal holdout with degree baselines, a pre-registered test of a coupled operator and a versioned pipeline with committed summaries. A reputation score can be refreshed at roughly one tenth of the cost of the transfer-graph baseline and captures a trust act that the baseline misses, but its predictive content about future endorsement is contained in the spender's approval count, and mixing the two edge types in one walk interpolates between the single-layer scores rather than exceeding them. For protocol operators, the practical reading is that the allowance count is the cheap signal for approval-related screening, EndorseRank is the propagated version of that count, and the transfer graph should be consulted separately when payment flow is the question. EndorseRank does not outperform AWP on transfer-hub detection. Its Sybil resistance depends on the economic cost of manufacturing approvals, which is modelled but not measured here.
