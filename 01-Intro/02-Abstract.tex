\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols increasingly rely on wallet-level reputation to gate integrations, calibrate risk parameters and screen for automated or adversarial behaviour. Existing on-chain reputation methods, exemplified by the transfer-graph Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) of Do, Do and Nguyen (2023), aggregate historical token transfers. That design is costly to refresh on a rollup that emits millions of transfers per month, and transfer volume measures economic throughput rather than an explicit act of trust. The problem addressed in this thesis is whether a cheaper endorsement signal that already exists on every EVM chain, the ERC-20 allowance an owner grants to a spender, can support a reputation score that is operationally interpretable, computationally light and empirically checkable against public ledger data.

The thesis introduces EndorseRank, PageRank computed on a directed endorsement graph whose edges are the latest non-zero ERC-20 allowances between wallets. Following a quantitative, positivist design, EndorseRank is compared with a faithful re-implementation of AWP on the same matched cohort of $n=5{,}521$ Arbitrum One wallets that have non-zero connectivity in both graphs over the window 1~December~2025 to 31~May~2026. Alignment with contemporaneous transfer, allowance and Sybil-stability statistics is measured with Kendall's $\tau$; uncertainty is quantified with a paired wallet bootstrap (400 resamples) for every coefficient and for six pre-specified differences between coefficients. A temporal holdout scores wallets on events up to 28~February~2026 and tests the scores against owner--spender approvals that first appear in March--May~2026. Runtime is measured under a single timing protocol (one warm-up, five timed solves), and node-count sensitivity is examined on an expanded pool of $N=99{,}080$ addresses.

Three results follow. First, EndorseRank solves PageRank in 1.43\,s against 11.25\,s for AWP on the same cohort (about 7.9 times faster), because its graph has 14,727 edges against 137,087; the ratio stays between 7.9 and 10.5 across staged node counts. Second, the two methods measure different constructs: AWP aligns more strongly with transfer proxies ($\tau=0.534$ against 0.333 for EndorseRank), whereas EndorseRank aligns with allowance proxies ($\tau=0.355$, 95\% CI 0.322--0.384) where AWP is slightly negative ($-0.030$); the inter-method agreement is $\tau=0.316$. EndorseRank's allowance alignment is not statistically higher than its own transfer alignment, so the allowance result is read as an intended-construct check, not as an external test. Third, on the holdout spender cohort ($n=1{,}335$) EndorseRank ranks wallets that later receive new approvals at $\tau=0.479$ (95\% CI 0.426--0.529) while AWP is near zero (0.044). Damping, top-20-token and sample-definition checks do not reverse these patterns.

The comparison is the first same-cohort evaluation of an allowance-based and a transfer-based PageRank that reports confidence intervals, difference tests, a temporal holdout and a versioned pipeline. A reputation score can be refreshed at roughly one eighth of the cost of the transfer-graph baseline and still capture a trust act that the baseline misses. Cheaper, more frequently refreshed authorization-aware scores lower the barrier to risk screening that today favours well-resourced actors. For protocol operators and regulators, an auditable score built on public approvals is a transparent complement to opaque proprietary risk engines. EndorseRank does not outperform AWP on transfer-hub detection. Its Sybil resistance depends on the economic cost of manufacturing approvals, which is modelled but not measured here.

\emph{Keywords---} DeFi, EndorseRank, Adaptive Weighted PageRank, on-chain reputation, ERC-20 allowance, temporal holdout, Arbitrum One, Kendall's tau
