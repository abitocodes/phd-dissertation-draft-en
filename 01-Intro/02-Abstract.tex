\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

A rollup holds many smart contracts, and wallets, explorers and auditors must choose which to examine first. PageRank reputation scores for ledgers walk over transfers among all addresses and ignore the ERC-20 allowance, which lets one contract spend another's tokens. This thesis asks whether one PageRank walk over allowance and transfer edges between contracts on Arbitrum One finds the contracts that users rely on sooner, and predicts their later use better, than single-layer walks and raw counts.

Its method, coupled PageRank (\mbox{C-PR}), mixes each contract's allowance and transfer rows in one walk; its references are Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) on transfers and EndorseRank on allowances. After the registered results were known, the study defined this analysis: graphs of contracts without user accounts; each allowance counted up to its owner's balance; amounts in US dollars on five tokens; scores from the twelve months before each freeze; and a discovery-order test, which measures how soon a crawl ordered by a score reaches the contracts with the most user-account demand. Out of window, it ranks $7{,}864$ and $7{,}915$ spender contracts against their new approval pairs and transfer senders.

The discovery-order test answers no: in its deciding run the crawl ordered by \mbox{C-PR} reached those contracts sooner than a breadth-first crawl (difference in discovery-curve area $+0.0520$ $[+0.0485,+0.0558]$ on inflow) but later than one ordered by contract backlinks ($-0.0051$ $[-0.0087,-0.0010]$). Rule~A is not met: \mbox{C-PR} ranks approval pairs below its allowance layer ($\Delta\tau=-0.025$ $[-0.045,-0.004]$) and senders below its transfer layer ($-0.014$ $[-0.019,-0.008]$). Rule~B is not met: the thesis applies two of its registered criteria, F1 and F2, and under the registration the rule holds only if each of its criteria holds; \mbox{C-PR} gains on approval pairs over its transfer layer ($+0.074$ $[0.060,0.087]$) but loses more than the margin on senders ($-0.016$ $[-0.022,-0.012]$). It also ranks senders below AWP and the transfer in-degree.

The allowance layer between contracts is thin. Counts and walks can serve as a first filter among contracts; no score reads a contract's code, and the thesis tests none for credit decisions.

\emph{Key terms:} coupled PageRank; smart contract; crawl ordering; on-chain reputation; ERC-20 allowance; EndorseRank; Adaptive Weighted PageRank; temporal holdout; Arbitrum One; Kendall's tau
