\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols use on-chain wallet reputation to screen counterparties, set risk parameters and detect automated or malicious behavior. A recent graph method, the Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) of Do, Do and Nguyen (2023), ranks wallets on the graph of their transactions. Transfers record payments and not trust. Every ERC-20 token also records a more explicit act, the allowance, by which an owner authorizes a spender to move tokens on the owner's behalf. This thesis asks whether adding allowance edges to PageRank improves on the transfer-only AWP: in what the score measures, in how well it anticipates later behavior, in what it costs to compute, and when both edge types share one walk.

EndorseRank is PageRank on the directed graph of the latest non-zero ERC-20 allowances between addresses, with AWP's edge weights: each latest approval counts about once, discounted by its age, and the walk restarts uniformly. An allowance points from the owner to the spender, so EndorseRank ranks the spenders that receive approvals, such as routers and vaults, and not the owners that grant them. The coupled PageRank (C-PR) mixes the allowance and transfer rows of each node with weight $\lambda$, on two layers weighted by raw amounts; $\lambda=1$ and $\lambda=0$ walk one layer alone. The endorsement-seeded PageRank (S-PR) restarts the transfer walk from the allowance walk. Under a quantitative, positivist design, all scores are computed for the same $n=5{,}521$ GMX~V2 trading wallets on Arbitrum One from 1~December~2025 to 31~May~2026 and compared with Kendall's $\tau$ and a paired wallet bootstrap. The main test is a temporal holdout. Scores frozen on 28~February~2026 are set against new owner--spender approvals and new transfer senders counted from March to May~2026, on the $1{,}335$ spenders that held an approval at the freeze, with each wallet's raw degree at the freeze as a baseline. The value of $\lambda$ and a first rule for C-PR were fixed on 14~September~2026, before any coupled score was computed. A second rule, for C-PR against its transfer layer walked alone, was written on 28~September~2026 and registered on 1~October~2026, before the logs of its window were extracted: scores frozen on 31~May~2026 and labels counted from June to August~2026, among them the liquidation-free close rate of the matched traders. The comparisons between EndorseRank and AWP were made after the labels were known.

Out of window each edge type ranks its own construct better. On the spring holdout EndorseRank ranks the spenders that later gain new approvals at $\tau=0.394$ (95\% CI $[0.360,0.431]$) against $0.123$ for AWP, and AWP ranks new transfer senders better ($0.344$ against $0.283$); the June--August window repeats both. Much of both leads is coverage. AWP has no score for spenders without a transfer history, and among the spenders that had received a transfer EndorseRank's lead on new approvals falls to $+0.042$. The walks add nothing over the counts they smooth: the spender's approval count at the freeze ranks new approvals better than EndorseRank ($\tau=0.711$), and the transfer in-degree ranks new senders better than AWP ($0.472$). C-PR at $\lambda=0.5$ fails the first rule, which asked it to match the allowance walk on new approvals ($\Delta\tau=-0.184$, interval $[-0.213,-0.149]$) and to beat the transfer walk on new senders ($+0.007$, interval $[-0.012,0.028]$). Against the transfer walk alone it gained $0.251$ on new approvals with no detectable loss on new senders, in a comparison made after the labels were in, so the second rule tested it on the June--August window. There C-PR again beat the transfer walk on new approvals ($+0.179$, interval $[0.132,0.225]$) and ranked the liquidation-free closes of traders slightly better ($+0.018$, interval $[0.011,0.024]$), but on new senders the lower end of its interval ($-0.025$) fell below the margin of $-0.02$ fixed in advance. The rule needed all three, so the thesis does not claim that C-PR improves on the transfer walk; against AWP, in a registered sensitivity analysis, it lost $0.073$ on new senders. S-PR behaves like the transfer walk, so allowance edges help when the walk moves along them and not when they only set the restart. In the same window, AWP aligns more closely with transfer statistics ($\tau=0.612$ against $0.330$) and EndorseRank with allowance statistics ($0.375$ against $-0.024$), but EndorseRank separates only 131 of the traders from two tied values, so these figures rest on few wallets and on which wallets appear in the allowance graph at all. The allowance graph has about a ninth of the transfer graph's edges, and its PageRank solve is about nine times faster ($0.55$ against $5.03$ seconds).

The thesis contributes an allowance edge for on-chain reputation, an out-of-window comparison with the transfer baseline and its degree counts, and a coupled walk tested under rules fixed in advance, one of them on new data registered before its extraction, with both rules reported as failed. For a protocol that needs to know which spenders owners trust, the approval count is the cheapest useful signal and EndorseRank its propagated form. Both scores come from public logs, so anyone can recompute and audit them. EndorseRank does not rank the traders who grant approvals, and in an exploratory run AWP was ahead of it on counts of their later profitable closes, while neither score ranked the rate. Under uniform teleportation, a farm of ten addresses can lift its target to 7.7 times the highest EndorseRank among the traders.

\emph{Keywords---} DeFi, EndorseRank, Adaptive Weighted PageRank, coupled PageRank, on-chain reputation, ERC-20 allowance, temporal holdout, pre-registration, Arbitrum One, Kendall's tau
