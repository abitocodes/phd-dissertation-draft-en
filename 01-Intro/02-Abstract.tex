\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols use on-chain wallet reputation to screen counterparties, set risk parameters and detect automated or malicious behavior. A published graph method, Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP), ranks wallets on the graph of their token transfers, but a transfer records a payment and not trust. Every ERC-20 token also records a more explicit act, the allowance, by which an owner authorizes a spender to move tokens on the owner's behalf. This thesis asks what allowance edges add to PageRank as a reputation score: what the score measures, whether it anticipates later behavior better than the raw count of the edges it is built from, what it costs to compute, and whether both edge types do better in one walk.

EndorseRank is PageRank on the graph of the latest non-zero allowances, with AWP's edge weights and uniform restarts. It ranks the spenders that receive approvals, such as routers and vaults, and not the owners that grant them. A coupled PageRank (C-PR) mixes the allowance and transfer rows of each wallet with a fixed weight $\lambda$, and a seeded variant (S-PR) restarts the transfer walk from the allowance walk. Under a quantitative design, all scores, AWP and the raw degrees among them, are computed for the same $5{,}521$ GMX~V2 traders on Arbitrum One from December~2025 to May~2026 and compared with Kendall's $\tau$ and paired wallet bootstrap intervals. The main test is a temporal holdout on the $1{,}335$ spenders that held an approval on 28~February~2026: scores frozen on that date are set against new approvals and new transfer senders counted from March to May. One rule for C-PR was fixed before any coupled score was computed, and a second was registered for a June--August window before its logs were extracted.

Out of window no PageRank ranks a label as well as the raw degree of the same edge type. The approval count at the freeze ranks the spenders that later gain approvals at $\tau=0.711$ (95\% CI $[0.665,0.759]$); the walks over allowances come closest, at $0.479$ for the raw-amount allowance layer and $0.394$ for EndorseRank, and the scores built on transfers alone stay at or below $0.123$. The transfer in-degree ranks new transfer senders at $0.472$, against $0.344$ for AWP. The June--August window repeats both gaps. C-PR fails the first rule, which asked it to match the allowance walk on new approvals ($\Delta\tau=-0.184$, $[-0.213,-0.149]$) and to beat the transfer walk on new senders ($+0.007$, $[-0.012,0.028]$). In the registered window it beat the transfer walk on new approvals ($+0.179$, $[0.132,0.225]$) and on the liquidation-free close rate of traders ($+0.018$, $[0.011,0.024]$), but on new senders the lower end of its interval ($-0.025$) fell below the margin of $-0.02$, so the thesis does not claim that coupling helps; against AWP it lost $0.073$ on new senders. In the same window each score agrees with the statistics of its own edges, AWP with transfers ($0.612$) and EndorseRank with allowances ($0.375$), which is partly built in, and EndorseRank separates only 131 traders from two tied values. Its solve is about nine times faster than AWP's ($0.55$ against $5.03$ seconds), in line with its ninefold smaller edge set, while the coupled walks, which also walk the transfers, take longer than AWP.

The thesis contributes an allowance edge for on-chain reputation, an out-of-window evaluation of every score against the degree counts it smooths, and a coupled walk tested under rules fixed in advance and reported as failed. For a protocol that needs to know which spenders owners trust, the approval count is the cheapest useful signal and EndorseRank its propagated form. Both come from public logs, so anyone can recompute and audit them. Neither ranks the traders who grant approvals, and under uniform teleportation a farm of ten addresses can lift its target to 7.7 times the highest EndorseRank in the cohort, so the score should not decide credit or rewards without Sybil controls.

\emph{Keywords---} DeFi, EndorseRank, Adaptive Weighted PageRank, coupled PageRank, on-chain reputation, ERC-20 allowance, temporal holdout, pre-registration, Arbitrum One, Kendall's tau
