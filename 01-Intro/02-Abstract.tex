\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols seek on-chain wallet reputation to screen counterparties and set risk parameters. Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) ranks wallets on token transfers, but a transfer records a payment, not trust. An ERC-20 allowance records an owner authorizing a spender to move its tokens. This thesis asks what a PageRank walk gains when it follows allowance edges together with transfers.

Its method, a coupled PageRank (C-PR), mixes the allowance and transfer rows of every address in one walk with a fixed weight. Its single-layer references are AWP and EndorseRank, PageRank on the latest non-zero allowances with AWP's edge weights. User wallets are used in too many ways to share one scale, so the study scores contracts. It computes all scores, raw degrees included, for $15{,}107$ contracts that at least three owners approved on one of five tokens on Arbitrum One between October~2023 and June~2026, and compares them with Kendall's $\tau$ and paired bootstrap intervals. It tests scores frozen on 31~March~2026 on $17{,}050$ spender contracts and registered a July--September~2026 window before extracting its logs. After the registered results were known, it revalued amounts in US dollars and rebuilt the cohort on these tokens; its main analysis re-applies the rules fixed in advance to the revised scores, with the same verdicts.

Under these rules C-PR beats neither layer walked alone: it is no worse than the allowance layer on later approval pairs ($\Delta\tau=+0.029$ $[0.022,0.036]$) but below the transfer layer on later senders ($-0.034$ $[-0.040,-0.029]$), and in the registered window it gains on approvals ($+0.110$ $[0.098,0.122]$) but falls behind on senders by more than the margin ($-0.028$ $[-0.033,-0.023]$). In contrasts computed afterwards, C-PR ranks both labels above EndorseRank in both windows (approval pairs $+0.021$ $[0.013,0.028]$ and $+0.016$ $[0.010,0.024]$) and later approval pairs above AWP ($+0.056$ $[0.042,0.070]$), but only through the spenders that AWP scores zero, and it ranks later senders below AWP ($-0.085$ $[-0.092,-0.078]$). In these contrasts neither C-PR nor EndorseRank ranks later arrivals above the raw count of the same edge type: the approval count beats EndorseRank in both windows ($-0.019$ $[-0.026,-0.011]$ and $-0.034$ $[-0.038,-0.029]$), whereas AWP beats the transfer in-degree ($+0.043$ $[0.038,0.048]$ and $+0.022$ $[0.017,0.027]$). EndorseRank with AWP's activity restarts does beat the approval count ($+0.059$ $[0.050,0.067]$ and $+0.027$ $[0.021,0.034]$), so the restart rule, not the edge type, appears to decide. On later liquidation avoidance of GMX~V2 contract traders, built from neither edge type, the approval count led the transfer count only in the earlier window ($\Delta\tau=+0.182$, 97.5\% interval $[0.032,0.338]$; registered window $+0.084$ $[-0.026,0.188]$, $70$ traders). C-PR takes $1.62$ times AWP's solve time; EndorseRank, on a graph with $2.23$ times fewer edges, takes $2.17$ times less.

The thesis contributes a walk that couples authorization and payment, its failed test under rules fixed in advance, the allowance as a reputation edge, a same-cohort and out-of-window evaluation of every score against the counts it smooths, and a closed form of such walks at depth one that reduces EndorseRank to an owner-normalized in-strength. About 22 colluding addresses can lift a contract past the cohort's 90th percentile on the allowance graph, and the same bound covers C-PR, so no score should decide rewards without Sybil controls; none measures creditworthiness or the safety of a contract's code.

\emph{Key terms:} coupled PageRank; decentralized finance; on-chain reputation; ERC-20 allowance; EndorseRank; Adaptive Weighted PageRank; smart contract; temporal holdout; pre-registration; Arbitrum One; Kendall's tau
