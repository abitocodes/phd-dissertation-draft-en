\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

A rollup holds many smart contracts, and wallet applications, explorers and auditors must choose which to examine first. PageRank methods for ledgers walk over transfers among all addresses, user accounts included, and do not read the ERC-20 allowance by which one contract lets another spend its tokens. This thesis asks whether one PageRank walk over allowance and transfer edges between contracts on Arbitrum One finds the contracts that users rely on sooner, and predicts their later use better, than single-layer walks and raw counts.

Its method, a coupled PageRank (C-PR), mixes the allowance and transfer rows of each contract in one walk; its references are Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) on transfers and EndorseRank, the same weights on the latest allowances. After the registered results were known, the study defined this analysis: graphs of contracts, with no user account in any; each allowance counted only up to its owner's balance; amounts in US dollars on five tokens; scores from the twelve months before each freeze (31~March and 30~June~2026); and a discovery-order test, which crawls the contract graph in the order a score gives on the contracts read so far and measures how soon it reaches those that user accounts pay and authorize most. Out of window, it tests $7{,}864$ and $7{,}915$ spender contracts against the approval pairs and senders of the next three months, under the rules of its analysis plan and registered second window.

The discovery-order test answers no. In its deciding run the crawl ordered by C-PR reached the contracts with the most user-account inflow and user-authorized value sooner than a breadth-first crawl (difference in area under the discovery curve $+0.0520$ $[+0.0485,+0.0558]$ and $+0.0061$ $[+0.0014,+0.0111]$) but later than a crawl ordered by contract backlinks ($-0.0051$ $[-0.0087,-0.0010]$ and $-0.0571$ $[-0.0621,-0.0519]$). Rule A is not met: C-PR ranks approval pairs below its allowance layer ($\Delta\tau=-0.025$ $[-0.045,-0.004]$) and senders below its transfer layer ($-0.014$ $[-0.019,-0.008]$). Rule B is not met: the thesis applies two of its registered criteria, F1 and F2, and under the registration the rule holds only if each of its criteria holds; C-PR gains on approval pairs over its transfer layer ($+0.074$ $[0.060,0.087]$) but loses more than the margin on senders ($-0.016$ $[-0.022,-0.012]$). EndorseRank ranks approval pairs below the in-approve degree it smooths in both windows ($-0.036$ $[-0.046,-0.027]$, $-0.034$ $[-0.043,-0.025]$); AWP ranks senders slightly above the transfer in-degree ($+0.012$ $[0.003,0.023]$, $+0.011$ $[0.001,0.022]$) but below it among first-window spenders a contract had already paid ($-0.035$ $[-0.049,-0.022]$); and C-PR lies above the count of both edge types on approval pairs ($+0.016$ $[0.007,0.024]$, $+0.011$ $[0.003,0.019]$) and below it on senders ($-0.053$ $[-0.061,-0.043]$, $-0.056$ $[-0.065,-0.047]$). C-PR takes $1.09$ times AWP's solve time, and EndorseRank, with $22.4$ times fewer edges, $27.9$ times less.

The thesis contributes the coupled walk, the balance-capped allowance between contracts as a ranking edge, a discovery-order test for ledger rankings, the walk's negative answers under that test and the rules of the plan and registration, and an evaluation of every walk against its count. The allowance layer between contracts is thin, and the labels count mostly user accounts. Every Sybil address must be a deployed, funded contract, yet a closed farm collects $6.16$ times its population share of EndorseRank and about $374$ farm contracts reach the cohort's top score, so no score should decide rewards without Sybil controls; none reads a contract's code or was tested for credit decisions about contracts.

\emph{Key terms:} coupled PageRank; smart contract; crawl ordering; on-chain reputation; ERC-20 allowance; EndorseRank; Adaptive Weighted PageRank; decentralized finance; temporal holdout; pre-registration; Arbitrum One; Kendall's tau
