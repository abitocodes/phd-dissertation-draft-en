\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols use on-chain wallet reputation to filter integration candidates, determine risk parameters, and detect automated and malicious behavior. Previous on-chain reputation approaches, such as the Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) on the transfer graph by Do, Do and Nguyen\cend{3}, utilize historical token transfers. It is computationally expensive to update on a rollup that publishes millions of token transfers every month, and token transfers quantify economic activity but not an explicit action of endorsement. This thesis examines whether an alternative endorsement mechanism that is already available on every EVM-compatible chain, the ERC-20 allowance an owner allows a spender to spend on their behalf, can be used to construct a wallet reputation score that is interpretable by users, computationally cheap, and verifiable on public blockchain records.

This thesis presents EndorseRank, a PageRank score on the directed endorsement graph where edges correspond to the most recent non-zero ERC-20 allowances between wallets, and examines whether combining the allowance and transfer layers provides any benefit. The combination is the coupled PageRank (C-PR), where the transition row for each wallet node is a convex combination of the two layers with weight $\lambda$, such that $\lambda=1$ yields EndorseRank and $\lambda=0$ yields AWP on the same set of nodes; the endorsement-seeded PageRank (S-PR), a transfer graph walk initialized from the EndorseRank distribution, serves as the control. Using a quantitative, positivist research design, all methods are evaluated against a faithful implementation of AWP on the same subset of $n=5{,}521$ Arbitrum One wallets for the period 1~December~2025 through 31~May~2026. We evaluate alignment with corresponding transfer, allowance and Sybil stability measures using Kendall's $\tau$, and we calculate uncertainty via a paired wallet bootstrap (400 samples) for each parameter and for each pre-specified difference between parameters. A temporal holdout evaluates wallet scores using transactions occurring up to 28~February~2026 and tests them against two outcome variables that occur after the last transaction included in each score: owner--spender approvals occurring first in March--May~2026, and transfer senders occurring first in March--May~2026, with in-degree at the snapshot date as a baseline measure for each. The value of $\lambda$ and criteria for what constitutes an improvement were specified in a versioned configuration file prior to computing the coupled scores. We also measure execution time using one timing setup (one warm-up, five timed runs) and examine sensitivity to node count on a larger pool of $N=99{,}080$ addresses.

We present four findings. First, EndorseRank solves PageRank in 0.40 seconds compared to 4.11 seconds for AWP on the same subset (almost 10 times faster) due to its endorsement graph having 14,727 edges versus 137,087 for the transfer graph; this ratio remains between 8.1 and 12.2 at different node counts. The coupled operator is just as expensive as AWP because it walks over both edge sets. Second, the methods measure different things: AWP is more correlated with the transfer proxies ($\tau=0.534$ versus 0.333 for EndorseRank), whereas EndorseRank is more correlated with the allowance proxies ($\tau=0.355$, 95\% CI $[0.322,0.384]$) where AWP is slightly negative ($-0.030$); their cross-correlation is $\tau=0.316$. Since EndorseRank's correlation with the allowance proxies is not statistically higher than with its own transfer proxies, the latter finding is treated as a check on intended construct rather than an external validation. Third, on the holdout spenders ($n=1{,}335$), EndorseRank ranks wallets that subsequently receive new approvals at $\tau=0.479$ (95\% CI $[0.426,0.529]$), whereas AWP is near zero ($0.044$); the in-approve degree at the freeze date is $\tau=0.711$, and the difference in favor of EndorseRank is negative. AWP is also lower than the transfer in-degree on new senders. Neither PageRank adds information outside the window to its own count on this cohort. Fourth, C-PR at $\lambda=0.5$ violates the pre-registered rule on both counts: it is worse than EndorseRank on new approvals ($\Delta\tau=-0.184$, interval $[-0.213,-0.149]$) and no better than AWP on new senders ($\Delta\tau=+0.007$, interval $[-0.012,0.028]$); S-PR mirrors AWP. The damping, top-20-token and sample-definition checks do not change this pattern.

This comparison is the first same-cohort evaluation of an allowance-based and a transfer-based PageRank with confidence intervals, difference tests, a two-label temporal holdout with degree baselines, a pre-registered test of a coupled operator and a versioned pipeline with committed summaries. A reputation score can be updated at around one tenth the cost of the transfer-graph baseline and represents a trust action that the baseline does not, but its ability to predict future endorsement is contained within the spender's approval count, and walking over both edge types in a single chain interpolates between the single-layer scores rather than surpassing them. For practitioners, the takeaway is that the allowance count is the inexpensive proxy for approval-related filtering, EndorseRank is the propagated version of that count, and the transfer graph should be consulted separately when payment flows are relevant. EndorseRank does not beat AWP on transfer hub identification. Its Sybil resistance depends on the economic cost of generating approvals, which is modelled but not quantified here.
