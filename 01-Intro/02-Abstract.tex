\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols seek on-chain wallet reputation to screen counterparties and set risk parameters. Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) ranks wallets on token transfers, but a transfer records a payment, not trust. An ERC-20 allowance records an owner authorizing a spender to move its tokens. This thesis asks what allowance edges add to PageRank as a reputation score.

EndorseRank is PageRank on the latest non-zero allowances, with AWP's edge weights and uniform restarts; it ranks spenders, not the owners approving them. A coupled PageRank (C-PR) walks both edge types. User wallets are used in too many ways to share one scale, so the study scores contracts. It computes all scores, raw degrees included, for $15{,}107$ contracts that at least three owners approved on one of five tokens on Arbitrum One between October~2023 and June~2026, and compares them with Kendall's $\tau$ and paired bootstrap intervals. It tests scores frozen on 31~March~2026 on $17{,}050$ spender contracts and registered a July--September~2026 window before extracting its logs. After the registered results were known, it revalued amounts in US dollars and rebuilt the cohort on these tokens; its main analysis re-applies the rules fixed in advance to the revised scores, with the same verdicts.

Out of window, in contrasts computed after the labels were known, EndorseRank ranks later approval pairs below the approval count in both windows ($\Delta\tau=-0.019$ $[-0.026,-0.011]$ and $-0.034$ $[-0.038,-0.029]$), whereas AWP ranks new senders above the transfer in-degree ($+0.043$ $[0.038,0.048]$ and $+0.022$ $[0.017,0.027]$). Also post hoc, EndorseRank with AWP's activity restarts beats its count ($+0.059$ $[0.050,0.067]$ and $+0.027$ $[0.021,0.034]$), so the restart rule, not the edge type, appears to decide. C-PR meets neither its first nor its registered rule: in the registered window it ranks later approval pairs above its transfer layer and AWP ($+0.110$ $[0.098,0.122]$ and $+0.081$ $[0.069,0.095]$, the latter traced post hoc to the sixth of spenders AWP cannot order) and new senders below both ($-0.028$ $[-0.033,-0.023]$ and $-0.067$ $[-0.072,-0.061]$). Coupled post hoc with the edges and activity restarts of EndorseRank, AWP itself ranks later approval pairs higher in both windows, also among the spenders it orders ($+0.118$ $[0.106,0.132]$ and $+0.127$ $[0.114,0.139]$), at a small loss on new senders ($-0.015$ $[-0.019,-0.011]$ and $-0.018$ $[-0.021,-0.015]$), a hypothesis for a registered window. On later liquidation avoidance of GMX~V2 contract traders, an outcome built from neither edge type, the approval count led the transfer count in the earlier window ($\Delta\tau=+0.182$, 97.5\% interval $[0.032,0.338]$) but not resolvably in the registered one ($+0.084$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.026,0.188]$, $70$ traders), where it ranked lower on the profitable share of closes; the PageRank versions are unresolved. EndorseRank solves $2.17$ times faster than AWP, on a graph with $2.23$ times fewer edges, and C-PR slower than AWP.

The thesis contributes the allowance as a reputation edge, a same-cohort and out-of-window evaluation of every score against the counts it smooths, a closed form that reduces EndorseRank at depth one to an owner-normalized in-strength, and a coupled walk whose rules, fixed in advance, failed. About 22 colluding addresses can lift a contract past the cohort's 90th percentile of EndorseRank, although reaching the top would take about $135{,}895$, so no score should decide rewards without Sybil controls, and none measures creditworthiness or the safety of a contract's code.

\emph{Key terms:} decentralized finance; on-chain reputation; ERC-20 allowance; EndorseRank; Adaptive Weighted PageRank; coupled PageRank; smart contract; temporal holdout; pre-registration; Arbitrum One; Kendall's tau
