\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols use on-chain wallet reputation to screen counterparties and set risk parameters. Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) ranks wallets on token transfers, but a transfer records a payment, not trust. An ERC-20 allowance records an owner authorizing a spender to move its tokens. This thesis asks what allowance edges add to PageRank as a reputation score.

EndorseRank is PageRank on the latest non-zero allowances, with AWP's edge weights and uniform restarts; it ranks spenders, not the owners approving them. A coupled PageRank (C-PR) walks both edge types. User wallets are used in too many different ways to be ranked on one scale, so the scored wallets are contract accounts. All scores, raw degrees included, are computed for $15{,}107$ contracts that at least three owners approved on Arbitrum One between October~2023 and June~2026, and compared with Kendall's $\tau$ and paired bootstrap intervals. Scores frozen on 31~March~2026 are tested on $17{,}050$ spender contracts, and a July--September~2026 window was registered before extraction. After the registered results were known, amounts were revalued in US dollars on five widely traded tokens; the main analysis reports these revised scores, on which the rules fixed in advance reach the same verdicts.

Out of window the approval count ranks later approval pairs above EndorseRank in both windows ($\Delta\tau=-0.019$ $[-0.026,-0.011]$ and $-0.034$ $[-0.038,-0.029]$), whereas AWP ranks later new senders above the transfer in-degree ($+0.043$ $[0.038,0.048]$ and $+0.022$ $[0.017,0.027]$). A closed form shows where the allowance gap comes from: for $12{,}039$ of $14{,}406$ cohort spenders EndorseRank reduces to an owner-normalized in-strength, and the multi-hop propagation of the others did not lift it above the count. C-PR meets neither its first nor its registered rule. On later liquidation avoidance of GMX~V2 contract traders, an outcome built from neither edge type, the approval count led the transfer count in the earlier window ($\Delta\tau=+0.182$, 97.5\% interval $[0.032,0.338]$) but not resolvably in the registered one ($+0.084$ $[-0.026,0.188]$, $70$ traders); the PageRank versions are not resolved, and the approval count is lower on the profitable and non-losing shares. EndorseRank solves $2.17$ times faster than AWP, on a graph with $2.23$ times fewer edges, while C-PR, which also walks the transfers, takes longer than AWP.

The thesis contributes the allowance as a reputation edge, an out-of-window evaluation of every score against the counts it smooths, a closed form of EndorseRank at depth one, and a coupled walk whose rules, fixed in advance, failed. Twenty-two colluding addresses can lift a contract past the cohort's 90th percentile of EndorseRank, although reaching the top would take about $135{,}895$, so no score should decide rewards without Sybil controls, and none measures creditworthiness or the safety of a contract's code.

\emph{Key terms:} decentralized finance; on-chain reputation; ERC-20 allowance; EndorseRank; Adaptive Weighted PageRank; coupled PageRank; smart contract; temporal holdout; pre-registration; Arbitrum One; Kendall's tau
