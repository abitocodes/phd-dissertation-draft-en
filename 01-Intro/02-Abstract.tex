\chapter*{Abstract}

\label{ch:abstract}

Decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols use on-chain wallet reputation to screen integration candidates, set risk parameters, and detect automated and malicious behavior. Earlier approaches to on-chain reputation, such as the Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) of Do, Do and Nguyen\cend{3} on the transfer graph, rely on historical token transfers. Such scores are computationally expensive to update on a rollup that publishes millions of token transfers each month. Token transfers also quantify economic activity without capturing an explicit act of endorsement. Every EVM-compatible chain already supports an alternative endorsement mechanism, the ERC-20 allowance, through which an owner authorizes a spender to move tokens on the owner's behalf. This thesis asks whether bringing allowance edges into PageRank improves wallet reputation over the transfer-only baseline: in what the score measures, in what it costs to compute, in how well it anticipates later behavior, and when both edge types share a single walk.

We introduce EndorseRank, a PageRank score on the directed endorsement graph whose edges are the most recent non-zero ERC-20 allowances between wallets, and we ask whether combining the allowance and transfer layers improves on the transfer walk. The joint operator is the coupled PageRank (C-PR), in which each wallet node's transition row is a convex combination of the two layers with weight $\lambda$; on the same set of nodes, $\lambda=1$ yields EndorseRank and $\lambda=0$ yields AWP. The control is the endorsement-seeded PageRank (S-PR), a walk on the transfer graph that is restarted from the EndorseRank distribution, used as its teleport vector. Under a quantitative, positivist research design, all methods are evaluated against a faithful implementation of AWP on the same subset of $n=5{,}521$ Arbitrum One wallets, over the period 1~December~2025 to 31~May~2026. Alignment with the corresponding transfer, allowance and Sybil stability measures is assessed with Kendall's $\tau$, and a paired wallet bootstrap (400 samples) quantifies the uncertainty of each parameter and of each pre-specified difference between parameters. In the temporal holdout, wallet scores are computed from transactions up to 28~February~2026 and tested against two outcomes that occur after the last transaction included in each score: owner--spender approvals first occurring in March--May~2026, and transfer senders first occurring in March--May~2026. For each outcome, the baseline is in-degree at the snapshot date. The value of $\lambda$ and a first rule for the coupled operator were fixed in a versioned configuration file before any coupled score was computed. A second rule, for the comparison with AWP alone, was registered on 28~September~2026 together with a new window: scores frozen on 31~May~2026 and labels counted from June to August~2026, among them the liquidation-free close rate of the matched traders. We also measure runtime under a single timing setup (one warm-up, five timed runs) and examine sensitivity to node count on a larger pool of $N=99{,}080$ addresses.

We report four findings. First, EndorseRank solves PageRank about 10 times faster than AWP on the same subset (0.40 seconds against 4.11 seconds), because its endorsement graph has 14,727 edges and the transfer graph 137,087; as the node count varies, the ratio remains between 8.1 and 12.2. The coupled operator walks both edge sets and is therefore as expensive as AWP. Second, the two scores measure different things. AWP correlates more strongly with the transfer proxies ($\tau=0.534$ versus 0.333 for EndorseRank), and EndorseRank more strongly with the allowance proxies ($\tau=0.355$, 95\% CI $[0.322,0.384]$), on which AWP is slightly negative ($-0.030$). Each proxy family is built from the events behind the matching graph, so this is a construct check and not external validation. Third, out of window the allowance edge carries information that the transfer baseline lacks. On the holdout spenders ($n=1{,}335$), EndorseRank ranks the wallets that later receive new approvals at $\tau=0.479$ (95\% CI $[0.427,0.529]$) against 0.044 for AWP, and it also ranks later new transfer senders better (0.372 against 0.270); both contrasts were fixed before the run. The spender's approval count at the freeze date does better still ($\tau=0.711$), so the gain sits in the edge type and not in propagation. Fourth, in an exploratory comparison on the spring holdout, the coupled walk improved on AWP without beating EndorseRank. At $\lambda=0.5$, C-PR raises the coefficient on new approvals from AWP's 0.044 to 0.295 (paired difference $+0.251$, interval $[0.203,0.306]$) and is level with AWP on new senders ($+0.007$, interval $[-0.012,0.028]$). It fails both parts of the first rule, which asked it to match EndorseRank on new approvals ($\Delta\tau=-0.184$, interval $[-0.213,-0.149]$) and to beat AWP on new senders. The gain over AWP was noticed only after the labels were in, so the second rule tests it again on the June--August window: \freshpending{registered result}. S-PR mirrors AWP, and none of the damping, top-20-token and sample-definition checks changes the same-window pattern.

To our knowledge, this is the first same-cohort evaluation of an allowance-based and a transfer-based PageRank that reports confidence intervals, paired contrasts and degree baselines, tests a coupled operator against a rule fixed in advance and registers a second rule for new data, and runs from a versioned pipeline with committed summaries. Bringing ERC-20 allowance edges into PageRank improves wallet reputation in one respect. The score sees spending authorization, which the transfer baseline cannot see, and it is about a tenth as expensive to solve. The gain comes from the edge rather than the walk: the approval count anticipates new approvals better than any PageRank tested, which makes it the inexpensive first choice for approval-related filtering. EndorseRank scores the spender side, and it separates only 122 of the 5,521 matched traders from its common floor value. In an exploratory holdout on those traders, AWP was ahead of EndorseRank on later trading success, on counts and sums of profitable closes rather than on their rate. Under uniform teleportation, ten farm addresses can lift their target to 7.7 times the highest score in the cohort.
