\section{Data Description}
\label{ch:appendix-data}

This appendix describes the extracted data behind Chapter~\ref{ch:results}: the volume of the ego extraction, the rows that the revised analysis keeps and the size of its two graphs, the five tokens and their prices, the US-dollar amounts of transfers and allowances and how often owners grant unlimited allowances, and the activity of GMX~V2 contract accounts by quarter. The account types of the cohorts are in Table~\ref{tab:account-types}, the extraction itself is described in Appendix~\ref{sec:bq-extraction}, and the revision of the amounts in Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:data-events]{Extracted events and graphs}

The ego extraction of the observation window was made for the plan's cohort of $27{,}844$ contracts. It holds $389{,}866{,}882$ approval logs, zero-value ones included, and $1{,}538{,}404{,}067$ transfer logs of non-zero value of every ERC-20 token, each with a contract of that cohort at one end. The revised analysis keeps the $224{,}954{,}202$ approvals and $1{,}149{,}168{,}538$ transfers that are of the five tokens and have a contract of the revised cohort at one end, $57.7\%$ and $74.7\%$ of the extracted logs; of the July--September logs of W1 it keeps $12{,}209{,}894$ approvals and $60{,}182{,}192$ transfers. Table~\ref{tab:usd-tokens} gives the rows of each token and those left out. From the kept events the allowance graph at $T_{\text{obs}}$ has $6{,}221{,}665$ nodes and $11{,}210{,}114$ edges, and the transfer graph $7{,}884{,}602$ nodes and $25{,}001{,}216$ edges. These edge counts are those of the pairs with a positive EndorseRank or AWP weight: the $9{,}002$ allowance pairs and $17{,}676$ transfer pairs whose amounts give a value weight of zero in double precision are not counted, while the node counts include the $2{,}602$ and $2{,}246$ addresses that only such pairs touch. At the freeze of W0 the pair tables of the two graphs hold $10{,}650{,}258$ and $23{,}680{,}692$ pairs, counted without that filter. The allowance graph has fewer than half as many edges as the transfer graph. It keeps one latest allowance per token, owner and spender and drops the revoked ones, whereas the transfer graph is built from every sender--recipient pair that exchanged a transfer of the five tokens in the window.

Membership in the cohort does not depend on either graph at $T_{\text{obs}}$. Every cohort contract received non-zero approvals of the five tokens from at least three owners in the window, but $523$ of the $15{,}107$ neither hold nor grant a positive latest allowance at $T_{\text{obs}}$, so they lie outside the allowance graph; $2{,}670$ lie outside the transfer graph, and $80$ outside both. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} scores such a contract zero under the method whose graph it misses, and Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} reports the rule that keeps it as an isolated node instead.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:data-tokens]{Tokens and allowance amounts}

The revised analysis keeps the approvals and transfers of five tokens (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}). A token qualified when Chainlink's directory of data feeds on Arbitrum One has a market-price feed rated low or medium market risk that prices the token itself, the asset it wraps one-to-one, or the Ethereum token of which it is the canonical-bridge copy \parencite{chainlinknddatafeeds}, and the token occurs in the observation-window data. Of the $133$ Arbitrum One tokens that the directory maps, $64$ qualified and occur in the data of the observation window, and $46$ of these have a daily price from DefiLlama \parencite{defillamanddocs}. Among the $46$ the analysis keeps the five with the most rows, counting the transfers up to $T_{\text{obs}}$ and the positive latest allowances at $T_{\text{obs}}$ in the ego tables of the plan's cohort: WETH, the native USDC, USDT0, ARB and WBTC. Together they hold $75.75\%$ of the rows of all tokens, and all five are priced by feeds rated low risk. Table~\ref{tab:usd-tokens} gives for each of them the address, the decimals and the feed, the approval and transfer logs, counted together, that the ego tables of the plan's cohort hold and those that the revised analysis keeps, the US-dollar volume of the kept transfers and the data behind their daily prices.

The price of a token on a UTC day is DefiLlama's daily close (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}). DefiLlama's series under the Arbitrum One address begins in August~2024 for WETH and WBTC and in January~2025 for USDT0, so $319$, $308$ and $481$ of their $1{,}096$ days take the price of the same asset under its CoinGecko identifier, the second key, which differs from the Arbitrum series by less than $0.1\%$ in median on the days both cover. USDC and ARB need no second key. None of the five tokens has a day filled from the previous day or a point replaced as a data error, and no kept transfer precedes its token's first price. Against the Chainlink feeds read on the chain on $38$ dates per token, the DefiLlama prices differ by a median of $0.01\%$ to $0.08\%$ per token and by at most $0.72\%$, for WETH on 15~January~2024.

Table~\ref{tab:top-tokens} lists $27$ tokens: the twenty with the most rows and seven more from the list by amount of the registered token check. The revised analysis keeps the first five; the sixth, the bridged USDC, qualifies and has a price but is left out. Seven of the twenty, among them NPC, UXUY and AIUS, do not qualify under the feed rule. The last two columns mark the two lists of the token check of the registered analysis, which rebuilt both graphs from twenty tokens chosen once by the number of rows and once by the summed raw amount (\texttt{sql/11\_top\_tokens.sql}) and which the revision replaced by the value-slope check (Section~\ref{sub:top-tokens}); the two lists share $13$ tokens. In that analysis raw amounts were summed without regard to decimals, and an unlimited allowance counted as $2^{256}-1$ base units, so the list by amount took in seven tokens with comparatively few rows, among them aArbWETH, the Aave deposit receipt for WETH, and two Fiat24 tokens with two decimals.

In US dollars the kept transfers of the observation window have a median of $193.57$ dollars. A tenth of them are worth less than $0.14$ dollars and a quarter less than $12.19$, while three quarters are worth less than $891.94$ dollars and the 90th and 99th percentiles are $2{,}967.6$ and $19{,}999.9$ dollars. The finite positive latest allowances at $T_{\text{obs}}$, valued at the price of 30~June~2026, have a median of $60.58$ dollars, with a tenth below $0.33$, a quarter below $5.44$ and three quarters below $1{,}198.4$ dollars; their 90th percentile is about $2.1\times10^{9}$ dollars and their 99th about $5.9\times10^{45}$, because many allowances below $2^{256}-1$ base units, such as $2^{255}$ or $2^{128}$, are unlimited in effect. The constants of Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision} come from the same quantities up to $t_1$: medians of $203.71$ dollars for transfers and $61.51$ dollars for finite allowances give $m_T=200$ and $m_A=62$ dollars, and the 99th percentile of single transfers, $20{,}266.4$ dollars, gives $U=20{,}000$ dollars. At $T_{\text{obs}}$ both medians lie just below $m_T$ and $m_A$, so more than half of the transfers and of the finite allowances enter AWP and EndorseRank with a value weight below one half, and between the lower and the upper quartile of either amount the weight rises from near zero to near one.

Unlimited allowances are common. Of the $12{,}590{,}613$ positive latest allowances at $T_{\text{obs}}$, $5{,}763{,}757$ ($45.8\%$) are of $1.15\times10^{77}$ base units or more, close to the largest \texttt{uint256} value $2^{256}-1\approx1.16\times10^{77}$, and $6{,}776{,}289$ ($53.8\%$), the unlimited ones included, are worth at least the cap $U=20{,}000$ dollars. The $12{,}590{,}613$ allowances belong to $6{,}208{,}769$ owners, of whom $3{,}294{,}370$ hold at least one unlimited allowance and $797{,}852$ hold both an unlimited and a finite one (\texttt{sql/18\_usd\_describe.sql}). EndorseRank gives an allowance of $m_A=62$ dollars the value weight one half, one of $124$ dollars $0.8$, and an unlimited allowance one, the most an approval can weigh. In the allowance layer of C-PR every allowance at or above $U$ counts $U$, so in the row of an owner with both kinds an unlimited allowance weighs as much as a finite allowance of $20{,}000$ dollars, and the smaller allowances keep a share of the row in proportion to their amounts (Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}). In the registered analysis, which kept every token in raw base units with $b=1$, $99.52\%$ of the ego transfers and $99.78\%$ of the $28{,}069{,}418$ latest allowances at $T_{\text{obs}}$ were of ten base units or more, where $V$ is within $10^{-4}$ of one, so the amount played no part in EndorseRank or AWP. In that analysis $1{,}532{,}301$ of the $13{,}029{,}306$ owners with a positive latest allowance held both an unlimited and a smaller allowance, and the raw allowance layer of C-PR let the unlimited ones take nearly all of such an owner's row (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}).

Contract accounts closed the most positions in the second half of 2025, $28{,}753$ and $40{,}240$ in the third and fourth quarters, when $1{,}567$ and $1{,}744$ of them closed at least three. In the first half of 2026 their closes fell to $6{,}026$ and $3{,}783$, and the accounts with at least three closes to $171$ and $127$. A trader cohort further requires the account to be a node of the graph at the freeze: of the $127$ contract accounts with at least three closes in April--June~2026, $88$ form the trader cohort of W0, and of the $131$ in July--September, $70$ form that of W1. These small cohorts are why every trader result of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} has wide intervals.
