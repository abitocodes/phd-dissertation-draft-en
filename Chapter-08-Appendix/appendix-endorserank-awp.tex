\section{EndorseRank and AWP Side by Side}
\label{ch:appendix-er-awp}

Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports every score of the thesis together and reads each PageRank against the raw degree it smooths. This appendix collects the direct comparisons between the two single-layer scores, EndorseRank and AWP: their family means and paired differences in the same window, their per-proxy coefficients, and their paired differences out of window. Apart from the trader grid at its end, none of these comparisons was fixed in advance, and none carries a claim of the thesis. The two scores walk on different edges and are built for different constructs, so on each check the score ahead is, almost every time, the one whose edges the check counts. Outcomes built from neither edge type are the exception, since neither score was built for them, and are reported in Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}. Their coverage differs as well: no allowance edge touches $523$ of the matched contracts, and no transfer edge touches $2{,}670$. Out of window AWP ranks later new senders above the transfer in-degree, while EndorseRank ranks later new approval pairs below the in-approve degree (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}), so a lead of EndorseRank over AWP does not show that propagation over approvals adds anything to the count of approving owners.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:er-awp-same-window]{Same window}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} gives the family means of the two scores with their intervals, and the last row of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} their agreement. The two patterns cross: AWP tracks the transfer family most closely and the allowance family least, while EndorseRank tracks the allowance family most closely. Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff} tests five paired differences on the same contract resamples (Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests}), numbered i to v in the order of its rows.

\input{results/tables/tau-diff.tex}

All five intervals lie above zero. AWP's transfer alignment is higher than EndorseRank's (contrast~ii, $+0.414$, $[0.401,0.427]$), EndorseRank's allowance alignment is higher than AWP's (contrast~iii, $+0.392$, $[0.380,0.405]$), and AWP leads EndorseRank on the Sybil-stability family (contrast~iv, $+0.388$, $[0.376,0.401]$). The remaining two compare EndorseRank with itself: its allowance alignment is higher than its transfer alignment (contrast~i, $+0.427$, $[0.416,0.440]$), and its in-approve-degree alignment is higher than its in-degree alignment (contrast~v, $+0.473$, $[0.461,0.486]$). EndorseRank ties only $0.17\%$ of the contract pairs, so its own ties cap none of these coefficients, and the ties of the proxies do not explain gaps of this size (Appendix~\ref{sec:kendall-properties}). With the contracts outside the allowance graph kept as isolated nodes instead of being scored zero, none of the family means changes (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).

The opposite directions of contrasts~ii and~iii follow from the constructs. Each score is ahead on the family built from its own edges, and the main proxies of each family include the in-degree that the corresponding PageRank smooths, so the agreement is partly built in and checks the intended construct of each score. Neither difference says which score is the better measure of reputation. On every scaling stage of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size} the order of the two scores within each family is the same as on the full cohort.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:proxy-family-detail]{Proxy detail}

Each family mean condenses several per-proxy coefficients. Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} to~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability} report them one proxy at a time for EndorseRank and AWP, as Spearman $\rho$, Kendall $\tau$ and the 95\% interval of $\tau$; the transfer proxies are the ones used in the evaluation of AWP by its authors \parencite{do2023awp}.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:transfer-family]{Transfer family}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} gives the coefficients of the two scores with the in-degree and the in-value.

\input{results/tables/alignment-transfer.tex}

AWP agrees with the in-value ($0.566$, $[0.558,0.575]$) almost as closely as with the in-degree ($0.578$, $[0.569,0.587]$), for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:same-window-alignment}. EndorseRank's transfer coefficients have intervals that overlap neither zero nor AWP's intervals. They mainly reflect that contracts that many owners approve receive transfers from more senders than the rest of the cohort.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:allowance-family]{Allowance family}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-allowance} gives the coefficients of the two scores with the in-approve degree and the in-approve value.

\input{results/tables/alignment-allowance.tex}

EndorseRank follows the in-approve degree more closely than the in-approve value, for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:same-window-alignment}. AWP's coefficients lie above zero: among the cohort's contracts, those that many owners approve also tend to be central in the transfer graph. The comparison is partly built in, since EndorseRank is computed from the same approvals.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:sybil-family]{Sybil-adjusted stability family}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability} gives the coefficients of the two scores with the inbound counterparty ratio, the transfer tenure and the active months.

\input{results/tables/alignment-sybil-stability.tex}

AWP is ahead of EndorseRank on tenure and active months, as one would expect from a transfer score that rewards activity spread over the window; the paired difference on the family mean excludes zero (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}, contrast~iv). On the inbound counterparty ratio the two coefficients have opposite signs, negative for EndorseRank and positive for AWP. The ratio falls when the same senders return, so contracts that many owners approve are, on this measure, contracts whose senders return. AWP's positive coefficient comes from coverage: the $2{,}670$ contracts outside the transfer graph have ratio zero and AWP score zero, so they fill the bottom of both orderings. The family describes how much longitudinal transfer information each score carries.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:er-awp-holdout]{Out of window}

Table~\ref{tab:endorserank-awp-holdout} collects the paired differences between the two scores in both holdout windows. In every row $a$ is EndorseRank and $b$ AWP.

\input{results/tables/endorserank-awp-holdout.tex}

On the spenders of W0, EndorseRank ranks new approval pairs above AWP, a paired difference of $+0.035$ (interval $[0.019,0.051]$). On new transfer senders the order reverses, with a difference of $-0.146$ ($[-0.156,-0.136]$). Every label falls after all scored events, so on this cohort each edge type carries information about its own construct that the other lacks. Coverage accounts for part of the first gap. AWP scores zero for the $2{,}891$ spenders without a transfer edge before the freeze, and these gained new approval pairs more often than the rest. On the $14{,}094$ spenders that had received a transfer from another address, AWP is ahead on both labels (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}), without a paired test.

In July--September, with the contrasts computed from the W1 scores after all labels were known, the pattern repeats: EndorseRank leads on new approval pairs ($+0.065$, $[0.051,0.081]$) and AWP on new senders ($-0.136$, $[-0.146,-0.127]$).

On the W0 traders (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-traders}), only AWP's intervals lie above zero on the number of profitable closes and on the realized gain, two labels that grow with the number of closes. On the two shares, which do not, both scores are negative, with intervals below zero on the non-loss share only. Neither score ranks these contract traders by the share of successful closes.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:er-awp-neutral-grid]{All trader labels in both windows}

Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-grid} sets the allowance side against the transfer side on all six trader labels in both windows. Its pairs were registered with W1 before the study extracted the logs of July--September (Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-protocol}); here they are computed on the revised scores, after the registered results were known, so the table is a re-analysis (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}). P and Q on the liquidation-free close rate enter rules R1 and R2, and P on the two shares enters rule R3 (Claim~C6, Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}). In W1 no pair separates the two sides on either liquidation label. In W0 the counts (P) favor the allowance side on both liquidation labels, while the three PageRank pairs lie below zero with intervals that include zero. On the four profit labels of W1 every pair favors the transfer side: the intervals of Q and of the EndorseRank pair lie below zero on all four, those of P on the two shares, and those of the C-PR layers on realized gain and on both shares. In W0 every interval on the profit labels includes zero except that of the C-PR layers on realized gain.
