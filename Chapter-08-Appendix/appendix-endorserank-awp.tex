\section{EndorseRank and AWP Side by Side}
\label{ch:appendix-er-awp}

Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports every score of the thesis together and reads each PageRank against the raw degree it smooths. This appendix collects the direct comparisons between the two single-layer scores, EndorseRank and AWP: their family means and paired differences in the same window, their per-proxy coefficients, and their paired differences out of window. None of these comparisons belongs to rule~A or to rule~B, and none carries a claim of the thesis. The two scores walk on different edges and are built for different constructs, so on each check of a whole cohort the score ahead is the one whose edges the check counts. Their coverage differs as well: $5{,}193$ of the matched contracts lie outside the allowance graph and $2{,}384$ outside the transfer graph, and each score is zero there (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}). Out of window EndorseRank ranks later new approval pairs below the in-approve degree, with uniform and with activity restarts, while AWP ranks later new senders slightly above the transfer in-degree on the whole cohorts and below it among the W0 spenders that had received a transfer from another contract before the freeze (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}; computed after the labels were known), so a lead of EndorseRank over AWP does not show that propagation over approvals adds anything to the count of approving owners.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:er-awp-same-window]{Same window}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} gives the family means of the two scores with their intervals, and the last row of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} their agreement. The two patterns cross: AWP tracks the transfer family most closely and the allowance family least, while EndorseRank tracks the allowance family most closely. Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff} tests five paired differences on the same contract resamples (Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests}), numbered i to v in the order of its rows.

\input{results/tables/tau-diff.tex}

All five intervals lie above zero. AWP's transfer alignment is higher than EndorseRank's (contrast~ii, $+0.458$, $[0.438,0.476]$), EndorseRank's allowance alignment is higher than AWP's (contrast~iii, $+0.613$, $[0.591,0.632]$), and AWP leads EndorseRank on the stability family (contrast~iv, $+0.422$, $[0.404,0.438]$). The remaining two compare EndorseRank with itself: its allowance alignment is higher than its transfer alignment (contrast~i, $+0.542$, $[0.520,0.563]$), and its in-approve-degree alignment is higher than its in-degree alignment (contrast~v, $+0.542$, $[0.521,0.564]$). Ties weigh on these coefficients: EndorseRank ties $65.4\%$ of the contract pairs and AWP $13.7\%$, mostly among the contracts outside each graph (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}; Appendix~\ref{sec:kendall-properties}). With those contracts kept as isolated nodes and not scored zero, EndorseRank's allowance mean rises, its transfer and stability means fall and AWP's means stay as they are, so the gaps of contrasts~i to~iv widen in their point estimates (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).

The opposite directions of contrasts~ii and~iii follow from the constructs. Each score is ahead on the family built from its own edges, and the main proxies of each family include the in-degree that the corresponding PageRank smooths, so the agreement is partly built in and checks the intended construct of each score. Neither difference says which score is the better measure of reputation.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:proxy-family-detail]{Proxy detail}

Each family mean condenses several per-proxy coefficients. Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} to~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability} report them one proxy at a time for EndorseRank and AWP, as Spearman $\rho$, Kendall $\tau$ and the 95\% interval of $\tau$; the transfer proxies are the ones used in the evaluation of AWP by its authors \parencite{do2023awp}. Every proxy is computed from the events between contracts, and a contract outside a layer has zero on the proxies of that layer.

\input{results/tables/alignment-transfer.tex}

On the transfer family (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer})\label{sub:transfer-family} AWP agrees with the in-value almost as closely as with the in-degree, for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:same-window-alignment}. EndorseRank's transfer coefficients have intervals that overlap neither zero nor AWP's intervals. Part of them is coverage: the $2{,}173$ contracts outside both graphs lie at the bottom of both orderings, and among the $4{,}270$ contracts inside the union graph the two coefficients are $0.174$ ($[0.146,0.200]$) and $0.153$ ($[0.127,0.177]$).

\input{results/tables/alignment-allowance.tex}

On the allowance family (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-allowance})\label{sub:allowance-family} EndorseRank follows the in-approve degree and the in-approve value about equally, for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:same-window-alignment}. AWP's coefficients lie above zero, also among the contracts inside the union graph ($0.122$, $[0.091,0.151]$, and $0.116$, $[0.087,0.145]$): among the cohort's contracts, those with capped allowances from more owner contracts also tend to be central in the transfer graph.

\input{results/tables/alignment-sybil-stability.tex}

On the stability family (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability})\label{sub:sybil-family} AWP is ahead of EndorseRank on all three proxies, as one would expect from a transfer score that rewards activity spread over the window; the paired difference on the family mean excludes zero (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}, contrast~iv). Coverage shapes these coefficients. The $2{,}384$ contracts outside the transfer graph have zero on all three proxies and score zero under AWP, and $2{,}173$ of them score zero under EndorseRank as well. Among the $4{,}270$ contracts inside the union graph AWP's family mean is $0.380$ ($[0.367,0.393]$) and EndorseRank's $0.028$ ($[0.009,0.046]$); on the inbound counterparty ratio AWP's coefficient does not exclude zero there ($0.018$, $[-0.007,0.042]$), and EndorseRank's is negative ($-0.091$, $[-0.112,-0.068]$). The ratio falls when the same senders return, so inside the graph the contracts that EndorseRank ranks high are, on this measure, contracts whose senders return.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:er-awp-holdout]{Out of window}

Table~\ref{tab:endorserank-awp-holdout} collects the paired differences between the two scores on the spender cohorts of both label windows. In every row $a$ is EndorseRank and $b$ AWP. The rows were computed after the labels were known.

\input{results/tables/endorserank-awp-holdout.tex}

On the spenders of W0, EndorseRank ranks new approval pairs above AWP, a paired difference of $+0.070$ (interval $[0.042,0.096]$). On new transfer senders the order reverses, with a difference of $-0.246$ ($[-0.271,-0.220]$). Every label falls after all scored events. The first gap rests on coverage. AWP scores zero for the $2{,}421$ spenders without a transfer pair before the freeze, nearly a third of the cohort, which only allowance edges can order, and EndorseRank for the $5{,}966$ without an allowance edge of positive weight. On the $4{,}569$ spenders that had received a transfer from another contract before the freeze, AWP's coefficient is the higher on both labels (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}), without a paired test.

In July--September the pattern repeats: EndorseRank leads on new approval pairs ($+0.083$, $[0.059,0.110]$) and AWP on new senders ($-0.250$, $[-0.273,-0.225]$).
