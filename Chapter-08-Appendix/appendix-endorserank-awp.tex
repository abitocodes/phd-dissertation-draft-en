\section{EndorseRank and AWP Side by Side}
\label{ch:appendix-er-awp}

Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports every score of the thesis together and reads each PageRank against the raw degree it smooths. This appendix collects the direct comparisons between the two single-layer scores, EndorseRank and AWP: their family means and paired differences in the same window, their per-proxy coefficients, their paired differences out of window, and their contrasts on every trader label. Every number here comes from the revised analysis, with amounts in US dollars over five tokens (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}); the registered analysis is in Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}. None of the comparisons on the matched cohort or on the spender cohorts was fixed in advance, and none of them carries a claim of the thesis; the trader pairs were registered with W1 for the registered analysis and are recomputed here on the revised scores (Section~\ref{sec:er-awp-neutral-grid}). The two scores walk on different edges and are built for different constructs, so on each check over a full cohort the score ahead is the one whose edges the check counts. Outcomes built from neither edge type are the exception, since neither score was built for them; Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results} reports them.

Coverage differs between the two. Of the matched cohort, $523$ contracts lie outside the allowance graph and $2{,}670$ outside the transfer graph, and $2{,}891$ of the $17{,}050$ spenders of W0 had no transfer edge before the freeze, so AWP scores them zero. EndorseRank, by contrast, leaves almost no pair of cohort contracts tied (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}). Out of window the two scores stand differently against the raw degrees they smooth: AWP ranks later new senders above the transfer in-degree in both windows, while EndorseRank ranks later new approval pairs below the in-approve degree in both (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}). A lead of EndorseRank over AWP therefore does not show that propagation over approvals adds anything to the count of approving owners.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:er-awp-same-window]{Same window}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} gives the family means of the two scores with their intervals, and the last row of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} their agreement. The two patterns cross. AWP tracks the transfer family most closely ($0.572$, interval $[0.564,0.580]$) and the allowance family least ($0.193$, $[0.182,0.203]$), while EndorseRank tracks the allowance family most closely ($0.585$, $[0.578,0.592]$) and stands at $0.158$ ($[0.147,0.169]$) on the transfer family. Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff} tests five paired differences on the same contract resamples (Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests}), numbered i to v in the order of its rows.

\input{results/tables/tau-diff.tex}

All five intervals lie above zero. EndorseRank's allowance coefficient exceeds its transfer coefficient (contrast~i, $+0.427$, interval $[0.416,0.440]$); AWP's transfer coefficient exceeds EndorseRank's (contrast~ii, $+0.414$, $[0.401,0.427]$); EndorseRank's allowance coefficient exceeds AWP's (contrast~iii, $+0.392$, $[0.380,0.405]$); AWP leads on the Sybil-stability family (contrast~iv, $+0.388$, $[0.376,0.401]$); and EndorseRank follows the in-approve degree more closely than the in-degree (contrast~v, $+0.473$, $[0.461,0.486]$). Contrasts~i and~v compare EndorseRank with itself across proxies. EndorseRank ties only $0.17\%$ of the contract pairs, so its own ties cap none of these coefficients, and the ties of the proxies do not explain gaps of this size: on the in-degree AWP reaches $0.578$ ($[0.569,0.587]$), where EndorseRank reaches $0.182$ ($[0.169,0.194]$). Keeping the contracts outside the allowance graph as isolated nodes changes none of the family means (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).

The opposite signs of contrasts~ii and~iii follow from the constructs. Each score is ahead on the family built from its own edges, and each of these families includes the in-degree that the corresponding PageRank smooths. The agreement is partly built in and checks the intended construct of each score; it is not evidence from outside the score. Every contract of the cohort received approvals of WETH, USDC, USDT0, ARB or WBTC from at least three owners, and AWP's allowance coefficients lie above zero (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-allowance}): contracts that many owners approve also tend to be central in the transfer graph. Neither difference says which score is the better measure of reputation.

On the scaling stages of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size}, whose values are not comparable with those of the full cohort, the order of the two scores within each family is the same at every stage: AWP is ahead on the transfer and Sybil-stability families and EndorseRank on the allowance family.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:proxy-family-detail]{Proxy detail}

Each family mean condenses several per-proxy coefficients. Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} to~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability} report them one proxy at a time for EndorseRank and AWP, as Spearman $\rho$, Kendall $\tau$ and the 95\% interval of $\tau$. Every proxy counts the events of the five tokens, with amounts in US dollars (Section~\ref{sec:proxy-formulas}). Before correlation, every proxy is oriented so that a higher value means higher expected reputation, and the transfer proxies are the ones used in the evaluation of AWP by its authors \parencite{do2023awp}.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:transfer-family]{Transfer family}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} gives the coefficients of the two scores with the in-degree and the in-value.

\input{results/tables/alignment-transfer.tex}

AWP agrees with the in-degree at $0.578$ (interval $[0.569,0.587]$) and with the in-value at $0.566$ ($[0.558,0.575]$). AWP's value weight is one half at the median transfer of 200 dollars and comes close to one only for amounts several times larger (Section~\ref{sub:awp-graph}), so larger transfers count for more, and AWP follows the summed US-dollar value of a contract's inbound transfers almost as closely as their number. EndorseRank's transfer coefficients lie above zero, at $0.182$ ($[0.169,0.194]$) and $0.134$ ($[0.123,0.145]$), and their intervals lie far below AWP's: contracts that many owners approve receive transfers from more senders than the rest of the cohort. Out of window AWP ranks later new senders above the raw in-degree in both windows (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}), so on the transfer side the walk adds to the count it smooths.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:allowance-family]{Allowance family}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-allowance} gives the coefficients of the two scores with the in-approve degree and the in-approve value.

\input{results/tables/alignment-allowance.tex}

EndorseRank agrees with the in-approve degree at $0.655$ (interval $[0.648,0.662]$) and with the in-approve value, the sum of the capped allowances, at $0.515$ ($[0.506,0.524]$). The value weight gives an allowance of the median finite amount, 62 dollars, one half, but more than half of the positive latest allowances are unlimited or reach 20{,}000 dollars (Table~\ref{tab:usd-tokens}) and count fully, so EndorseRank follows the number of approving owners more closely than the summed amounts they approve. For a spender whose owners receive no approvals it equals the closed form of Section~\ref{sub:theory-closed-form}, a function of the owner-normalized in-strength. AWP agrees with the two proxies at $0.206$ ($[0.193,0.217]$) and $0.180$ ($[0.169,0.191]$). The comparison is partly built in, since EndorseRank is computed from the same approvals as the proxies.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:sybil-family]{Sybil-adjusted stability family}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability} gives the coefficients of the two scores with the inbound counterparty ratio, the transfer tenure and the active months.

\input{results/tables/alignment-sybil-stability.tex}

The table shows AWP ahead of EndorseRank on the transfer tenure ($0.508$, interval $[0.499,0.517]$, against $0.080$, $[0.069,0.091]$) and on the active months ($0.549$, $[0.540,0.558]$, against $0.098$, $[0.087,0.109]$), as one would expect from a transfer score that rewards activity spread over the window; the paired difference on the family mean excludes zero (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}, contrast~iv). On the inbound counterparty ratio the two coefficients have opposite signs, $-0.156$ ($[-0.166,-0.147]$) for EndorseRank and $0.127$ ($[0.114,0.141]$) for AWP. The ratio divides the distinct other senders by the transfers received from them, so it falls when the same senders return; EndorseRank's negative coefficient says that contracts that many owners approve are, on this measure, contracts whose senders return. AWP's positive coefficient comes from coverage: the $2{,}670$ contracts outside the transfer graph have ratio zero and AWP score zero, so they fill the bottom of both orderings. Among the contracts that AWP scores above zero, its order also falls with the ratio, as EndorseRank's does; this check was made after the results were known and has no interval. The family describes how much longitudinal transfer information each score carries.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:er-awp-holdout]{Out of window}

Table~\ref{tab:endorserank-awp-holdout} collects the paired differences between the two scores in both holdout windows. In every row $a$ is EndorseRank and $b$ AWP.

\input{results/tables/endorserank-awp-holdout.tex}

On the spenders of W0, EndorseRank ranks new approval pairs at $\tau=0.231$ (interval $[0.220,0.242]$) against $0.196$ ($[0.181,0.210]$) for AWP, a paired difference of $+0.035$ ($[0.019,0.051]$). On new transfer senders the order reverses: AWP reaches $0.346$ ($[0.336,0.356]$) against $0.200$ ($[0.189,0.210]$) for EndorseRank, a difference of $-0.146$ ($[-0.156,-0.136]$). Every label falls after all scored events, so on this cohort each score ranks the later arrivals of its own edge type better than the other score does. Coverage accounts for part of the first gap. AWP scores zero for the $2{,}891$ spenders without a transfer edge before the freeze, and $13.3\%$ of them gained a new approval pair, against $11.9\%$ of the rest. On the $14{,}094$ spenders that had received a transfer from another address, the order on new approval pairs reverses: AWP reaches $0.298$ ($[0.287,0.308]$) and EndorseRank $0.236$ ($[0.224,0.249]$), without a paired test (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}). On new senders AWP stays ahead there, at $0.357$ ($[0.347,0.367]$) against $0.231$ ($[0.219,0.244]$).

In July--September, with the contrasts computed from the revised W1 scores after all labels were known, the pattern on the full spender cohort repeats. EndorseRank ranks new approval pairs at $0.234$ ($[0.222,0.244]$) against $0.169$ ($[0.157,0.183]$) for AWP ($+0.065$, $[0.051,0.081]$), and AWP ranks new senders at $0.330$ ($[0.321,0.340]$) against $0.194$ ($[0.184,0.205]$) ($-0.136$, $[-0.146,-0.127]$). On the liquidation-free close rate of the traders, a pair that the registration of W1 named for the registered analysis, EndorseRank reaches $0.224$ ($[0.105,0.339]$) in W0 and $0.297$ ($[0.171,0.410]$) in W1, and AWP $0.240$ ($[0.073,0.380]$) and $0.329$ ($[0.207,0.440]$). Neither difference can be told from zero ($-0.017$, $[-0.156,0.128]$, and $-0.032$, $[-0.133,0.066]$); the trader cohorts hold $88$ and $70$ contracts, so the intervals are wide.

On the W0 traders' profit labels (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-traders}), EndorseRank's intervals on profitable closes and realized gain include zero, at $0.150$ ($[-0.019,0.295]$) and $0.078$ ($[-0.094,0.226]$), while AWP's lie above zero, at $0.155$ ($[0.005,0.297]$) and $0.196$ ($[0.048,0.351]$); both labels grow with the number of closes. On the two shares, which do not, both scores are negative, and for both the interval lies below zero on the non-loss share only: AWP at $-0.254$ ($[-0.404,-0.104]$) against $-0.124$ ($[-0.265,0.015]$) on the profitable share, and EndorseRank at $-0.178$ ($[-0.298,-0.042]$) against $-0.055$ ($[-0.196,0.084]$). The transfer in-degree is negative on both shares as well, at $-0.191$ ($[-0.329,-0.041]$) and $-0.197$ ($[-0.350,-0.035]$). Neither score ranks these contract traders by the share of successful closes.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:er-awp-neutral-grid]{All trader labels in both windows}

Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-grid} sets the allowance side against the transfer side on all six trader labels in both windows. Its four pairs and six labels were registered with W1 for the registered analysis, before the logs of July--September were extracted (Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-protocol}); by then that analysis had scored the W0 traders on the profit labels but not on their liquidation labels. The table gives the same pairs on the revised scores, which were defined after every registered result, the liquidation labels included, was known, so its entries are a re-analysis and not registered tests (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}). P and Q on the liquidation-free close rate, in W1 and with their signs in W0, enter rules R1 and R2, and P on the two shares in W1 enters rule R3, all applied to the revised scores (Claim~C6, Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}); the other entries, the column for EndorseRank minus AWP included, are reported beside them.

\input{results/tables/neutral-label-grid.tex}

In W1 no pair separates the two sides on either liquidation label: every interval includes zero, P on the liquidation-free close rate at $+0.084$ ($[-0.013,0.176]$) and the EndorseRank pair at $-0.032$ ($[-0.133,0.066]$). In W0 the counts favor the allowance side on both liquidation labels, P at $+0.182$ ($[0.052,0.314]$) on the liquidation-free close rate and $+0.197$ ($[0.059,0.333]$) on the no-liquidation flag, while the three PageRank pairs lie below zero with intervals that include zero. On the four profit labels of W1 every pair is negative. The intervals of Q and of the EndorseRank pair lie below zero on all four, those of P on the two shares, and those of the C-PR layers on realized gain and on both shares. In W0 every interval on the profit labels includes zero except that of the C-PR layers on realized gain, $-0.248$ ($[-0.433,-0.067]$), which thus lies below zero in both windows on a label that grows with the number of closes; on the two shares Q, the C-PR layers and the EndorseRank pair lean to the allowance side with intervals that include zero, so the shares show no direction that holds in both windows. With $70$ and $88$ traders, none of these differences is precise.
