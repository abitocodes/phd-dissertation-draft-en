\section{Elementary Properties of PageRank and Rank Correlation}
\label{ch:appendix-theory}

Chapters~\ref{ch:literature}--\ref{ch:results} rely on the elementary properties summarized here. The material is standard: the PageRank properties follow \textcite{page1999pagerank} and \textcite{langville2006pagerank}, and the rank correlations \textcite{spearman1904proof} and \textcite{kendall1938rank}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-existence]{Existence and uniqueness of PageRank}

Take $P$ to be a row-stochastic matrix on $|V|$ nodes, possibly after the dangling-node repair of Equation~\eqref{eq:transition}. For $d\in(0,1)$ the Google matrix of Equation~\eqref{eq:google-matrix},
\[
G_{\mathrm{PR}}=d P^{\top}+\frac{1-d}{|V|}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top},
\]
is positive and column-stochastic. By the Perron--Frobenius theorem for positive matrices, $G_{\mathrm{PR}}$ has a single eigenvalue of modulus one, and the corresponding eigenvector $\mathbf{r}$ is strictly positive; we can scale it so that $\sum_i r_i=1$. It follows that, once the damping and the dangling-node rules are fixed, every directed graph has unique PageRank scores \parencite{langville2006pagerank}.

Three restart vectors in this thesis are not uniform. AWP's, proportional to activeness, is zero at every address that has sent nothing, that of EndorseRank with activity restarts is zero at every address that has approved nothing, and S-PR's, the normalized score of the allowance walk, is zero at every node of the transfer layer that has no allowance score, so none of the three Google matrices is positive. The fixed point is still unique: for two probability vectors the restart term cancels in their difference, and the operator multiplies their $\|\cdot\|_1$ distance by at most $d$. In general it is not strictly positive, however, because a node with no restart mass that no path from a restart node reaches keeps a score of zero. Uniqueness means that, once its parameters are fixed, each method assigns exactly one score vector to a given edge set, so any difference between $\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{ER}}$ and $\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{AWP}}$ arises from their edges and restart distributions.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-continuity]{Continuity in edge weights}

For fixed $d\in(0,1)$, PageRank depends continuously on the entries of $P$. If $\tilde P$ is a second row-stochastic matrix on the same nodes, with PageRank vector $\tilde{\mathbf{r}}$, then
\[
\|\mathbf{r}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}}\|_1\le\frac{d}{1-d}\,\max_i\bigl\|P_{i\cdot}-\tilde P_{i\cdot}\bigr\|_1,
\]
a bound that does not depend on $|V|$ \parencite{langville2006pagerank}.

Continuity in $P$ is not the same as continuity in the edge weights. A row $P_{i\cdot}=w_{i\cdot}/o_i$ moves continuously with the weights only while the out-strength $o_i$ stays positive. When a node gains its first out-edge or loses its last one, its row jumps between the uniform dangling row and a row concentrated on its out-neighbors, however small the weight involved. In the worked example of Section~\ref{sec:pagerank-worked-example}, adding an edge $D\to A$ of weight $10^{-12}$ moves $\mathbf{r}$ by $0.37$ in the $\|\cdot\|_1$ norm and raises $r_A$ from $0.1375$ to $0.3202$.

The damping sweep is covered by continuity: it keeps $P$ fixed and changes $d$ smoothly, and for $d<1$ the PageRank vector varies continuously with $d$. The value-slope check is covered only in part. Since $V(z)>0$ for every $z>0$, a change of the slope $b$ adds or removes no edge in exact arithmetic, but in double precision an edge that carries only tiny amounts can round to zero weight, and a tenfold change of $b$ is too large for the bound above to say much, so the stability of that check rests on the data (Table~\ref{tab:robustness-slope}). Empirically, the family means of EndorseRank move by at most $0.0031$ and those of AWP by at most $0.021$ across $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}).

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-sparsity]{Sparsity and per-iteration cost}

With $m=|E|$ nonzero edges and $n=|V|$ nodes, the sparse product $P^{\top}\mathbf{x}$ costs $O(m)$ arithmetic operations, each iteration also updates vectors of length $n$, and building the sparse operator from the edge list is $O(m)$, so power iteration over $I$ iterations is $O(m+(m+n)I)$. Both scores run the same solver. In the measured calls an iteration of AWP takes about $1.95$ times as long as one of EndorseRank, less than the edge ratio of $2.21$, and AWP needs more iterations (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-results}), so neither $m$ nor $I$ alone sets the time ratio.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:spearman-properties]{Properties of Spearman's \texorpdfstring{$\rho$}{rho}}

Spearman's $\rho$ lies in $[-1,1]$. A value of $\rho=1$ means perfect monotone agreement, and $\rho=-1$ perfect reverse agreement. The evaluation pipeline computes $\rho$ and $\tau_b$ with standard tie-aware implementations. When $n$ is large, $\rho$ and $\tau$ usually agree in sign, but they differ in magnitude. Across the fourteen pairs of EndorseRank or AWP with a proxy (Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} to~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability}), every $|\tau|$ exceeds $0.05$, the ratio $\rho/\tau$ lies between about $1.2$ and $1.5$ except for AWP against the inbound-counterparty ratio, where $\tau=0.055$ and $\rho=0.163$, and $|\rho|$ is always the larger of the two; no pair has coefficients of opposite sign.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:kendall-properties]{Properties of Kendall's \texorpdfstring{$\tau$}{tau}}

Among the scores of this thesis ties are rare: on the matched cohort no score ties more than $3.4\%$ of the contract pairs, and Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence} gives the shares of EndorseRank and AWP. The denominator of $\tau_b$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:kendall} differs from $n_0$ only through ties, so for EndorseRank against AWP it differs from $n_0$ by less than two percent, and $\tau_a$, computed from the pair counts of Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}, lies within two percent of the reported $\tau_b$.

Ties in a proxy cap $\tau_b$ as well: the integer proxies, such as the in-degree and the in-approve degree, tie many pairs, and the largest $\tau_b$ that any score can reach against such a proxy falls with the share of tied pairs. Because the PageRank scores tie few pairs, this cap differs little between two of them compared on one proxy, so such a contrast is not distorted by it; a contrast between a PageRank and a raw degree, or between two proxies, can be, to the extent that their ties differ (Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests}).
