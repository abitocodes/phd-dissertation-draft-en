\section{Elementary Properties of PageRank and Rank Correlation}
\label{ch:appendix-theory}

This appendix summarizes the elementary properties used implicitly throughout Chapters~\ref{ch:literature}--\ref{ch:results}. This material is standard \cend{15,23,20,21}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-existence]{Existence and uniqueness of PageRank}

Let $P$ be a row-stochastic matrix on $n$ nodes (possibly with dangling-node repair as in Equation~\eqref{eq:transition}). For $d\in(0,1)$ the Google matrix
\[
G_{\mathrm{PR}}=d P^{\top}+\frac{1-d}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
\]
is positive and column-stochastic (up to the transpose convention of Equation~\eqref{eq:google-matrix}). From the Perron--Frobenius theorem for positive matrices, $G_{\mathrm{PR}}$ has a single eigenvalue of modulus one and a corresponding eigenvector $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ that is strictly positive, and we can normalize it such that $\sum_i\pi_i=1$. So there are unique PageRank scores for any directed graph, assuming fixed damping and dangling-node rules \cend{23}.

Uniqueness ensures a well-defined comparison between EndorseRank and AWP: with fixed parameters, each method defines only one score vector on a given edge set. Hence differences between $\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{ER}}$ and $\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{AWP}}$ must stem from edge semantics and weights rather than multiple solvers.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-continuity]{Continuity in edge weights}

PageRank is a continuous function of the entries of $P$ for fixed $d\in(0,1)$. Allowing for minor variations in the values of the allowance and transfer weights will have only a small effect on scores in the $\|\cdot\|_1$ norm, with Lipschitz constants dependent on $d$ and $n$ \cend{23}.

Continuity enables us to carry out robustness checks by changing damping or limiting to the top tokens. If alignment was overly sensitive to the exact hyperparameters, such small changes would disrupt the family-level $\tau$ altogether. Empirically, EndorseRank's allowance family mean is unchanged to three decimal places across $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}), suggesting a stable ranking geometry.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-sparsity]{Sparsity and per-iteration cost}

A sparse matrix--vector product $P^{\top}\mathbf{x}$ takes $O(m)$ arithmetic operations for $m$ nonzero edges, and constructing the sparse operator from the edge list also takes $O(m)$ time. So power iteration takes $O(m + mI)$ time for $I$ iterations. The transfer graph on the matched cohort has around $9.3$ times as many edges as the endorsement graph, meaning the transfer method would take approximately an order of magnitude longer even with identical $I$. The gap is further widened by different iteration counts and the measured wall-time ratio (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}) is on the same scale as the edge ratio.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:spearman-properties]{Properties of Spearman's \texorpdfstring{$\rho$}{rho}}

Spearman's $\rho$ is Pearson's correlation applied to the ranks. It is invariant to strictly increasing transformations of either variable and takes values in $[-1,1]$. $\rho=1$ indicates perfect monotone agreement and $\rho=-1$ indicates perfect reverse agreement. Ties are handled via mid-rank adjustments; the evaluation pipeline uses standard tie-aware implementations. Due to the continuous and heavy-tailed nature of reputation scores and proxies, rank-based association is more appropriate than Pearson correlation on raw values. The AWP baseline paper similarly evaluates its ranking using rank statistics~\cend{3}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:kendall-properties]{Properties of Kendall's \texorpdfstring{$\tau$}{tau}}

Kendall's $\tau$ is the normalized difference between the number of concordant and discordant pairwise orderings. It can be understood probabilistically: for two randomly chosen distinct wallets, $\tau$ is the probability their relative ordering is the same under both rankings, minus the probability their relative ordering is different \cend{21}. A $\tau$ in the mid-$0.3$ range, like EndorseRank on the allowance family (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}), implies a substantial but imperfect agreement between the two rankings.

For large $n$, $\tau$ and $\rho$ tend to have similar signs and magnitudes, though they are not the same. We report both, following the AWP baseline~\cend{3}, and we use the family-mean $\tau$ with a paired-bootstrap interval~\cend{34} as our main summary statistic.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:construct-validity-note]{Construct validity note}

Cronbach and Meehl~\cend{22} distinguish between criterion-related validity and construct validity. Without a definitive gold standard default label, this research uses a construct-oriented approach: each method is tested against validation checks that embody its conceptual definition (e.g.\ receiving allowances for EndorseRank; making inbound transfers for AWP), as well as a holdout period of future new approvals. High within-construct $\tau$ supports interpretation. Low cross-construct $\tau$ is not automatically failure; it may indicate discriminant validity.
