\section{Elementary Properties of PageRank and Rank Correlation}
\label{ch:appendix-theory}

Chapters~\ref{ch:literature}--\ref{ch:results} rely on the elementary properties summarized here. The material is standard: the PageRank properties follow \textcite{page1999pagerank} and \textcite{langville2006pagerank}, and the rank correlations \textcite{spearman1904proof} and \textcite{kendall1938rank}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-existence]{Existence and uniqueness of PageRank}

Take $P$ to be a row-stochastic matrix on $|V|$ nodes, possibly after the dangling-node repair of Equation~\eqref{eq:transition}. For $d\in(0,1)$ the Google matrix of Equation~\eqref{eq:google-matrix},
\[
G_{\mathrm{PR}}=d P^{\top}+\frac{1-d}{|V|}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top},
\]
is positive and column-stochastic. By the Perron--Frobenius theorem for positive matrices, $G_{\mathrm{PR}}$ has a single eigenvalue of modulus one, and the corresponding eigenvector $\mathbf{r}$ is strictly positive; we can scale it so that $\sum_i r_i=1$. It follows that, once the damping and the dangling-node rules are fixed, every directed graph has unique PageRank scores \parencite{langville2006pagerank}.

Two restart vectors in this thesis are not uniform. AWP's, proportional to activeness, is zero at every address that has sent nothing, and S-PR's, the normalized score of the allowance walk, is zero at every node of the transfer layer that has no allowance score, so neither Google matrix is positive. The fixed point is still unique: for two probability vectors the restart term cancels in their difference, and the operator multiplies their $\|\cdot\|_1$ distance by at most $d$. In general it is not strictly positive, however, because a node with no restart mass that no path from a restart node reaches keeps a score of zero. This is why AWP scores zero every cohort contract outside the transfer graph under both rules of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}.

Uniqueness guarantees a well-defined comparison between EndorseRank and AWP. Once its parameters are fixed, each method assigns exactly one score vector to a given edge set, so any difference between $\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{ER}}$ and $\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{AWP}}$ must arise from their edges and restart distributions and cannot be traced to the solver.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-continuity]{Continuity in edge weights}

For fixed $d\in(0,1)$, PageRank depends continuously on the entries of $P$. If $\tilde P$ is a second row-stochastic matrix on the same nodes, with PageRank vector $\tilde{\mathbf{r}}$, then
\[
\|\mathbf{r}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}}\|_1\le\frac{d}{1-d}\,\max_i\bigl\|P_{i\cdot}-\tilde P_{i\cdot}\bigr\|_1,
\]
a bound that does not depend on $|V|$ \parencite{langville2006pagerank}.

Continuity in $P$ is not the same as continuity in the edge weights. A row $P_{i\cdot}=w_{i\cdot}/o_i$ moves continuously with the weights only while the out-strength $o_i$ stays positive. When a node gains its first out-edge or loses its last one, its row jumps between the uniform dangling row and a row concentrated on its out-neighbors, however small the weight involved. In the worked example of Section~\ref{sec:pagerank-worked-example}, adding an edge $D\to A$ of weight $10^{-12}$ moves $\mathbf{r}$ by $0.37$ in the $\|\cdot\|_1$ norm and raises $r_A$ from $0.1375$ to $0.3202$.

The damping sweep is covered by continuity: it keeps $P$ fixed and changes $d$ smoothly, and for $d<1$ the PageRank vector varies continuously with $d$. The value-slope check is covered only in part. Changing the slope $b$ of $V$ changes the weight of every finite amount, while an unlimited allowance keeps $V=1$, and in exact arithmetic it adds or removes no edge, since $V(z)>0$ for every $z>0$, so $P$ moves continuously with $b$. In floating point an edge that carries only tiny amounts can round to zero weight (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}), and a change of $b$ can move such an edge to or from zero. A tenfold change of $b$ is not small, however, and the bound above says little about it, so the stability of that check rests on the data (Table~\ref{tab:robustness-slope}). Empirically, the family means of EndorseRank and AWP change little across $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$, AWP's transfer mean the most (Section~\ref{sub:damping}; Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}), although the number of iterations grows with $d$.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:pr-sparsity]{Sparsity and per-iteration cost}

With $m=|E|$ nonzero edges, the sparse product $P^{\top}\mathbf{x}$ costs $O(m)$ arithmetic operations, and building the sparse operator from the edge list is also $O(m)$, so power iteration over $I$ iterations is $O(m+mI)$. On the matched cohort the transfer graph has $2.23$ times as many edges as the allowance graph (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-results}), so at equal iteration counts the transfer method would take about $2.23$ times as long. Both scores run the same solver, so any further difference in time comes from the iteration counts and from the vector operations over $|V|$, not from the algorithm. In the measured calls the time ratio of AWP to EndorseRank is $2.17$, with $92$ and $69$ iterations (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-results}), so the transfer method's time per edge and iteration is lower than EndorseRank's on these graphs.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:spearman-properties]{Properties of Spearman's \texorpdfstring{$\rho$}{rho}}

Spearman's $\rho$ is Pearson's correlation computed on ranks. It lies in $[-1,1]$ and does not change under strictly increasing transformations of either variable. A value of $\rho=1$ means perfect monotone agreement, and $\rho=-1$ perfect reverse agreement. Tied values receive mid-ranks, and the evaluation pipeline relies on standard tie-aware implementations. Because reputation scores and proxies are continuous and heavy-tailed, rank-based association suits them better than Pearson correlation on the raw values. The AWP baseline paper likewise evaluates its ranking with rank statistics \parencite{do2023awp}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:kendall-properties]{Properties of Kendall's \texorpdfstring{$\tau$}{tau}}

Kendall's $\tau$ compares the numbers of concordant and discordant pairs, $n_c$ and $n_d$. Without tie correction, $\tau_a=(n_c-n_d)/n_0$ with $n_0=n(n-1)/2$ is the probability that two randomly drawn contracts are ordered the same way by both rankings minus the probability that they are ordered oppositely \parencite{kendall1938rank}. Every reported $\tau$ is the tie-corrected $\tau_b$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:kendall}, whose denominator removes the pairs tied on either ranking from $n_0$. The two coincide without ties, and among the scores of this thesis ties are rare: on the matched cohort no score ties more than $3.2\%$ of the contract pairs, and Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence} gives the shares of EndorseRank and AWP. For EndorseRank against AWP the denominator of $\tau_b$ therefore differs from $n_0$ by less than two percent, and $\tau_a$, computed from the pair counts of Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}, lies within two percent of the reported $\tau_b$ (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).

Ties in a proxy cap $\tau_b$ as well. The integer proxies, such as the in-degree and the in-approve degree, tie many pairs, and the largest $\tau_b$ any score can reach against such a proxy falls with the share of tied pairs. Because no score here ties more than $3.2\%$ of the pairs, this cap differs little between the scores compared on one proxy, so paired contrasts on one proxy are not distorted by it. Contrasts between proxies can be, to the extent that the proxies' ties differ.

When $n$ is large, $\tau$ and $\rho$ usually agree in sign, but they differ in magnitude. Across the fourteen pairs of EndorseRank or AWP with a proxy (Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} to~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability}), every $|\tau|$ exceeds $0.07$, the ratio $\rho/\tau$ lies between about $1.2$ and $1.8$, and $|\rho|$ is always the larger of the two. No pair has coefficients of opposite sign. We report both, as the AWP baseline does \parencite{do2023awp}. Our main summary statistic is the family-mean $\tau$ with a paired-bootstrap interval \parencite{efron1979bootstrap}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:construct-validity-note]{Construct validity note}

\textcite{cronbach1955construct} distinguish criterion-related validity from construct validity. Since no definitive gold-standard label of contract reputation exists, this research takes a construct-oriented approach. Each score is checked against statistics that follow from its conceptual definition, such as receiving allowances for EndorseRank and receiving inbound transfers for AWP. Agreement with statistics built from a score's own edges is a check of the intended construct and is partly built in. Each score is also set against later arrivals of the same constructs, new approval pairs and new transfer senders, against the raw degree it smooths, and in the windows W1 and W0 against a trader outcome built from neither edge type. Agreement within a construct supports the intended reading of a score, subject to that overlap. Low agreement across constructs does not by itself signal failure, since a score built for one construct need not track the other.
