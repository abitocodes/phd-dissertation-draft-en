\section{Claim-to-Evidence Map}
\label{ch:appendix-claims}

This appendix maps each hypothesis, research question, and empirical claim to the relevant evidence in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h1]{H1 / RQ1: Rank divergence}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: on the same cohort, EndorseRank and AWP rank wallets differently.
\item Evidence: inter-method Kendall $\tau_b=0.316$ with its 95\% CI (last row of Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}; Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}); the rank scatter plot in Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}; score distributions plotted in Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}; $\tau_b=-0.025$ when the wallets outside each graph are kept as isolated nodes (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).
\item Interpretation: the rankings are not equivalent, and their agreement comes from the wallets that EndorseRank scores zero; the O1 criterion is met (Table~\ref{tab:objectives-achievement}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h2]{H2 / RQ2: Construct-specific alignment}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: at the same time points, the two methods produce different results on allowance vs.\ transfer checks.
\item Evidence: family means with their CIs (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}); the paired $\Delta\tau$ contrasts ii and iii (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}); per-proxy results (Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} and~\ref{tab:alignment-allowance}); the heatmap in Figure~\ref{fig:alignment-heatmap}.
\item Note: contrasts i and v in Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff} include zero, and for EndorseRank their two coefficients have different ceilings (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}), so the claim is limited to between-method comparisons. Both contrasts are large and positive with the wallets outside the allowance graph kept as isolated nodes (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}), a rule tried after the results were known.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h3]{H3 / RQ3: Computational efficiency}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: at the same $n$, EndorseRank runs faster than AWP because it has fewer edges.
\item Evidence: Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} (runtime, SD, memory, iterations, $|V|$, $|E|$); Tables~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling-incohort} and~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling}; Figure~\ref{fig:runtime-scaling}.
\item Note: the expanded-pool stages add sampled addresses but no edges, and hence no solver-graph nodes, beyond the matched-cohort graph (Section~\ref{sub:runtime-scaling}); the claim is confined to edge count.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-rq4]{RQ4: Temporal holdout}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: with scores fixed at $t_1$, EndorseRank predicts the new owner--spender approvals that appear after $t_1$ and up to $t_2$ on the spender cohort ($n=1{,}335$) better than AWP does, but not better than the $t_1$ in-approve degree.
\item Evidence: Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} and~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}). EndorseRank minus AWP is above zero and EndorseRank minus the $t_1$ in-approve degree is below zero; on new transfer senders, AWP minus the $t_1$ in-degree is also below zero.
\item Qualification: both labels are the constructs as observed in a later period (new approvals, new senders), not credit or trading outcomes, and both are counts of arrivals. RQ4 replaced the proposal's trading-success validation (Section~\ref{sub:gf-pr}). The lead on new approvals also holds against AWP in its published form; the lead on new senders does not (Table~\ref{tab:awp-paper-form}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-rq5]{RQ5: Coupled operator}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: under the rule of 14~September, a per-node convex mixture of the allowance and transfer layers (C-PR) does not beat both single-layer methods, and the seeded ablation (S-PR) reproduces AWP. The registered replication does not confirm that C-PR improves on AWP alone, as it did in an exploratory comparison on the spring holdout: F1 and F3 hold, F2 fails.
\item Evidence, comparison with AWP alone (spender cohort, exploratory): C-PR minus AWP is $+0.251$ on new approval pairs (interval $[0.203,0.306]$) and $+0.007$ on new transfer senders (interval $[-0.012,0.028]$). Registered replication (June--August, Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}): C-PR minus AWP is $+0.179$ on new approval pairs (interval $[0.132,0.225]$; F1 holds), $-0.011$ on new transfer senders (interval $[-0.025,0.003]$; F2 fails, since the lower end lies below $-0.02$) and $+0.018$ on the traders' liquidation-free close rate (interval $[0.011,0.024]$; F3 holds). The decision, which needs all three, is not met.
\item Evidence, comparison with AWP in its published form (spender cohort, post hoc): C-PR minus the published form is $+0.172$ on new approval pairs (interval $[0.111,0.226]$) and $-0.067$ on new transfer senders (interval $[-0.095,-0.039]$) (Section~\ref{sub:awp-paper-form}).
\item Evidence, primary rule (spender cohort): Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}. Neither condition is met. At $\lambda=0.5$, C-PR is below EndorseRank on new approval pairs ($\Delta\tau=-0.184$, interval $[-0.213,-0.149]$) and not above AWP on new transfer senders ($\Delta\tau=+0.007$, interval $[-0.012,0.028]$).
\item Evidence, secondary rule (matched cohort): Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}. The rule asks that C-PR's point estimate lie inside the 95\% CI of the better single-layer method on the transfer and allowance families and that C-PR exceed both single-layer methods on Sybil stability. The transfer coefficient ($0.527$) lies inside AWP's interval $[0.522,0.547]$, and C-PR exceeds both methods on Sybil stability, by $+0.004$ over AWP and $+0.057$ over EndorseRank with both paired intervals above zero (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}). The allowance coefficient ($-0.001$) lies far outside EndorseRank's interval $[0.322,0.384]$, so the secondary rule fails on its allowance part. The paired contrasts of that table are reported alongside as a stricter check; on transfer they put C-PR slightly but significantly below AWP ($\Delta\tau=-0.007$, interval $[-0.009,-0.005]$).
\item Evidence, cost: runtime rows of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}.
\item Qualification: tested with fixed $\lambda\in\{0.25,0.5,0.75\}$, uniform teleportation, transition-level mixing and one chain. The rule of 14~September and the values of $\lambda$ were set in the configuration before any coupled score was computed (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}); the rule for the comparison with AWP alone was written on 28~September~2026 for the June--August window and registered on 1~October~2026 together with two sensitivity analyses, one with AWP in its published form, before the extraction (Section~\ref{sub:fresh-holdout}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-c1c4]{Claims C1--C5}

\begin{itemize}
\item C1: the runtime evidence listed under H3/RQ3. The AWP/EndorseRank runtime ratio is $10.2\times$ on the matched cohort and ranges from $8.1\times$ to $12.2\times$ across the in-cohort and pool stages. In a profiling run on the matched cohort, matrix assembly takes about 94\% of the timed call and the iterations under 2\%; assembly is $O(|E|)$ like the iterations, so the ratio follows the edge ratio of about $9.3$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}). The claim is specific to EndorseRank, since C-PR costs at least as much as AWP (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}).
\item C2: intended-construct checks in Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}, namely allowance for EndorseRank and transfer for AWP.
\item C3: the construct-specific winners identified under H2/RQ2, tested in Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}; both between-method contrasts keep their sign against AWP in its published form (Section~\ref{sub:awp-paper-form}).
\item C4: the out-of-window comparison on the spender holdout (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} and~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}), bounded by the degree baselines and by the re-weighting and published-form checks (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-weighting} and~\ref{tab:awp-paper-form}), with the exploratory trader run (Section~\ref{sub:trader-results}) as its limit on the traders and the registered June--August window as its replication: the lead on new approvals replicates ($+0.280$), the lead on new senders does not (Section~\ref{sub:fresh-results}); robustness is reported in Tables~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}--\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size} and Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity}.
\item C5: coupling does not beat both single-layer methods, a bounded negative result, and the registered replication does not confirm its exploratory gain over AWP alone (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}); see RQ5 above. On the holdout, C-PR lies between EndorseRank and AWP on new approval pairs at every $\lambda$, and on new transfer senders at $\lambda=0.5$ and $0.75$; at $\lambda=0.25$ it is $0.004$ below AWP on new senders, with overlapping intervals (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders}). In the same window, at every $\lambda$, it is below AWP but within $0.015$ of it on the transfer family, near zero on the allowance family (from $-0.009$ to $0.006$, against $0.355$ for EndorseRank), and above both single-layer methods on Sybil stability (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-nonclaims]{Explicit non-claims}

This research does \emph{not} claim:

\begin{itemize}
\item that AWP has become obsolete;
\item that EndorseRank aligns more closely with the allowance family than with the transfer family (the two coefficients have different ceilings);
\item that either EndorseRank or AWP forecasts the future growth of its own construct better than the raw $t_1$ degree does;
\item that the AWP of this thesis is the published method (it is an adaptation; Section~\ref{sub:awp-graph});
\item that EndorseRank predicts new transfer senders better than AWP whatever the edge weights (the lead holds for raw amounts only, and not against AWP in its published form);
\item that C-PR improves on AWP (the registered rule failed on new senders) or on AWP in its published form (it falls behind that form on new senders in both windows);
\item that no combination of the two edge types can beat the single-layer methods, only that the per-node convex mixture tested here, with fixed $\lambda$, does not;
\item that runtime scales favorably when the edge population grows beyond the cohort graph;
\item that, without replication, the results generalize beyond Arbitrum One and the study window;
\item that on-chain scores substitute for collateral or legal identity systems.
\end{itemize}
