\section{Claim-to-Evidence Map}
\label{ch:appendix-claims}

This appendix links each hypothesis, research question, and empirical claim to the corresponding evidence in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h1]{H1 / RQ1: Rank divergence}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: EndorseRank and AWP produce different wallet rankings on the same cohort.
\item Evidence: inter-method Kendall $\tau$ with 95\% CI (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}, last row; Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}); rank scatter plot in Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}; score distribution plots in Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}.
\item Interpretation: related but non-equivalent rankings; O1 criterion is satisfied (Table~\ref{tab:objectives-achievement}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h2]{H2 / RQ2: Construct-specific alignment}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: the two methods yield different results on allowance vs.\ transfer checks at the same time points.
\item Evidence: family means with CI (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}); paired $\Delta\tau$ contrasts ii and iii (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}); per-proxy detail (Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer} and~\ref{tab:alignment-allowance}); heatmap in Figure~\ref{fig:alignment-heatmap}.
\item Note: contrasts i and v in Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff} contain zero; the claim applies only to between-method comparisons.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h3]{H3 / RQ3: Computational efficiency}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: EndorseRank is faster than AWP at the same $n$, due to fewer edges.
\item Evidence: Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} (runtime, SD, memory, iterations, $|V|$, $|E|$); Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling-incohort}; Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling}; Figure~\ref{fig:runtime-scaling}.
\item Note: expansion steps add nodes without adding edges (Section~\ref{sub:runtime-scaling}); the claim is restricted to edge count.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-rq4]{RQ4: Temporal holdout}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: after scores are fixed at t1, EndorseRank predicts t2 new owner--spender approvals on inbound spenders better than AWP; it does not predict them better than the t1 in-approve degree.
\item Evidence: Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} and~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}): EndorseRank minus AWP is greater than zero; EndorseRank minus t1 in-approve degree is less than zero; AWP minus t1 in-degree on new transfer senders is less than zero.
\item Qualification: both labels are the constructs in the future tense (new approvals, new senders), not credit or trading outcomes, and both are counts of arrivals; RQ4 replaced the proposal's trading-success comparison (Section~\ref{sub:gf-pr}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-rq5]{RQ5: Coupled operator}

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: a per-node convex mixture of the allowance and transfer layers (C-PR) does not rank wallets better than the appropriate single-layer method; the seeded ablation (S-PR) reproduces AWP.
\item Evidence: Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} (pre-registered primary rule, both parts not met); Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} and~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} (same-window position, secondary rule); runtime rows in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}.
\item Qualification: fixed $\lambda\in\{0.25,0.5,0.75\}$, uniform teleportation, transition-level mixing, one chain; the rule and the values of $\lambda$ were fixed in configuration before the run (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-c1c4]{Claims C1--C5}

\begin{itemize}
\item C1: runtime evidence as in H3/RQ3; specific to EndorseRank, since C-PR costs at least as much as AWP (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}).
\item C2: intended-construct checks (allowance for EndorseRank, transfer for AWP) in Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}.
\item C3: construct-specific winners as in H2/RQ2, tested in Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}.
\item C4: efficiency--alignment trade-off combining C1, C3 and the spender holdout (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} and~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}), bounded by the degree baselines; robustness in Tables~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}--\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size} and Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity}.
\item C5: bounded negative result on coupling, as in RQ5.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-nonclaims]{Explicit non-claims}

This research does \emph{not} claim:

\begin{itemize}
\item that AWP is obsolete;
\item that EndorseRank's allowance alignment exceeds its own transfer alignment;
\item that EndorseRank or AWP predicts the future growth of its own construct better than the raw t1 degree;
\item that no combination of the two edge types can beat the single-layer methods; only that the tested per-node convex mixture with fixed $\lambda$ does not;
\item that runtime scales favorably as the edge population grows beyond the cohort graph;
\item that results generalize beyond Arbitrum One and the study window without replication;
\item that on-chain scores replace collateral or legal identity systems.
\end{itemize}

\item Optional archived extended-baseline matrices.
\end{enumerate}
