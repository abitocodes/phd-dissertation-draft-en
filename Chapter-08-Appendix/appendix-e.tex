\section{Claim-to-Evidence Map}
\label{ch:appendix-claims}

This appendix maps each hypothesis, research question and empirical claim to the relevant evidence in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}, and lists what the evidence does not show. The evidence is that of the revised analysis, whose amounts in US dollars on five tokens were set after the registered results were known (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}); the registered results are kept in Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h1]{H1 / RQ1: Rank divergence}

RQ1 asks whether EndorseRank and AWP order the same contracts differently.

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: on the matched cohort, EndorseRank and AWP rank contracts differently.
\item Evidence: Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}; Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}, whose last row gives the inter-method $\tau_b$ with its interval, also with the contracts outside each graph kept as isolated nodes; Figures~\ref{fig:rank-scatter} and~\ref{fig:score-distributions}.
\item Interpretation: the rankings are not equivalent. A large share of the pairs that both scores order are ordered oppositely, and the rule for contracts outside a graph does not change their agreement. The O1 criterion is met (Table~\ref{tab:objectives-achievement}), as it was in the registered analysis (Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h2]{H2 / RQ2: Same-window alignment}

RQ2 asks how closely each score agrees with the allowance checks and with the transfer checks of the same window.

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: in the same window, each score agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on, EndorseRank with the allowance family and AWP with the transfer family, and the C-PR mixtures lie between their two layers on every family. With amounts in US dollars, AWP follows the summed value of a contract's inbound transfers almost as closely as their number of senders, the in-degree.
\item Evidence: the family means of every score with their intervals (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}); the transfer proxies one by one (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer}); the paired contrasts of C-PR and S-PR (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}); the same-window coefficients with the contracts outside a graph kept as isolated nodes (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).
\item Note: this is a check of the intended construct and is partly built in, since the proxies come from the same events as the graphs. EndorseRank leaves few ties among the cohort, and the rule for contracts outside a graph changes no coefficient. Only the same-window part of rule~A, which concerns C-PR (Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results}), was fixed in the plan, and here it is applied to scores defined after the registered results were known, so none of these comparisons was fixed before its data. The paired differences between EndorseRank and AWP and their per-proxy results are in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp}.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h3]{H3 / RQ3: Computational cost}

RQ3 asks what each score costs to compute on the same set of contracts.

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: on the same contracts, the time of a solver call follows the edge count of its graph times its iterations, so EndorseRank, on the sparser graph, takes the least time, while C-PR, on the larger union of both layers, and S-PR, which adds a solve of the allowance layer for its restarts, take longer than AWP.
\item Evidence: Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} (runtime, SD, memory, iterations, $|V|$, $|E|$); Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling}; every timed run in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runs}.
\item Note: all scores run the same solver, so the speed is a property of $|E|$ and not an advantage of an algorithm (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}). The scaling stages are subsamples of the cohort graph on the five tokens, so the claim is confined to graphs up to its size.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-rq4]{RQ4: Temporal holdout}

RQ4 asks whether scores frozen at $t_1$ rank the later arrivals of spender contracts, and whether any PageRank ranks them better than the raw degree it smooths.

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: with scores frozen at the start of each label window ($t_1$ for W0, $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ for W1), each score ranks best the later arrivals of its own edge type on the spender cohort. Against the raw degree at the freeze the two walks differ: AWP ranks later new transfer senders above the transfer in-degree in both windows, while EndorseRank, which keeps the uniform restarts fixed in the plan, ranks later new approval pairs below the in-approve degree in both windows. The registered analysis gave these contrasts the same signs (Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}).
\item Evidence: Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} and~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}); the window W1, Table~\ref{tab:fresh-posthoc}; the spenders that had received a transfer before the freeze, Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}; EndorseRank with activity restarts, Table~\ref{tab:endorserank-restarts}.
\item Qualification: both labels are the constructs as observed in a later period, counts of new approval pairs and new senders on the five tokens, not credit or trading outcomes. In the registered analysis the two contrasts of rule~A were fixed in the plan and those of rule~B, with F4--F6, in the W1 registration, each before its data; on the revised scores, defined after the registered results were known (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}), none was. The degree contrasts of EndorseRank and AWP, the increment of C-PR ($\lambda=1$) over the in-approve degree, the contrasts of S-PR and the differences between EndorseRank and AWP (Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp}) were computed after the labels were known. EndorseRank with AWP's activity restarts ranks new approval pairs above the in-approve degree in both windows, but that comparison too was made after the labels were known, so it is a hypothesis for a registered test and not a finding.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-rq5]{RQ5: Coupled operator}

RQ5 asks whether C-PR improves on a walk over the transfers alone, and whether it ranks contracts better than a walk over either layer alone.

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: on the revised scores, one walk over both edge types does not beat its layers walked alone. Rule~A fails: C-PR is no worse than the allowance walk on new approval pairs but worse than the transfer walk on new senders, and its same-window part fails as well. Rule~B is not met: C-PR improves on the transfer walk on new approval pairs (F1), so the gain seen in W0 recurs in W1, but it fails the margin on new senders (F2) and, narrowly, on the liquidation-free close rate of the traders (F3), where the two point estimates are equal but the lower end of the interval lies below $-\delta$. With AWP as the comparator, the first sensitivity analysis, F2 fails clearly. In W0 contrasts computed after the labels were known, S-PR stays level with the transfer walk on new senders and below the allowance walk on new approval pairs: allowance edges help only when they carry the walk. The registered analysis reached the same verdicts (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}).
\item Evidence, rule~A: Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} for the primary part, and Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} and~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} for the secondary part (Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results}).
\item Evidence, rule~B: Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}, with F1--F3, F4--F6 reported beside them and both sensitivity analyses (Section~\ref{sub:fresh-results}); the exploratory contrast of W0 in Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}; the registered verdicts beside the revised ones in Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}.
\item Evidence, cost: the rows of C-PR and S-PR in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}.
\item Qualification: tested with fixed $\lambda\in\{0.25,0.5,0.75\}$, layers in US dollars on five tokens with each allowance capped at $U$, uniform teleportation, transition-level mixing and one chain. Rule~A and the values of $\lambda$ were fixed in the plan and configuration committed in \texttt{43d8045} on 9~October~2026, before any log was extracted (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}). Rule~B, its margin and its two sensitivity analyses were written after the results of W0 were known and registered in \texttt{0d3a057} before scan~B (Section~\ref{sub:fresh-holdout}). The US-dollar layers and the cap were set on 10~October~2026, after the registered results were known (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}), so the verdicts above apply these rules to the revised scores as a re-analysis and are not registered tests.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-c1c4]{Claims C1--C5}

Chapter~\ref{ch:results} states Claims C1--C6. The first five rest on the evidence below; the evidence for C6 follows in Appendix~\ref{sec:map-c6}.

\begin{itemize}
\item C1: the runtime evidence listed under H3/RQ3, with the edge ratio and the time ratio in Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-results} and Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}. The speed belongs to the sparser graph, not to the algorithm, and C-PR and S-PR cost more than AWP.
\item C2: the same-window agreement of every score with the proxies of its own edges (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}), a check of the intended construct that is partly built in, with AWP following the US-dollar value of inbound transfers almost as closely as their number of senders (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer}); the ties and the rule for contracts outside a graph (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}). The damping and value-slope checks, which recompute the same-window family means of EndorseRank and AWP, are in Tables~\ref{tab:robustness-damping} and~\ref{tab:robustness-slope} and Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity}; the sample-definition stages (Table~\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size}) show how the statistic moves with the evaluation set and are not robustness evidence.
\item C3: the rank divergence listed under H1/RQ1 (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}, Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}).
\item C4: the comparison of each walk with the raw degree of its own edge type out of window (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders}, \ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:fresh-posthoc}), with the coverage check (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}) and the variant with activity restarts (Table~\ref{tab:endorserank-restarts}).
\item C5: on the revised scores coupling does not beat its two layers walked alone, a bounded negative result, and rule~B for the comparison with the transfer walk alone, applied to these scores, is not met (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}); the registered analysis reached the same verdicts (Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}); see RQ5 above and Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results}.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-nonclaims]{Explicit non-claims}

This research does \emph{not} claim:

\begin{itemize}
\item that any of the scores is the better measure of reputation in general, or that AWP has become obsolete (the comparisons between EndorseRank and AWP in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp} follow the constructs and depend on coverage);
\item that the agreement of a score with proxies built from its own edges is evidence from outside the score (it checks the intended construct and is partly built in);
\item that EndorseRank with the uniform restarts of the plan ranks later new approval pairs better than the raw in-approve degree (it ranks them lower in both windows);
\item that EndorseRank with activity restarts beats the in-approve degree (that comparison was made after the labels were known and awaits a registered test);
\item that multi-hop propagation, which is present in the allowance graph, made EndorseRank beat the raw count (Section~\ref{sub:theory-closed-form});
\item that allowance edges lead transfer edges on liquidation avoidance, at the count level (R1) or at the PageRank level (R2), or that EndorseRank beats AWP there (neither rule is met on the revised scores or in the registered analysis);
\item that any score measures creditworthiness, default risk or trading skill (in W1 the approval count ranks traders below the transfer count on the profitable and non-loss shares, and the data contain no default label);
\item that receiving approvals makes a contract trader avoid liquidation (the samples are small, the recipients close more positions, and the transfer count also ranks this label);
\item that the value slopes $b=\ln 3/m$, the cap $U$ of the allowance layer of C-PR or EndorseRank's uniform restart is the best choice (the slopes and the cap were set in the revision after the registered results were known, and the value-slope check of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-slope} varies the slopes but not the cap; the uniform restart was fixed in the plan, and the comparison with activity restarts was made after the labels were known);
\item that the verdicts of rules~A and~B and of R1--R3 on the revised scores are registered tests (the rules were fixed for the registered analysis and are applied to scores defined after its results were known; the registered verdicts are in Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered});
\item that C-PR improves on a walk over the transfers alone (rule~B fails on new senders and on the traders) or on AWP (it falls behind AWP on new senders in both windows);
\item that no combination of the two edge types can beat the single-layer methods, only that the per-node convex mixture tested here, with fixed $\lambda$, does not;
\item that EndorseRank's shorter solve time is an advantage of its algorithm (it follows from the smaller edge count), or anything about runtime on graphs larger than the ego network of the cohort;
\item that EndorseRank resists Sybil farms (reaching an ordinary or above-median score is cheap, and an approval of one of the five tokens needs no balance, so the token list does not raise the cost of a farm; Section~\ref{sec:sybil-cost-model});
\item that the results generalize beyond contracts approved by at least three owners on WETH, USDC, USDT0, ARB or WBTC on Arbitrum One in the months studied, or to the approvals and transfers of other tokens, which the revised analysis leaves out (Table~\ref{tab:usd-tokens});
\item that on-chain scores substitute for collateral, audits or identity systems, or that they draw on off-chain information, which this study does not use.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-c6]{C6: Outcomes built from neither edge type}

C6 compares the two edge types on a trader outcome that neither graph contains.

\begin{itemize}
\item Statement: on the liquidation-free close rate of GMX~V2 contract traders, with the rules registered with W1 applied to the revised scores, the count-level lead of the approval count over the transfer count (R1) is not supported in W1, the window on which the rules decide, although P is positive in W0; the PageRank lead (R2) is not supported either; and R3 applies. In the wording fixed at registration: there is no count-level lead of allowance over transfer on later liquidation avoidance, the PageRank versions are not resolved, and the signal concerns forced-liquidation avoidance, not trading success. These verdicts are a re-analysis, not registered tests, and the registered analysis reached the same ones (Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}).
\item Evidence: Tables~\ref{tab:neutral-label-contrasts} and~\ref{tab:neutral-label-recipients} (Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}); all six trader labels in both windows, Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-grid}; the protocol, Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-protocol}; the registration, section~3 of \texttt{docs/registration\_w1.md}, committed in \texttt{0d3a057} before scan~B; its application to the revised scores, \texttt{docs/revision\_price\_weighting.md} (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}).
\item Qualification: the trader cohorts are small, $70$ contracts in W1 and $88$ in W0, because contract accounts traded little on GMX~V2 in 2026 (Table~\ref{tab:gmx-quarters}). Every trader that had received an approval of one of the five tokens avoided liquidation in both windows, against about three in four of the others, but the recipients closed more positions, and the transfer count also ranks this label. All traders are contracts, so account type is held fixed. Any use in screening would first need a registered test on a larger sample (Section~\ref{sec:future-work}).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-registered]{The registered analysis}

The analysis plan and the registration of W1 fixed amounts in raw token base units, every ERC-20 token, and the value transform with $b=1$. The amounts were revised after the results of that analysis were known (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}). The chapters report the revised analysis, and the registered analysis is kept here as it was obtained. Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised} sets its decision contrasts and verdicts beside the same contrasts on the revised scores; its last four rows, the degree contrasts of both windows, were computed after the labels were known in both analyses and carry no verdict. Every verdict in the registered column is the outcome of a test fixed before its data; no verdict in the revised column is, because the rules were applied to scores defined after the registered results were known. The complete registered results, with every coefficient, interval and verdict, are published as they were computed in \texttt{data/3-published-results-for-thesis/} (\texttt{results\_digest.md}); the revised results are in its subfolder \texttt{revised-usd/}.
