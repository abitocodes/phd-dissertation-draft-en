Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript ``Coupling Allowance and Transfer Edges in One PageRank Walk for Ranking Smart Contracts on an Ethereum Rollup'' for consideration as a Research Article in \emph{IEEE Access}.

Wallets, block explorers and integrators need to know which of a rollup's more than a million smart contracts users rely on. Rankings of ledger addresses are usually computed from token transfers, although the same addresses are also linked by token allowances, by which an owner lets a spender draw its tokens. The manuscript treats the two relations as layers of one network of contracts and tests, out of window, whether a single PageRank walk that mixes them ranks smart contracts on the Arbitrum One rollup better than a walk over either layer alone. Two decision rules were committed to the authors' Git repository before this study extracted the logs that judge them.

Neither rule was met, and the manuscript reports them as not met. Its main analysis was defined after the registered results were known: graphs whose nodes are contracts only, amounts in US dollars on five tokens, scores from the twelve months before each freeze, and each allowance counted up to its owner's balance. It is reported as a re-analysis under the same rules, beside the registered one. In both analyses the coupled walk ranked later approvals better than the transfer walk but ranked later senders worse by more than the registered margin; on the contract graph it also ranked later approvals below the allowance walk.

The work matters to an international readership. Graph-based ranking is often proposed for ordering the contracts of a chain; the paper shows on public data that on contract graphs the coupled walk beat neither of its layers, that plain counts of approving and of sending contracts did better than the coupled walk, and that about 27\% of the contracts to be ranked have no edge to another contract. \emph{IEEE Access} reviews for technical soundness, which suits a study whose central findings are rules that were not met.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. It is derived from the first author's doctoral research at the University of South Africa. A related manuscript by the same authors is being submitted at the same time to \emph{PLOS ONE}. That paper reports a crawl-ordering test of whether walks reach the contracts users rely on sooner than counting, and sets the single-layer walks against the counts they smooth; the present paper concerns the coupling operator, its two pre-specified rules, its weight sweep and its seeded ablation. The two papers share the data and cite each other. Both report the spender holdout coefficients of the raw counts, EndorseRank and AWP with their count contrasts, the closed form of EndorseRank and the solver runtimes, attributed to the companion here. The crawl-ordering test appears only in the companion; the present paper quotes its verdict on the coupled walk. We upload the companion manuscript as confidential material for the reviewers. The study uses only public blockchain and price data and involves no human participants. The code, pre-specification documents, result summaries and the manuscript's scripts are public at \href{https://github.com/abitocodes/ERC20-Allowance-PageRank-Wallet-Reputation}{\nolinkurl{github.com/abitocodes/ERC20-Allowance-PageRank-Wallet-Reputation}} and \href{https://github.com/abitocodes/phd-dissertation-draft-en}{\nolinkurl{github.com/abitocodes/phd-dissertation-draft-en}}. The research received no external funding. The authors declare no competing interests; all have approved the submission.
