\chapter{Implementation and Empirical Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter runs the design of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} on Arbitrum One as a single case study: one chain and one score window per freeze, one matched cohort of contracts for every same-window comparison, and spender cohorts for the two label windows W0 and W1. It reports the contract-graph analysis, the main analysis of the thesis (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}): the data (Section~\ref{sec:dataset}); the cost, agreement, divergence and robustness of the two single-layer walks (Sections~\ref{sec:benchmark-results} to~\ref{sec:robustness-results}); the discovery-order test (Section~\ref{sec:discovery-results}); the out-of-window prediction of the single-layer walks against the counts they smooth (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}); and C-PR, the method of the thesis, under its rules and beside every other score (Section~\ref{sec:cpr-results}). Rules~A and~B were fixed in the analysis plan and in the registration of W1 for the raw-unit analysis; here they are applied to the scores of the contract graph as a re-analysis, and the registered analysis gave the same readings (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}, Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}). Each Kendall $\tau$ comes with its confidence interval except in the value-slope and sample-definition checks, and each walk is read against the raw count it smooths.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:dataset]{Case study setting: Arbitrum One}

Arbitrum One is a heavily used Ethereum rollup with mature DeFi protocols (Section~\ref{sec:ethereum-l2}), and its logs are in the public BigQuery export that the pipeline reads. The score windows run from 1~July~2025 to 30~June~2026 for the same-window evaluation and W1 and from 1~April~2025 to 31~March~2026 for W0, and the time decay is anchored at the freeze (Figure~\ref{fig:logistic-decay-methods}). After the registered results were known, the revised analysis was defined: amounts in US dollars on five tokens; scores from the twelve months before each freeze; graphs whose nodes are contracts other than smart accounts, with edges read from the whole chain; each allowance counted up to its owner's balance at the freeze, with balances reconstructed from \texttt{Transfer} logs; constants set on the contract graph; and the discovery-order test. That analysis is the one reported here, so no result of this chapter is a registered result.

From 1~April~2025 to 30~September~2026 the five tokens (WETH, USDC, USDT0, ARB and WBTC) have 1,240,275,042 \texttt{Approval} and non-zero \texttt{Transfer} rows, which name 25,537,280 addresses other than the zero address. The 17,477,779 that sent a transaction are user accounts. The addresses that could still end an edge between contracts, 6,230,270 with the cohort members and two audit samples, were read with \texttt{eth\_getCode} at block $513{,}216{,}506$; those without code, EIP-7702 accounts, Safes and ERC-4337 accounts are user accounts too, which leaves 1,829,719 contracts (Section~\ref{sub:account-types}; Table~\ref{tab:contract-types} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-contract-graph}). Only the 603,475,784 rows with a contract at both ends can make an edge: no user account is a node, and no score or baseline reads an edge of one.

The contract graph numbers 1,403,959 contracts over both freezes and has two layers (Table~\ref{tab:graph-layers}): transfer pairs join a sender contract to a recipient contract, and allowance pairs an owner contract to a spender contract whose latest allowance of the window is positive and stays positive under the balance cap (Section~\ref{sec:graph-construction}). The cap decides the size of that layer. At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, 223,892 of the 339,282 positive latest allowances between contracts are unlimited (66.0\%), 222,559 are capped to zero because the owner holds none of the token (65.6\%), and 116,723 stay positive; at $t_1$, 289,299 of 308,922 are capped to zero (93.6\%) and 19,623 stay positive (Table~\ref{tab:allowance-cap} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-contract-graph}). For 1,493 of 1,500 sampled (token, owner) pairs (99.5\%) the reconstructed balance (Section~\ref{sub:balances}) equals \texttt{balanceOf} at the last block of 30~June~2026.

\input{results/tables/graph-layers.tex}

The allowance layer is far smaller than the transfer layer: 115,006 pairs against 2,580,709 at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (4.5\%) and 17,940 against 2,269,073 at $t_1$. At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ it is also concentrated: one owner contract, which this study did not identify, holds 98,073 of the 115,006 pairs, whereas the largest owner at $t_1$ holds 256. The allowance-side results at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, those of the same window and of W1, are read with this in mind, and W0 gives the same comparisons on a layer without such an owner. The union graph, the distinct pairs of different contracts with an edge in either layer, has 2,575,632 pairs over 1,299,438 contracts at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and 2,269,421 over 1,111,554 at $t_1$. The constants, set on the W0 score window of the contract graph, are those of Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}: $m_T=160$ and $m_A=0.071$ dollars, $U=16{,}000$ dollars for C-PR's allowance layer, and $U_{\mathrm{user}}=31{,}000$ dollars for the importance measures of Section~\ref{sec:discovery-protocol}.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:cohorts]{Cohorts}

The rules of the three contract sets below count every approving account, user accounts included, so membership does not depend on the contract graph; Table~\ref{tab:cohort-coverage} shows where the members lie in it.

\begin{itemize}
\item Matched cohort ($n=6{,}443$). The rule of Section~\ref{sec:data-sources}, a non-zero \texttt{Approval} of one of the five tokens from at least three distinct owners in the score window of $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, lists 6,483 addresses with code; 40 of them are smart accounts and so user accounts, and the cohort is the other 6,443. Every same-window alignment, difference test and benchmark is computed on it. The same rule in the W0 score window lists 6,343 addresses, of which $6{,}292$ are contracts; the spenders and labels of each window are drawn from the events around the addresses listed for its freeze.
\item Spender cohorts of W0 and W1 ($n=7{,}864$ and $7{,}915$). In W0 the scores are frozen at $t_1$ (31~March~2026, 23:59:59~UTC), and the labels are counted for every spender that holds, in the events around the addresses listed for W0, a positive latest allowance of one of the five tokens at $t_1$, set inside the W0 score window. The cohort is the 7,864 contracts among these 7,962 spenders; 97 are user accounts and 1 is a system address. In W1 the scores are frozen at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, and the same rule gives 7,915 contracts among 7,995 spenders. A spender needs only one owner, so these cohorts are not subsets of the matched cohort.
\item Subsamples of the matched cohort ($n=1{,}000$, $2{,}000$ and $5{,}000$). Deterministic SHA256 subsamples (seed \texttt{contract-benchmark-v1}), used only for runtime against size and for the sensitivity to sample definition (Section~\ref{sub:expanded-pool}). Each stage keeps the pairs incident to its sampled contracts.
\end{itemize}

\input{results/tables/cohort-coverage.tex}

The contract graph does not reach every member. Of the matched cohort, 1,250 contracts (19.4\%) end a capped allowance pair, 4,059 (63.0\%) a transfer pair and 4,270 (66.3\%) a pair of the union graph; the other 2,173 have no transfer with another contract in the window and no allowance to or from one that stays positive under the cap. Of the W0 spenders 5,700 (72.5\%) and of the W1 spenders 5,759 (72.8\%) lie in the union graph. A member outside a graph stays in its cohort and scores zero under every score and baseline of that graph, so the scores tie on these members. Every coefficient of this chapter therefore reflects both how a score orders the members that the graph reaches and how it sets them apart from the others; Table~\ref{tab:union-members} repeats the main out-of-window contrasts inside the union graph.

Contracts that one actor deploys can send transfers or grant allowances only among themselves, and a walk keeps the mass that reaches a group with no edge out of it \parencite{langville2006pagerank}; link farms raise PageRank in this way \parencite{gyongyi2005alliances}. A check made after every result was known, which decides no rule, therefore looked for such closed groups in the graph of each walk: sets of two or more contracts that are strongly connected and have no edge to a contract outside the set. The allowance layer has none at either freeze. The transfer layer has 6,860 at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, with 31,563 contracts (2.4\% of its contracts) and 4.9\% of AWP's stationary mass, and 492 at $t_1$, with 2,846 contracts and 0.3\% of that mass; in the graph of C-PR they hold 6.0\% and 0.7\% of its mass. They contain 72 of the 6,443 contracts of the matched cohort and 82 of the 6,292 of the W0 cohort, and at most one of the hundredth that any walk ranks highest in a cohort or spender set. Such groups therefore do not shape the rankings of the contracts studied here; the check does not show who controls a contract.

The solver graphs of EndorseRank and AWP in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} do not hold the 9 allowance pairs and 328 transfer pairs whose value weight is zero in floating point, nor the 8 and 75 contracts that end no other pair; the layers of C-PR, whose weights are sums of amounts, hold them. Table~\ref{tab:usd-tokens} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-tokens} gives the five tokens with their allowances and price checks.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:implementation]{Implementation}

Every algorithm runs on one Python implementation of PageRank ($d=0.85$, $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$, $I_{\max}=300$); the methods differ in their edge lists and restart vectors. The tables of this chapter come from the summary files of the evaluation runs, the benchmark and the discovery-order test, as collected in the results digest. At $d=0.85$ every solve of the same-window and out-of-window evaluations converged well inside the iteration cap. Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} lists the runs, the repository revision, the configuration file and the summary files.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:benchmark-results]{Computational benchmark (Claim~C6)}

Claim~C6 concerns what each score costs to compute on the same input. Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} reports the cost of each algorithm on the contract graph at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, on which the matched cohort is scored, following the timing procedure of Section~\ref{sec:computational-benchmark}. Each graph and setting was timed once, so the same measurement reappears as the last row of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} and as the $d=0.85$ row of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}; Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runs} in Appendix~\ref{sec:benchmark-run-logs} lists every timed run.

\input{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}

EndorseRank solves the allowance graph ($114{,}997$ edges) in $0.101$\,s and AWP the transfer graph ($2{,}580{,}381$ edges) in $2.82$\,s, so AWP takes $27.9$ times as long, at an edge ratio of $22.4$. The cost of an iteration grows with $|E|$ and with $|V|$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}). Per iteration, assembly included, AWP's solve takes $15.9$ times as long as EndorseRank's ($0.029$ against $0.0018$\,s), between the node ratio of $11.9$ and the edge ratio, and AWP needs $1.75$ times as many iterations ($98$ against $56$); together the two factors give the time ratio (for other $d$, Section~\ref{sub:damping}). Both scores use the same solver, so the difference in speed comes from the size of each graph and from the iterations its walk needs there, which the benchmark does not separate; it is no advantage of the EndorseRank algorithm.

C-PR walks on the union of both layers ($2{,}591{,}253$ distinct pairs, the pairs of a contract with itself included). Its solver keeps one matrix per layer and multiplies both in every iteration; it converges in $92$ iterations and takes $3.08$\,s, $1.09$ times as long as AWP. S-PR takes $2.66$\,s and $153$ iterations, the solve of the allowance layer that supplies its restart vector included. Every algorithm that walks the transfer layer, alone or with allowance edges, pays for its size.

The time covers only the solver call on edge lists built in advance, not the reading of events, the typing of accounts, the balances or the edge lists, which all algorithms share wholly or in part.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:same-window-alignment]{Same-window alignment (Claim~C5)}

Claim~C5 asks what each score agrees with in the window it is computed from. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} reports the family-mean Kendall $\tau$ of every score on the transfer, allowance and stability families; the proxies are computed from the events between contracts (Section~\ref{sec:proxy-formulas}). Apart from the secondary part of rule~A, fixed in the analysis plan (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}) and applied here as a re-analysis, the comparisons below belong to no rule.

\input{results/tables/alignment-hybrid.tex}

The scores divide by the edges they walk on. The two that walk on transfers alone, AWP and C-PR ($\lambda=0$), reach $0.755$ ($[0.748,0.762]$) and $0.804$ ($[0.799,0.809]$) on the transfer family and $0.227$ ($[0.206,0.245]$) and $0.238$ ($[0.218,0.258]$) on the allowance family. The two that walk on allowances alone, EndorseRank and C-PR ($\lambda=1$), reach $0.839$ ($[0.828,0.849]$) and $0.838$ ($[0.827,0.850]$) on the allowance family and $0.297$ ($[0.280,0.317]$) and $0.298$ ($[0.281,0.318]$) on the transfer family; S-PR and the interior mixtures of C-PR lie between the two layers. On the stability family the scores that walk on transfers lead, AWP with $0.643$ ($[0.635,0.651]$), while EndorseRank stays at $0.220$ ($[0.206,0.237]$). Part of this agreement is built in: the main proxies of each family come from the same events as one of the layers, in-degree and in-value from the transfers and in-approve degree and value from the capped latest allowances, and a PageRank smooths the in-degree of its own graph. Agreement with the proxies of a score's own edge type is therefore an intended-construct check, not an external test.

On the allowance side the reach of the layer builds in a second part. EndorseRank sets only the $926$ cohort contracts with an in-edge in its graph above the rest: it gives $317$ others, which are in the graph only as owners, the score of a node without in-edges, and the remaining $5{,}200$ zero (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}). The in-approve degree and value of a contract that receives no capped allowance are zero. Most of the pairs that EndorseRank and these proxies order alike therefore set a contract that the layer reaches as a spender against one that it does not, and the coefficient of $0.839$ says mainly that score and count agree on that boundary.

Appendix~\ref{sec:proxy-family-detail} breaks the family means of EndorseRank and AWP into their proxies. AWP follows the transfer in-degree ($0.764$, $[0.757,0.772]$) and the in-value ($0.746$, $[0.738,0.754]$) about equally, and EndorseRank the in-approve degree ($0.847$, $[0.835,0.857]$) and the in-approve value ($0.831$, $[0.820,0.841]$). C-PR ($\lambda=1$) walks the allowance layer with capped amounts, also restarts uniformly, and stays within $0.002$ of EndorseRank on every family.

The first half of Claim~C5 is supported in a narrow sense: every score that walks on one edge type agrees most with the proxies of that edge type, the mixtures of C-PR lie between their two layers on every family, and the agreement is partly built in, by the shared events and by the reach of each layer. Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results} locates C-PR within the window.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:rank-divergence]{Rank divergence (H1 / RQ1)}

Hypothesis~H1 and RQ1 concern whether EndorseRank and AWP put the same contracts in different orders. The inter-method Kendall $\tau_b$ between the two score vectors is $0.275$, with interval $[0.258,0.294]$ (last row of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}), so the interval excludes both zero and one. Of the $20{,}752{,}903$ contract pairs, only $32.3\%$ are ordered by both scores, and $26.7\%$ of those pairs are discordant.

Ties explain the first of these shares (Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}). EndorseRank gives $801$ distinct scores and ties $65.4\%$ of the contract pairs; AWP gives $3{,}982$ and ties $13.7\%$. Under each score the largest tie block is formed by the contracts that score zero, $5{,}200$ under EndorseRank and $2{,}384$ under AWP: the $5{,}193$ and $2{,}384$ cohort contracts outside each layer and, under EndorseRank, $7$ whose only pairs carry amounts too small for the value weight to differ from zero. Under EndorseRank $317$ further contracts share the score $c=8.45\times10^{-6}$ of a node without in-edges. In Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}, among the contracts that EndorseRank ranks above $c$, the points spread over most of AWP's range.

The zero blocks come from one rule of this pipeline: a contract missing from a graph scores zero (Section~\ref{sec:graph-construction}). The analysis plan fixed the alternative, keeping the missing contracts as isolated nodes, as a sensitivity analysis, and Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} gives it. Under uniform restarts an isolated node scores like any node without in-edges, so the $5{,}193$ contracts outside the allowance layer tie with the $317$. EndorseRank's allowance coefficient then rises from $0.839$ to $0.956$ ($[0.952,0.960]$), because the score now ties the contracts that the allowance proxies tie at zero, and its transfer coefficient falls from $0.297$ to $0.259$ ($[0.239,0.278]$). The agreement between the two scores falls from $0.275$ to $0.227$ ($[0.207,0.245]$): the zero rule sets the $317$ above the $5{,}193$, and AWP on balance orders those pairs the same way. AWP restarts only at contracts that sent a transfer, so an isolated contract keeps the score zero and AWP's coefficients are the same under both rules.

H1 is supported: the two scores order the cohort differently, but the support is narrower than the coefficient suggests. Two thirds of the pairs are not ordered by both scores, because the allowance layer between contracts reaches a fifth of the cohort, and the rule for missing contracts, which leaves AWP's coefficients as they are, moves EndorseRank's. This divergence, the second half of Claim~C5, is also the premise of the coupled walk (Section~\ref{sec:cpr-results}).

\dissertationSubheading[sec:robustness-results]{Robustness and sensitivity checks}

Three checks ask whether the results of EndorseRank and AWP hinge on one damping value, one slope of the value weight or one sample definition; the third varies the sample itself, so its coefficients are expected to move, and it is not evidence of robustness. Two more test EndorseRank's restart rule and the balance cap.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:damping]{Damping}

Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping} recomputes the family means at $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$ on the same graphs.

\input{results/tables/robustness-damping.tex}

For EndorseRank no family mean moves at the third decimal across the three values. For AWP the transfer coefficient rises from $0.750$ ($[0.742,0.757]$) to $0.761$ ($[0.754,0.768]$) as $d$ rises, and its other two family means move by at most $0.004$. Neither score's pattern is an artifact of the conventional $d=0.85$. EndorseRank's stability is largely structural. Its two tie blocks do not depend on $d$, and for a spender whose owners receive no approvals EndorseRank equals $c\,(1+d\,\delta(v))$, with $\delta(v)$ the owner-normalized in-strength (Section~\ref{sub:theory-closed-form}), so the order among such spenders does not depend on $d$ either. At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the formula holds within 1\% for all $651$ such spenders among the $926$ cohort contracts with in-edges, and for $33.8\%$ of the other $275$. Runtime grows with the iterations, which rise with $d$ from $32$ to $176$ for EndorseRank and from $57$ to $294$ for AWP, the last just inside the cap of $300$; the time ratio is $20.5$, $27.9$ and $34.5$ at an edge ratio of $22.4$. Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity} plots the family means.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:endorserank-restarts]{Restarts of EndorseRank}

EndorseRank restarts the walk uniformly, while AWP restarts it at contracts in proportion to their recent sending activity (Section~\ref{sub:endorserank-graph}). The analysis plan fixed uniform restarts for EndorseRank and named the variant as a score to report beside it, without fixing any comparison between the two. Table~\ref{tab:endorserank-restarts} scores both versions, with AWP beside them.

\input{results/tables/endorserank-restarts.tex}

With AWP's rule the restart mass goes to the owner contracts in proportion to their approving activity. In the same window EndorseRank's allowance coefficient then falls from $0.839$ to $0.799$ ($[0.782,0.813]$), and its transfer coefficient hardly moves ($0.299$, $[0.281,0.318]$). Out of window the variant changes little. On new approval pairs it is slightly below the uniform version in W0 ($-0.004$, $[-0.008,-0.001]$) and level with it in W1 ($+0.001$, $[-0.002,0.005]$); on new senders it is slightly above it in both windows ($+0.005$, $[0.002,0.009]$, and $+0.008$, $[0.004,0.012]$). The W1 contrasts are in Table~\ref{tab:fresh-posthoc}; all were computed after the labels were known. Neither version ranks new approval pairs above the in-approve degree (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}), and Section~\ref{sub:cpr-contrasts} sets the variant beside C-PR.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:uncapped-results]{Balance-cap sensitivity}

Table~\ref{tab:uncapped-sensitivity} repeats the out-of-window comparisons on an allowance layer without the cap, in which every positive latest allowance between contracts is an edge at its own value (Section~\ref{sub:uncapped-protocol}); that layer has $275{,}917$ pairs at $t_1$ and $302{,}559$ at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$. It decides no rule.

\input{results/tables/uncapped-sensitivity.tex}

Without the cap the allowance side has lower coefficients on later approvals and higher ones on later inflow. EndorseRank ranks new approval pairs at $0.088$ ($[0.067,0.107]$) in W0 and $0.078$ ($[0.058,0.099]$) in W1, about half its coefficients with the cap, and new senders at $0.185$ ($[0.166,0.202]$) and $0.177$ ($[0.156,0.197]$), more than twice those with the cap; the in-approve degree moves likewise. \dissertationSubheading[sec:discovery-results]{Discovery order (RQ2)}

RQ2 asks whether a crawl ordered by C-PR on the contracts read so far reaches the contracts with the most user-account demand sooner than a breadth-first crawl and than a crawl ordered by the number of contract backlinks. The discovery-order test (Section~\ref{sec:discovery-protocol}) repeats the crawl-ordering experiment of \textcite{cho1998crawling} on the contract graph. It was defined after the registered results were known and decides no rule of the analysis plan or the registration; its own decision rule is read in one run, at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ in the primary reveal mode. In that mode, \texttt{both}, a visit reveals a contract's in-edges and out-edges, and the universe $\mathcal{U}$ is the weakly connected component that holds the giant strongly connected component, $1{,}169{,}044$ contracts. The protocol's rule made it primary because the other universe, the giant strongly connected component with the contracts reachable from it ($\mathrm{SCC}\cup\mathrm{OUT}$), holds fewer than half as many contracts in the structure pilot at $t_1$ (Table~\ref{tab:discovery-sizes} in Appendix~\ref{sec:discovery-detail}). The pilot also raised the protocol's stop flag on a thin allowance layer (Section~\ref{sec:discovery-protocol}); what the allowance layer adds is read from the contrasts outside the decision. Importance is the demand of user accounts, which no ordering reads: the dollars they sent to a contract in the window ($I_T$) and the balance-capped dollars they had authorized it to spend at the freeze ($I_A$). The universe holds $96.5\%$ of $I_T$ and $93.0\%$ of $I_A$ (Table~\ref{tab:discovery-coverage}) and $16{,}776$ of the capped allowance pairs, so the pairs of the largest owner lie outside it. Its hot sets hold $13{,}141$ contracts by $I_T$ and $11{,}662$ by $I_A$; each ordering crawls from $20$ seeds, and $\mathrm{AUC}_{10\%}$ measures how early it visits the hot contracts.

Table~\ref{tab:discovery-decision} gives the two decision contrasts. $D_1$, C-PR$'$ minus the breadth-first crawl, is $+0.0520$ ($[+0.0485,+0.0558]$) on $I_T$ and $+0.0061$ ($[+0.0014,+0.0111]$) on $I_A$, both intervals above zero. $D_2$, C-PR$'$ minus the crawl ordered by contract backlinks, is $-0.0051$ ($[-0.0087,-0.0010]$) on $I_T$ and $-0.0571$ ($[-0.0621,-0.0519]$) on $I_A$, both intervals below zero. The verdict is V2 on both importances, with the sentence that an interval below zero adds, and the statement of the test reads: ``Ordered by C-PR on the contracts read so far, a crawl from a random contract of the giant strongly connected component reached the contracts of the universe with the most user-account inflow and those with the most user-authorised value sooner than a breadth-first crawl, but not sooner than a crawl ordered by the number of contract backlinks. A crawl ordered by the number of contract backlinks reached them sooner.'' The answer to RQ2 is therefore no, and the lead over the breadth-first crawl is small on $I_A$.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:holdout-results]{Temporal holdout of the single-layer walks (RQ3)}

The same-window check cannot separate a score from its own construct, and the discovery-order test reads importance in the score window; the out-of-window evaluation tests whether a score frozen at a cut-off predicts what happens after it. Following Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}, scores on the W0 spender cohort are frozen at $t_1$, and two labels are counted for each contract between 1~April and 30~June~2026: new owner--spender approval pairs and new transfer senders. Table~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} sets every score against both labels, with four raw counts as baselines: the in-approve degree and value, the transfer in-degree and the union in-degree. Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} gives the paired contrasts: the two of rule~A, fixed in the analysis plan, and those computed after the labels were known. This section reads each walk against the count it smooths; rule~A and the other rows of C-PR and S-PR are taken up in Section~\ref{sec:cpr-results}.

Two facts frame every out-of-window number. The labels count every account: $90.4\%$ of the $748{,}258$ new approval pairs of W0 come from user accounts, and $86.3\%$ of its $651{,}474$ new senders are user accounts (in W1 $87.9\%$ and $82.3\%$; Table~\ref{tab:label-split} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-contract-graph}), and no score reads an edge of a user account. And $27.5\%$ of the W0 spenders and $27.2\%$ of the W1 spenders lie outside the union graph and score zero under every score and count (Table~\ref{tab:cohort-coverage}). Both labels are also sparse: of the 7,864 spenders of W0, 1,725 gained a new approval pair and 1,416 a new sender. Each label is one kind of event carried into the future, so these results show whether a score predicts more of the activity it summarizes and not whether a contract is safe.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:cpr-results]{The coupled walk C-PR (RQ4, RQ5)}

C-PR, the method of this thesis, walks both US-dollar layers and mixes their transition rows at each node with weight $\lambda$ (Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}). The analysis plan fixed one decision rule, and its object is C-PR: rule~A asks the coupled walk to beat each of its layers walked alone (RQ4). The registration of W1 added rule~B, which asks it to improve on its transfer layer on new data (RQ5). Both rules compare C-PR with its own layers, C-PR ($\lambda=1$) and C-PR ($\lambda=0$), because only that contrast changes the coupling and nothing else; C-PR also differs from EndorseRank and AWP in its amount weights and its uniform restarts. Both are applied to the scores of the contract graph as a re-analysis. The contrasts of C-PR with every other score and count, at the end of the section, decide no rule.

Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} gives the paired contrasts in this window of C-PR and its ablation S-PR, which keeps the transfer layer but restarts from the allowance walk (Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}): first the eight of Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests} against the two layers walked alone, then six against EndorseRank and AWP, two of them computed afterwards from the saved scores. RQ4 and RQ5 are decided out of window; this window only locates the hybrids.

C-PR lies between its two layers on every family and stays nearer to its transfer layer. At the primary setting it reaches $0.738$ ($[0.729,0.747]$) on the transfer family, below C-PR ($\lambda=0$) by $\Delta\tau=-0.066$ ($[-0.074,-0.058]$), and $0.433$ ($[0.419,0.445]$) on the allowance family, below C-PR ($\lambda=1$) by $\Delta\tau=-0.406$ ($[-0.418,-0.393]$). The gap on the allowance family is in part one of reach. C-PR gives a positive score to the $4{,}270$ cohort contracts in the union graph, more than four times the $926$ that the allowance layer reaches as spenders, and so orders by their transfers more than three thousand contracts that the allowance proxies tie at zero, which $\tau_b$ counts against it. AWP ranks the transfer family better than C-PR ($-0.017$, $[-0.028,-0.006]$), and EndorseRank the allowance family ($-0.406$, $[-0.419,-0.393]$). On the family that each single-layer walk follows least, C-PR leads that walk: it ranks the transfer family $0.441$ above EndorseRank ($[0.423,0.457]$) and the allowance family $0.206$ above AWP ($[0.188,0.224]$). A node with out-edges in only one layer follows that layer whatever $\lambda$ is (Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition}); $\lambda$ acts only at the nodes with out-edges in both layers, and at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ only $9{,}459$ contracts are owners in the allowance layer.

On every family S-PR falls below the layer that leads that family. Restarting the transfer walk from the allowance walk lowers the transfer coefficient to $0.782$ ($[0.773,0.791]$; $\Delta\tau=-0.022$, $[-0.030,-0.013]$, against C-PR ($\lambda=0$)) and raises the allowance coefficient only as far as $0.371$ ($[0.350,0.391]$; $\Delta\tau=-0.467$, $[-0.488,-0.446]$, against C-PR ($\lambda=1$)). A restart vector changes where the walk lands after each teleport, and it carries only part of the allowance signal into the transfer walk.

As fixed in the analysis plan, the secondary part of rule~A asks C-PR's point estimate to lie within the 95\% interval of the better layer on the transfer and allowance families, and C-PR to exceed both layers on the stability family. At $\lambda=0.5$, C-PR fails all three parts, as in the registered analysis (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}). Its transfer coefficient ($0.738$) lies below the interval $[0.799,0.809]$ of C-PR ($\lambda=0$), its allowance coefficient ($0.433$) below the interval $[0.827,0.850]$ of C-PR ($\lambda=1$), and on the stability family it exceeds the allowance layer ($\Delta\tau=+0.352$, $[0.340,0.365]$) and not the transfer layer ($-0.063$, $[-0.071,-0.053]$). The paired contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}, reported alongside the rule as a stricter check, point the same way: the mixture gives up part of each layer's agreement with its own family and exceeds the better layer on no family.

Rule~A is not met out of window either. The rule asked C-PR to do no worse than C-PR ($\lambda=1$) on new approval pairs and to beat C-PR ($\lambda=0$) on new transfer senders in W0 (first block of Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}). Against its transfer layer walked alone C-PR ranks new approval pairs higher, $0.142$ ($[0.124,0.162]$) against $0.070$ ($[0.052,0.091]$), a paired difference of $+0.071$ ($[0.059,0.084]$); this contrast, computed after the labels were known, is the one that the registration of W1 made its criterion F1. Among the spenders that another contract had paid before the freeze its lead on approvals shrinks to $+0.026$ ($[0.013,0.041]$) in W0 and is not resolved in W1 ($+0.007$, $[-0.008,0.021]$), while AWP ranks new senders higher by more ($-0.084$, $[-0.097,-0.069]$, and $-0.094$, $[-0.108,-0.079]$) (lower block of Table~\ref{tab:cpr-contrasts}; this restriction, too, was chosen after the results were known). On that subset C-PR ranks new approval pairs above the in-approve degree ($+0.051$, $[0.025,0.076]$, and $+0.053$, $[0.025,0.080]$).

Against the counts, C-PR does not rank either label above the count of the same edge type. On new approval pairs it lies below the in-approve degree in both windows ($-0.062$, $[-0.082,-0.037]$, and $-0.077$, $[-0.097,-0.057]$) and below the in-approve value; on new senders it lies below the transfer in-degree ($-0.064$, $[-0.075,-0.054]$, and $-0.066$, $[-0.075,-0.057]$). It lies above each of these counts on the other label. Against the union in-degree, the count of the edges that C-PR walks, the result is split (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}), with the same directions and larger gaps inside the union graph: $+0.035$ ($[0.022,0.049]$) and $+0.027$ ($[0.014,0.041]$) on new approval pairs, $-0.089$ ($[-0.103,-0.075]$) and $-0.093$ ($[-0.107,-0.077]$) on new senders. Against its ablation S-PR it ranks new approval pairs higher ($+0.051$, $[0.038,0.064]$, and $+0.049$, $[0.036,0.063]$); on new senders it is slightly lower in W0 ($-0.011$, $[-0.021,-0.0004]$, the upper end just below zero) and level in W1 ($-0.006$, $[-0.015,0.002]$). C-PR thus stands between the two sides on each label, and no score or count of this study has the highest coefficient on both: the in-approve degree has it on new approval pairs and AWP on new senders.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:empirical-claims]{Claims (C1--C6)}

The chapter's results support the claims below, cross-referenced to the hypothesis and research question columns of Table~\ref{tab:hrq-claim-map}. C1 rests on the discovery-order test, which was defined after the registered results were known and decides no rule of the analysis plan or the registration. The verdicts of rules~A and~B in C3 are a re-analysis on the scores of the contract graph; the registered analysis gave the same readings (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}). C2 and C4 rest on contrasts outside every rule. The direct comparisons between EndorseRank and AWP are in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp} and carry no claim of the thesis.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[C1.] In the run that decides, at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ in the primary mode, a crawl ordered by C-PR on the contracts read so far reached the contracts with the most user-account demand sooner than a breadth-first crawl ($D_1=+0.0520$, $[+0.0485,+0.0558]$, on $I_T$ and $+0.0061$, $[+0.0014,+0.0111]$, on $I_A$) but not sooner than a crawl ordered by the number of contract backlinks, which was sooner ($D_2=-0.0051$, $[-0.0087,-0.0010]$, and $-0.0571$, $[-0.0621,-0.0519]$). The verdict is V2 on both importances, so the answer to RQ2 is no (Table~\ref{tab:discovery-decision}). AWP$'$ has the highest $\mathrm{AUC}_{10\%}$ of the crawls in that run, apart from the diagnostic $\delta'$, every crawl stays far from the ideal order, and the allowance layer adds a small, resolved amount to C-PR$'$ over its transfer layer (Tables~\ref{tab:discovery-auc} and~\ref{tab:discovery-contrasts}). The lead over breadth-first order on $I_A$ does not hold in every sensitivity. In the \texttt{out} mode, a sensitivity without a verdict, all four contrasts lie above zero at both freezes, so the result depends on what a visit reveals.
\item[C2.] Out of window EndorseRank does not beat the count it smooths, and AWP beats its count by a small margin that does not hold among the contracts with inflow. EndorseRank ranks new approval pairs below the in-approve degree in both windows (W0 $-0.036$, $[-0.046,-0.027]$; W1 $-0.034$, $[-0.043,-0.025]$), and so does its variant with activity restarts. AWP ranks new transfer senders slightly above the transfer in-degree on the whole cohorts (W0 $+0.012$, $[0.003,0.023]$; W1 $+0.011$, $[0.001,0.022]$) and below it among the W0 spenders that another contract had paid ($-0.035$, $[-0.049,-0.022]$) (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}, \ref{tab:fresh-posthoc} and~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}). Against the union in-degree, the count of the edges it walks, C-PR ranks new approval pairs higher and new senders lower in both windows (Table~\ref{tab:cpr-contrasts}). The labels count mostly user accounts, which no score reads, and about $27\%$ of each spender cohort lies outside the union graph and scores zero.
\item[C3.] Under its rules, C-PR, a fixed-$\lambda$ mixture of two dollar-weighted layers with uniform restarts, does not beat its two layers walked alone. Rule~A is not met: in W0 C-PR lies below its allowance layer on new approval pairs ($-0.025$, $[-0.045,-0.004]$) and below its transfer layer on new senders ($-0.014$, $[-0.019,-0.008]$), and its same-window part fails as well (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}). Rule~B is not met by its two applied criteria (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}): F1 holds ($+0.074$, $[0.060,0.087]$), so the gain over the transfer layer on new approval pairs recurs in W1, and F2 fails ($-0.016$, $[-0.022,-0.012]$, against the margin of $-0.01$). Inside the union graph and with the uncapped allowance layer the contrast with the allowance layer reads differently and the contrast with the transfer layer does not (Tables~\ref{tab:union-members} and~\ref{tab:uncapped-sensitivity}); neither decides a rule.
\item[C4.] Beside the rules, C-PR stands between the two sides on each label in both windows (Table~\ref{tab:cpr-contrasts}). Against EndorseRank it ranks new approval pairs lower and new senders higher (in W0 $-0.026$, $[-0.046,-0.004]$, and $+0.170$, $[0.148,0.191]$); against AWP it ranks new approval pairs higher and new senders lower (in W0 $+0.044$, $[0.029,0.058]$, and $-0.077$, $[-0.087,-0.068]$). It ranks new approval pairs above S-PR and below the in-approve degree and value, and new senders below the transfer in-degree.
\item[C5.] In the same window each single-layer walk agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on, EndorseRank with the allowance family ($0.839$, $[0.828,0.849]$) and AWP with the transfer family ($0.755$, $[0.748,0.762]$), and the mixtures of C-PR lie between their two layers (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}). These are intended-construct checks, and part of the agreement is built in. The two walks order the cohort differently: their agreement is $\tau_b=0.275$ ($[0.258,0.294]$), only $32.3\%$ of the contract pairs are ordered by both scores, because $5{,}200$ of the $6{,}443$ cohort contracts score zero under EndorseRank, and $26.7\%$ of the pairs that both order are discordant. With the contracts outside a graph kept as isolated nodes EndorseRank's allowance coefficient rises to $0.956$ ($[0.952,0.960]$), and AWP's coefficients do not change (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}, Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}).
\item[C6.] The cost of each score's solver call grows with the size of the layers it walks and with the iterations its walk needs. The allowance graph has $4.5\%$ of the transfer graph's edges, and AWP takes $27.9$ times as long as EndorseRank at an edge ratio of $22.4$ (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}). On the subsamples the time ratio is $0.99$ to $1.36$ times the edge ratio, and the rows do not test edge counts beyond those of the contract graph (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling}). The low cost belongs to the smaller graph and is no advantage of the algorithm: C-PR walks both layers and takes $1.09$ times as long as AWP.
\end{enumerate}
