\chapter{Implementation and Empirical Results}
\label{ch:results}

Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} described how EndorseRank and the Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) baseline are built, which construct checks are applied, how uncertainty is quantified and how runtime is measured. This chapter runs that design on Arbitrum One and reports what was observed. It is a single case study. There is one chain and one observation window, with one matched cohort for every same-window comparison, a spender cohort and a trader cohort for the temporal holdouts, and one expanded address pool for node-count sensitivity. A registered replication on labels from June to August~2026 closes Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}. Every table is generated from the evaluation summary of Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}, and each Kendall $\tau$ coefficient is reported with its confidence interval.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:dataset]{Case study setting: Arbitrum One}

Arbitrum One was chosen as the case because it is a heavily used Ethereum rollup with mature DeFi protocols (Section~\ref{sec:ethereum-l2}), because its logs are included in the public BigQuery export that the pipeline reads, and because one of its margin-trading protocols, GMX~V2, supplies the wallet seed set described below. The observation window covers 1~December~2025 to 31~May~2026. Six months is long enough to span several market regimes and to let the tenure and active-month proxies vary. It is also short enough that an approval made early in the window, which EndorseRank weights as much as a recent one, is at most six months old.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:cohorts]{Cohorts}

Three address sets are used, each with its own purpose.

\begin{itemize}
\item Matched cohort ($n=5{,}521$). These are the GMX~V2 margin-trading wallets on Arbitrum One with at least three closes (\texttt{PositionDecrease} events, partial closes and liquidations included) between 1~December~2025 and 31~May~2026. The list is fixed from GMX data before any approval or transfer is extracted, and the BigQuery approval and transfer queries are filtered to it. Each method's evaluation graph, $G_{\text{approve}}$ for the latest allowances and $G_{\text{transfer}}$ for the time-decayed transfers over the window, keeps every edge with at least one endpoint in the cohort. Membership does not depend on either graph. No allowance edge touches $966$ cohort wallets, no transfer edge touches $381$, and $370$ are touched by neither; a wallet that no edge of a graph touches is absent from it and scores zero under that method. Every result in the same-window alignment, difference-test and benchmark sections is computed on this cohort.
\item Holdout spender cohort ($n=1{,}335$). It contains every spender that holds at least one positive latest allowance at $t_1$, the end of the scoring period (28~February~2026, 23:59:59~UTC), in the extracted approval data. Scores for these wallets are tested against owner--spender approval pairs and transfer senders first seen in the holdout period, 1~March~2026 to 31~May~2026 (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}).
\item Expanded wallet pool ($N=99{,}080$). It combines the matched cohort with $93{,}559$ further addresses sampled from the chain's monthly ERC-20 activity. The pool is used for two analyses only, the node-count sensitivity of runtime and the sensitivity to sample definition (Section~\ref{sub:expanded-pool}). The added addresses bring no edges beyond those extracted for the cohort, so the full pool's solver graph is the cohort graph, with the same nodes and edges; the relevant results section restates this caveat.
\end{itemize}

The matched-cohort graph sizes are listed with the benchmark figures in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}. On the same wallets, the endorsement graph has roughly one-ninth as many edges as the transfer graph. Both graphs are drawn from the same wallets and the same window, so the imbalance lies in what each graph records; it is not a sign of an error in construction. EndorseRank keeps only the latest non-zero allowance for each token, owner and spender, in raw token base units, and sums these over tokens, so repeated approvals collapse into one directed edge per owner--spender pair; AWP instead sums every transfer between two wallets into a single time-decayed weight. Whatever differences in score and alignment separate the methods trace back to ranking logic and graph semantics, since the sampling is the same for both.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:implementation]{Implementation}

Every algorithm runs on one Python implementation of PageRank, with $d=0.85$, tolerance $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ and an iteration cap of $I_{\max}=300$. The single-layer methods differ only in their edge lists. The coupled operator mixes the two edge lists node by node, as in Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition} (Section~\ref{sec:implementation-notes}). Each algorithm run writes its scores, per-proxy statistics, bootstrap confidence intervals and timings to one file. The tables of this chapter come from the runs of 14~September~2026, and Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} lists their repository revision, configuration file and summary files.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:benchmark-results]{Computational benchmark (Claim~C1)}

Claim~C1 is that EndorseRank costs less to compute than AWP on the same input. Following the timing procedure of Section~\ref{sec:computational-benchmark}, Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} reports for each method, on the matched cohort, the PageRank solve time and its standard deviation over the five timed repeats, the peak memory of the warm-up solve, the number of iterations to convergence and the size of the evaluation subgraph.

\input{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}

Most of the timed call is spent before the first iteration. The call indexes the nodes, assembles the sparse weighted adjacency matrix from the edge list in a per-edge Python loop and row-normalizes it, and only then runs power iteration; the edge lists themselves are built before the timer starts. A separate profiling run of the same call on the same edge lists attributes about 94\% of the wall time to matrix assembly ($0.39$ of $0.41$\,s for EndorseRank, $3.67$ of $3.91$\,s for AWP). The power iterations take under 2\%: about $6$\,ms for EndorseRank's $37$ iterations and $70$\,ms for AWP's $100$. Assembly and each iteration are both linear in $|E|$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}), so the measured ratio of $10.2\times$ tracks the edge ratio of $9.3\times$. EndorseRank does converge in about one-third as many iterations, since the transfer operator, with its long-tailed decaying weights, mixes more slowly. That difference is real, but it adds little to the wall time. Peak memory likewise grows with $|E|$, because the adjacency, weight and residual buffers are all proportional to the length of the edge list. Judging by the standard deviations, run-to-run noise is about an order of magnitude smaller than the gap between the methods.

The two hybrid rows show what the speed result covers and where it stops. C-PR walks on the union of both edge sets ($151{,}814$ edges, $31{,}242$ nodes) and needs $4.76$\,s, slightly longer than AWP. It converges in fewer iterations ($76$ vs.\ $100$), but it has more edges to assemble, and assembly dominates the call. S-PR runs on the transfer layer and takes $4.16$\,s. Both its time and its iteration count include the EndorseRank solve that supplies its restart vector: the $145$ iterations are $37$ for that solve plus $108$ for the seeded transfer walk. Claim~C1 is thus a statement about EndorseRank alone: any algorithm that queries the transfer layer, on its own or combined with allowance information, pays the cost of that query.

Two caveats attach to this result. The baseline time covers only the solver call, matrix assembly included, on edge lists built in advance. A full end-to-end refresh would add parsing, decoding and edge-list construction, steps that all algorithms share wholly or in part, and C1 leaves them out for that reason. A faster method is also not necessarily a better one. The runtime result has to be read against the alignment results of Sections~\ref{sec:three-method-alignment}--\ref{sec:proxy-family-detail}, and against the holdout results that follow.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:three-method-alignment]{Same-window alignment: EndorseRank vs.\ AWP (Claims~C2--C3)}

Claim~C2 asks whether each method recovers its own construct on same-window local statistics. Claim~C3 asks whether the winner is construct-specific, that is, whether the better of the two methods changes from one family to another. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci} reports the family-mean Kendall $\tau$ for the transfer, allowance and Sybil-stability families, each with a 95\% paired-bootstrap confidence interval, together with the inter-method agreement between the two score vectors. The same family means are drawn as a heatmap in Figure~\ref{fig:alignment-heatmap}.

The two patterns cross. AWP tracks the transfer family most closely and is slightly negative on the allowance family, while EndorseRank tracks the allowance family most closely and the transfer family moderately. Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff} tests whether each contrast is significant beyond sampling variation, using the five pre-specified single-layer paired differences of Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests}. The hybrid contrasts are reported in Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} (Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results}).

\input{results/tables/tau-diff.tex}

In three of the five contrasts the interval excludes zero. AWP's transfer alignment is higher than EndorseRank's, EndorseRank's allowance alignment is higher than AWP's, and AWP leads EndorseRank on the Sybil-stability family. The remaining two are null: EndorseRank's allowance alignment is not statistically higher than its own transfer alignment (contrast~i), and its in-approve-degree alignment is not statistically higher than its in-degree alignment (contrast~v). These two nulls cannot be read as evidence either way. EndorseRank ties most of the cohort (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}), and the ties cap $\tau_b$ at different heights for different proxies: about $0.38$ for the allowance proxies, which EndorseRank nearly reaches, and about $0.57$ for a proxy without ties. Coefficients with different ceilings are not on one scale. When the wallets outside the allowance graph are kept as isolated nodes instead of being scored zero, the ceilings move and both contrasts come out large and positive (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}). Since EndorseRank is a global smoothing of spender in-approve degree, its agreement with the allowance proxies is an intended-construct check and cannot count as an external test. On this cohort the check rests on the 131 wallets that receive approvals, 122 of which EndorseRank lifts above its common value. AWP places those 122 near the middle of its ranking, so transfer centrality says little about which of the cohort's wallets are approved as spenders. EndorseRank's transfer coefficient ($0.333$, interval $[0.314,0.353]$) is below AWP's ($0.534$) and clear of zero, but most of the pairs behind it set a wallet at EndorseRank's common value against a zero-score wallet (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}), and with those wallets kept as isolated nodes it turns negative.

Claim~C2 is supported insofar as each method reaches non-trivial alignment on its own construct, with an interval well clear of zero. The two between-method contrasts (ii and iii) support Claim~C3. Their intervals exclude zero in opposite directions, so neither method dominates both checks.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:proxy-family-detail]{Proxy family detail}

Each family mean condenses several per-proxy coefficients. This section reports those coefficients one proxy at a time, as Spearman $\rho$, Kendall $\tau$ and the 95\% interval of $\tau$. Before correlation, every proxy is oriented so that a higher value means higher expected reputation, and the transfer proxies are the ones AWP's source paper used to validate it\cend{3}.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:transfer-family]{Transfer family}

\input{results/tables/alignment-transfer.tex}

In Table~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer}, AWP's agreement with in-degree ($\tau=0.617$) is the largest per-proxy Kendall $\tau$ on the matched cohort. Its agreement with in-value is lower, though still far above EndorseRank's. Both are primary-construct checks for AWP, which ranks on the time-decayed transfer graph. The informative observation is that AWP does not collapse to in-degree; logistic decay and value weighting shift a measurable share of pairwise orderings. EndorseRank's transfer coefficients are moderate, and their intervals overlap neither zero nor AWP's intervals. They mainly reflect that wallets which made an approval in the window also receive more transfers than wallets which made none (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}). A protocol that equates reputation with inbound flow can read that flow from the in-degree itself; AWP adds propagation and value weights, and out of window it ranks new senders below the in-degree (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}).

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:allowance-family]{Allowance family}

\input{results/tables/alignment-allowance.tex}

In Table~\ref{tab:alignment-allowance}, EndorseRank's coefficients on in-approve degree and in-approve value are almost equal. Both proxies are positive for the same 131 wallets, and EndorseRank lifts 122 of them above its common value. The other nine received only finite approvals, each from an owner who had also approved some spender without limit, so in raw base units their share of the owner's row is too small to change their score (Section~\ref{sub:threat-construct}). The two coefficients therefore mostly measure the same split and sit close to the ceiling that the ties allow. AWP's coefficients are slightly negative, with intervals that exclude zero: among the cohort's wallets, transfer centrality does not pick out those that are approved as spenders. The comparison rests on these 131 wallets and is partly built in, since EndorseRank is computed from the same approvals. The same construct is therefore tested out of window on the spender holdout of Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}, where every wallet has received approvals.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:sybil-family]{Sybil-adjusted stability family}

\input{results/tables/alignment-sybil-stability.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-sybil-stability} shows AWP ahead of EndorseRank on tenure and active months, as one would expect from time-decayed transfer edges that reward sustained activity; the paired difference on the family mean excludes zero (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}, contrast~iv). On the inbound-counterparty ratio both methods are weak, AWP negatively so. The family is reported as a robustness note on how much longitudinal transfer information each method carries, and not as a headline claim.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:rank-divergence]{Rank divergence (H1 / RQ1)}

Hypothesis~H1 and RQ1 concern whether EndorseRank and AWP put the same wallets in different orders. The inter-method Kendall $\tau_b$ between the two score vectors is $0.316$, with interval $[0.298,0.336]$ (last row of Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}), so the interval excludes both zero and one. The two rankings are related, yet far from interchangeable. Agreement runs above chance mainly because the wallets that EndorseRank scores zero also sit near the bottom of AWP (see below). Because $67.5\%$ of wallet pairs tie on EndorseRank, only $32\%$ of all pairs are ordered by both scores, and $22.3\%$ of those pairs are discordant.

The marginal score distributions are in Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}, and the EndorseRank distribution is dominated by one tie block. EndorseRank takes only $66$ distinct values on the cohort: $4{,}433$ wallets share the score $9.63\times10^{-5}$ that a wallet receiving no allowance gets from teleportation and redistributed dangling mass; $966$ score zero because no allowance edge touches them; only $122$ lie above the common value. Ties are rare under AWP, at $0.5\%$ of wallet pairs. A marginal distribution cannot show how two scores order the same wallets, so Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter} plots the two rankings against each other, with tied wallets at their mid-rank as in $\tau_b$. The EndorseRank tie blocks therefore appear as vertical columns. The $122$ wallets that EndorseRank separates sit mostly in the middle of the AWP ranking (median AWP rank $2{,}952$, interquartile range $2{,}818$--$3{,}797$), so its leaders are for the most part mid-table wallets under AWP. The column of $4{,}433$ tied wallets spans almost the whole AWP range (interquartile range $1{,}146$--$3{,}694$). The $966$ zero-score wallets sit mostly near the bottom of AWP (median AWP rank $4{,}850$), and $370$ of them score zero under AWP as well.

The tie blocks come from two rules, and one of them is a choice of this pipeline. The 4,433 wallets at the common value are in the allowance graph as owners that receive no approval, so PageRank gives them teleportation and dangling mass and nothing else. The 966 zero-score wallets are not in the graph at all, and the pipeline scores a missing wallet zero (Section~\ref{sec:graph-construction}); kept as isolated nodes, they would have received the common value too. This rule decides most of EndorseRank's ordering of the cohort. Of the 4.95 million wallet pairs that EndorseRank does not tie, 4.28 million (86.5\%) set a wallet at the common value against a zero-score wallet. On the matched cohort, EndorseRank therefore mostly separates wallets that made an approval in the window from wallets that made none. The ties also cap $\tau_b$. Against the allowance proxies, which are positive for 131 wallets, the largest $\tau_b$ that EndorseRank can reach with these ties is about $0.38$; against a proxy without ties the ceiling is about $0.57$.

Nothing in the graph favors the zero rule over the isolated-node alternative, so we reran the same-window alignment with the missing wallets kept as isolated nodes. This check was run after the results above were known. Under uniform teleportation an isolated node scores like any node without in-edges, so the 966 wallets join the block of 4,433 and every other score is multiplied by one common factor; recomputing PageRank with the isolated nodes added confirms both points. AWP's 381 missing wallets were handled the same way. Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} gives the result. No AWP coefficient moves by more than $0.001$. EndorseRank's allowance coefficient rises to $0.956$ (interval $[0.928,0.976]$), since EndorseRank and the allowance proxies now split the cohort at almost the same place. Its transfer coefficient falls to $-0.071$ ($[-0.086,-0.057]$), and its Sybil-stability and trading-success coefficients to $-0.033$ and $-0.019$. The agreement between the two scores falls from $0.316$ to $-0.025$ ($[-0.038,-0.012]$). Contrasts~i and~v, null under the zero rule, become $+1.027$ and $+1.054$, with intervals far above zero. EndorseRank's positive same-window coefficients on transfer, stability and trading, and its agreement with AWP, therefore measure which traders made an approval in the window.

Construct-specific alignment has a structural counterpart in rank divergence. Had the two methods ordered wallets almost identically, the family-level winners in Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci} might be noise around one shared ranking. The divergence actually observed, read together with the opposite-signed between-method contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}, supports treating the two as distinct instruments, related only through the wallets that EndorseRank scores zero. H1 is supported. On this cohort, much of the difference comes from EndorseRank's ties, so the claim that the two scores measure different things rests mainly on the holdout (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}). In operational terms, a protocol now relying on transfer-based scores cannot assume that its wallet ordering will survive a switch to allowance-based scores, or a blend with them. The largest disagreements are expected among transfer hubs rarely endorsed as spenders and among endorsed spenders with small transfer footprints.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:robustness-results]{Robustness and sensitivity checks}

The results above would be of limited use if they hinged on one damping value, one token mix or one sample definition. Three checks address this. The first two are robustness checks in the strict sense, holding the sample fixed while a parameter or the token universe is perturbed. The third is a sensitivity analysis: it varies the sample definition itself, and there we expect the coefficients to move.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:damping]{Damping}

\input{results/tables/robustness-damping.tex}

For EndorseRank the family means do not move at the third decimal place across $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$, and for AWP they shift by a few thousandths at most. The crossing of the patterns is no artifact of the conventional $d=0.85$. EndorseRank's stability is largely structural. Only $122$ cohort wallets lie above EndorseRank's common value (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}); a change in $d$ moves the common value but leaves the $4{,}433$ wallets that share it tied, so the rank correlations can hardly move. Runtime, by contrast, varies across the sweep. EndorseRank takes $0.594$, $0.401$ and $0.427$\,s at $d=0.75$, $0.85$ and $0.95$, and AWP $4.420$, $4.105$ and $5.420$\,s, so the AWP/EndorseRank ratio runs from $7.4\times$ to $12.7\times$. Each graph--damping pair was timed once, in one session. EndorseRank's variation is about twelve times its run-to-run standard deviation of $0.016$\,s (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}) and is not monotone in $d$, so it cannot be read as noise of that size. Matrix assembly takes most of each call and does not depend on $d$, and even at the cap of $300$ iterations the power iterations would take only about $0.2$\,s of AWP's call. Slower mixing therefore explains little of either method's variation, which most likely reflects timing conditions that shifted during the session; we do not interpret it. Relative runtime thus changes across the sweep while the alignment results do not. Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity} plots the family means.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:sample-size]{Sample-definition sensitivity}

\input{results/tables/robustness-sample-size.tex}

In Table~\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size} the transfer and allowance family means are recomputed on the staged pool subsamples from the runtime scaling. The transfer-family means come out much larger than on the matched cohort ($0.82$--$0.92$), and AWP's allowance coefficient turns positive ($0.23$--$0.24$), while EndorseRank's allowance coefficient ($0.327$--$0.343$) ends slightly below its cohort value of $0.355$. None of these shifts is evidence about the methods. The pool subsamples let in wallets with no edges in one graph or in both; after alignment such a wallet has a zero score and a zero proxy, and tied zeros at the bottom of both rankings push Kendall $\tau$ up mechanically (Section~\ref{sub:sample-size-protocol}). We therefore report the table as a sensitivity analysis of the sample definition. The question it answers is narrower, namely whether the relative ordering of the two methods within each family survives a change of population. At every stage it does, with AWP ahead on transfer and EndorseRank ahead on allowance. The absolute values, though, must not be compared with those of Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}. At every pool stage the EndorseRank graph has far fewer nodes than the sample has wallets (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling}), so most pool wallets score zero on it, against $966$ of the $5{,}521$ cohort wallets.

For AWP, the damping and top-token checks show that Claims~C2 and~C3 do not hinge on the parameter or the token mix; for EndorseRank they could hardly have moved, because its ties hold whatever $d$ or token set is used. The runtime ratio behind Claim~C1 is less steady, moving between $7.4\times$ and $12.7\times$ across the damping sweep. When the population changes, as in the sample-definition check, the comparative structure survives but the absolute values do not.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:coupled-results]{Coupled operator: same-window results (RQ5)}

Two hybrids were defined in Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}. The coupled operator C-PR mixes the allowance and transfer layers in each wallet's transition row with weight $\lambda$, and the endorsement-seeded walk S-PR keeps the transfer layer but restarts from the EndorseRank distribution. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} sets both beside the two single-layer methods on the three same-window families of Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}, while Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} tests the eight hybrid contrasts of Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests} against whichever single-layer method leads each family. RQ5 is decided by the out-of-window test in Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}. Here we only locate the hybrids on the checks that the single-layer methods were built to pass.

C-PR behaves like AWP with a small allowance-layer correction. Its transfer-family coefficient falls from $0.531$ at $\lambda=0.25$ to $0.519$ at $\lambda=0.75$, always below AWP's $0.534$ and within $0.015$ of it. At the primary setting the paired difference to AWP is $-0.007$ (interval $[-0.009,-0.005]$), small but clear of zero. On the allowance family C-PR cannot be told apart from zero at any $\lambda$ and lies far below EndorseRank's $0.355$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.356$, interval $[-0.388,-0.323]$). The cause is the sparsity of the allowance layer. The transfer layer has about nine times the allowance layer's edges, and $30{,}791$ nodes against $6{,}442$ (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}), so most nodes of the union graph have no allowance out-edge. At those nodes the mixing rule of Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition} falls back on the transfer row whatever $\lambda$ is; $\lambda$ acts only at the minority of nodes with out-edges in both layers. Once the walk leaves that minority, it travels on transfer edges until it teleports. Raising $\lambda$ to $0.75$ shifts the allowance coefficient by less than one hundredth. The mixture recovers the allowance construct only at the end point $\lambda=1$, where it is EndorseRank; at $\lambda=0.75$ its allowance coefficient is still $0.006$.

Only on the Sybil-adjusted stability family does C-PR exceed both single-layer methods, as the secondary criterion of Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol} asked. It scores $0.290$, against $0.286$ for AWP and $0.233$ for EndorseRank, and both paired intervals lie above zero ($[0.003,0.006]$ and $[0.043,0.069]$). The gain over AWP is four thousandths, which makes it a difference in the statistical sense but not in any operational one.

On every family, S-PR does worse than the method it borrows from. Restarting the transfer walk from the EndorseRank distribution drops the transfer coefficient to $0.494$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.040$ against AWP), raises the allowance coefficient only as far as $0.116$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.239$ against EndorseRank), and pushes Sybil-adjusted stability below both single-layer methods. A restart vector changes where the walk lands after each teleport, but on a graph of this density it does not change where the walk spends its time. This is the same-window counterpart of the finding that Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results} reaches out of window.

As fixed on 14~September, the secondary criterion asks C-PR's point estimate to lie within the 95\% interval of the better single-layer method on the transfer and allowance families, and C-PR to exceed both single-layer methods on Sybil-adjusted stability. At $\lambda=0.5$, C-PR passes two of the three parts and fails the third. Its transfer coefficient ($0.527$) falls inside AWP's interval ($[0.522,0.547]$), and its Sybil-adjusted stability exceeds both single-layer methods. The allowance coefficient ($-0.001$), however, lies far outside EndorseRank's interval ($[0.322,0.384]$), so the secondary rule fails. The paired contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}, reported alongside the rule as a stricter check, would fail the transfer part as well, since they place C-PR slightly but significantly below AWP. In the same-window tables the denser layer dominates the mixture. The holdout takes up the remaining question of whether the mixture has any predictive content that the single layers lack.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:holdout-results]{Temporal holdout (RQ4, RQ5)}

A same-window check cannot separate a method from its own construct. The holdout instead tests whether centrality measured up to a cut-off predicts behavior after it. Following Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}, scores on the spender cohort are frozen at 28~February~2026, and two labels are then counted for each wallet between 1~March and 31~May~2026: new owner--spender approval pairs (the endorsement construct) and new transfer senders (the flow construct). Table~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} sets every score computed at $t_1$, the two raw-degree baselines included, against both labels. The eight paired contrasts fixed before the run are in Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}.

We take the three findings in the order of the contrasts.

The first finding is that the allowance edge beats the transfer edge on both labels, in contrasts fixed before the run. On new approval pairs EndorseRank reaches $\tau=0.479$, and its paired difference to AWP ($\Delta\tau=+0.435$, interval $[0.365,0.511]$) is the largest in the chapter; on this label AWP's own interval includes zero. Every wallet in this cohort holds at least one approval, yet EndorseRank ties 913 of the 1,335 at the score of a node without in-edges. Each of their approvals is finite and comes from an owner who also approved some other spender without limit, so in raw base units it takes less than $10^{-43}$ of the owner's row. The coefficient therefore rests on the order of the other 422 spenders and on where they stand against that block. Every label falls after all scored events, so this is out-of-window evidence that endorsement centrality carries information about future endorsement that transfer centrality does not. The flow label shows the same ordering with a smaller gap. EndorseRank ($0.372$) exceeds AWP ($0.270$) on AWP's own construct, and the interval excludes zero ($[-0.148,-0.051]$ for AWP minus EndorseRank). This thesis does not test why. One possibility is that approvals and transfers from the same counterparties tend to arrive together, so that a wallet gaining new owners also gains new senders. AWP is also a weak reference point on this label: the transfer in-degree it smooths ranks new senders better than AWP does ($0.472$ against $0.270$), and so does the spender's approval count ($0.404$).

Two features of AWP could explain this, and a check run after the results were known separates them (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-weighting}). One is its raw weights, which add base units across tokens with different decimals. Weighting each transfer once, still discounted by its age, lifts AWP on new senders to $0.314$, a gain of $0.044$ (interval $[0.025,0.065]$) that leaves it $0.157$ below the in-degree ($[-0.183,-0.132]$). The other is coverage: 318 of the 1,335 spenders have no transfer edge up to the freeze and score zero under AWP. On the 977 spenders that had received a transfer from another address, AWP reaches $0.263$ on new senders and the in-degree $0.701$. Neither feature accounts for the gap; on this label the walk itself ranks worse than the count it smooths. The same check qualifies EndorseRank's lead on new senders. On the 977 the lead holds ($+0.212$, interval $[0.156,0.260]$), but when both walks weight each event once, EndorseRank and AWP are level on new senders ($-0.028$, $[-0.062,0.002]$), while EndorseRank keeps a lead of $0.329$ ($[0.267,0.393]$) on new approval pairs. Equal weights lower EndorseRank on both labels, to $0.398$ and $0.287$, so its raw weights do not explain why the in-approve degree beats it either.

\input{results/tables/holdout-weighting.tex}

The second finding answers the second part of RQ4 in the negative: neither PageRank improves on its own raw degree at $t_1$. In-approve degree at $t_1$ predicts new approval pairs with $\tau=0.711$, against EndorseRank's $\tau=0.479$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.233$, interval $[-0.291,-0.183]$), and transfer in-degree at $t_1$ predicts new transfer senders with $\tau=0.472$, against AWP's $\tau=0.270$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.201$, interval $[-0.230,-0.171]$). Both intervals lie well below zero. Within a three-month window, a wallet gains more new counterparties the more it already has, and the local count captures this directly. PageRank replaces the count with a weighted, propagated quantity into which owner-side centrality and allowance value also enter, and on this label those extra terms act as noise. EndorseRank's out-of-window predictive content is contained in, and diluted from, the spender's $t_1$ in-approve degree; AWP stands in the same relation to transfer in-degree. If either PageRank adds anything beyond its degree, it does not show in predicting the future growth of its own construct, and no same-window analysis in this chapter sets a PageRank against its raw degree as a competing score. The advantage over the transfer baseline in the first finding therefore belongs to the allowance edge. The in-approve degree, which uses no transfer information at all, also beats AWP on new senders ($0.404$ against $0.270$, Table~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders}).

Third, the coupled operator fails the rule of 14~September, although in an exploratory comparison it improves on AWP. That rule asked C-PR to match EndorseRank on new approval pairs and to beat AWP on new transfer senders. At $\lambda=0.5$, C-PR does worse than EndorseRank on new approval pairs ($\Delta\tau=-0.184$, interval $[-0.213,-0.149]$) and no differently from AWP on new transfer senders ($\Delta\tau=+0.007$, interval $[-0.012,0.028]$), so both conditions of its primary part fail. Against AWP alone, a comparison the rule did not include, C-PR does better. On new approval pairs C-PR reaches $0.295$ against AWP's $0.044$, a paired difference of $+0.251$ (interval $[0.203,0.306]$, on the same resamples), and on new senders it shows no detectable loss. This contrast was computed after the labels were known, so it is treated as a hypothesis and tested again in the registered window below. Across the three values of $\lambda$ the order is monotone on both labels. On new approval pairs $\tau$ rises from $0.271$ ($\lambda=0.25$) through $0.295$ to $0.314$ ($\lambda=0.75$), and on new transfer senders from $0.266$ through $0.277$ to $0.287$. More allowance weight helps on both labels; for this cohort the transfer layer adds no out-of-window signal to what the allowance layer carries. The coupled score lies between the two single-layer scores, with one exception: at $\lambda=0.25$ it is $0.004$ below AWP on new transfer senders, with overlapping intervals. The seeded ablation shows where the allowance signal has to sit. S-PR matches AWP on the flow label ($\Delta\tau=-0.006$, interval $[-0.017,0.006]$) and falls far below EndorseRank on the endorsement label ($\Delta\tau=-0.365$). When transfer edges decide where the walk goes, a restart distribution derived from endorsements changes very little. On the spender holdout, the allowance signal survives when allowance edges carry the walk, fully in EndorseRank and diluted in C-PR, and it is lost when they only decide where the walk restarts.

Two caveats apply to the whole table. Each label is the same construction carried into the future (new approvals, new senders), so none of these results bears on credit or loss. Both labels are also sparse. Of the $1{,}335$ wallets, $222$ gained a new approval pair and $175$ a new sender; every coefficient rests entirely on how that small subset is ordered.

Other candidate labels were computed on the holdout, and they are reported here without being offered as support. Revocation counts and the drain heuristic both rise with EndorseRank ($\tau=0.295$, interval $[0.229,0.355]$, and $0.461$, interval $[0.414,0.518]$), and more steeply still with the raw in-approve degree ($0.483$ and $0.575$). For an endorsement reading the sign is wrong, since a trusted spender should lose approvals less often, not more. The likelier reading is volume. A spender with many owners sees more of everything owners do, revocations and drains included, so these labels cannot separate trust from use. Aave liquidations are empty in this spender set. The same-window trading families of the research proposal (Section~\ref{sub:gf-pr}) are archived in Table~\ref{tab:alignment-summary}. Both scores align weakly with them, and AWP is ahead of EndorseRank on liquidation ($0.113$ against $0.093$) and trading success ($0.179$ against $0.096$). The inverse-risk family cannot be read: two of its three proxies, the summed loss and the worst-close loss, grow with the size of a wallet's losses, the opposite of their intended direction.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:trader-results]{Trader outcomes (exploratory)}

The exploratory trader run of Section~\ref{sub:trader-holdout} points the other way from the spender results. Among the 1,860 matched wallets with at least three closes from March to May, AWP ranks the number of profitable closes at $\tau=0.131$ (interval $[0.104,0.160]$) against $0.073$ ($[0.044,0.111]$) for EndorseRank, and the realized gain at $0.153$ ($[0.123,0.180]$) against $0.048$ ($[0.018,0.078]$). On the share of closes that were profitable neither score shows anything: $0.029$ ($[-0.006,0.059]$) for AWP and $-0.015$ ($[-0.047,0.028]$) for EndorseRank. AWP's lead is on counts and sums, which grow with how much a wallet trades, and not on how well it trades. The inverse-risk labels of the same run share the orientation problem noted above, and its Aave liquidation label covers only 174 wallets, 10 of them liquidated, which is too few to read. That is what a transfer score should do, and it is also what one expects from a spender-side score that leaves most traders tied. C-PR was not part of this run; the registered window gives it a trader label.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:empirical-claims]{Primary claims (C1--C5)}

The chapter's results support the main claims below, cross-referenced to the hypothesis and research question columns of Table~\ref{tab:hrq-claim-map}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[C1.] On identical wallet sets EndorseRank's solver call, matrix assembly included, takes a fraction of AWP's wall-clock time and memory, because its latest-allowance graph has roughly one-ninth as many edges as the transfer graph and both assembly and iteration scale with $|E|$; it also needs fewer iterations (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}). The ratio stays between $8.1\times$ and $12.2\times$ across in-cohort and pool stages whose edge counts differ by a factor of five to seven (Tables~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} and~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling-incohort}), although the pool stages do not test edge counts beyond those of the cohort graph. The claim covers EndorseRank alone. Running on both edge sets, the coupled operator costs at least as much as AWP.
\item[C2.] On same-window local statistics each method recovers the construct it targets, and the bootstrap confidence intervals exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}). These tests measure internal consistency; external validity is outside their scope.
\item[C3.] Which method performs better depends on the construct. AWP is better than EndorseRank on the transfer family and EndorseRank is better than AWP on the allowance family, with paired-difference confidence intervals that exclude zero in opposite directions (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}), and the two score vectors agree only in part (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}). Within EndorseRank, allowance alignment is not significantly higher than transfer alignment, but the two coefficients have different ceilings (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}), so this null says nothing either way. With the wallets outside the allowance graph kept as isolated nodes, the difference is large and positive (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).
\item[C4.] Out of window, the allowance edge carries information that the transfer edge lacks: on the spender holdout EndorseRank beats AWP on new approvals and on new senders alike (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}). The lead on new approvals holds under every edge weight tried; the lead on new senders disappears when both walks weight each event once (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-weighting}). The gain lies in the edge and not in propagation, because the spender's raw in-approve degree at $t_1$ predicts new approvals better than EndorseRank does, and the transfer in-degree predicts new senders better than AWP. On the matched traders, AWP is ahead on the same-window transfer and stability proxies and, in the exploratory spring run, on counts of later profitable closes.
\item[C5.] A single random walk over both edge types does not beat both single-layer methods, although in an exploratory comparison on the spring holdout it improves on AWP. On new approval pairs C-PR falls between EndorseRank and AWP at every $\lambda$ tested; on new transfer senders it does so at $\lambda=0.5$ and $0.75$, and at $\lambda=0.25$ it lies $0.004$ below AWP with overlapping intervals. The rule of 14~September is not met, and the seeded variant reproduces AWP (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} and~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}). Against AWP alone, C-PR at $\lambda=0.5$ adds $0.251$ on new approval pairs with no detectable loss on new senders. That comparison was made after the fact, and the registered replication decides it: \freshpending{F1--F3 verdicts}. Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results} reports where the hybrids stand within the window.
\end{enumerate}

The inter-method agreement and Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter} answer RQ1, the family tables and difference tests RQ2, the benchmark tables RQ3, and the holdouts RQ4 and RQ5. None of these claims retires AWP, and none promotes EndorseRank past what the intervals allow. EndorseRank is cheaper and aligns with the allowance checks, and its moderate agreement with AWP comes from the wallets it scores zero. Out of window it beats AWP on new approvals under every weighting tried and on new senders under raw amounts, yet the raw degree it smooths does better still. In an exploratory comparison, mixing the two layers kept more than half of that gain on new approvals, and the registered replication decides whether this holds.
