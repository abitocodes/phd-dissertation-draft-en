\chapter{Implementation and Empirical Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter runs the design of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} on Arbitrum One as a single case study: one chain and one score window per freeze, one matched cohort of contracts for every same-window comparison, spender and trader cohorts for the temporal holdouts W0 and W1, and subsamples of the matched cohort for runtime against size. It reports the two single-layer walks first, their cost, their agreement in the window, their divergence, robustness checks and their holdouts (Sections~\ref{sec:benchmark-results} to~\ref{sec:holdout-results}), and then the coupled walk C-PR, the method of the thesis, under its rules and beside every other score (Section~\ref{sec:cpr-results}); a comparison on outcomes built from neither edge type follows (Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}). It reports the revised analysis of Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}, with amounts in US dollars, five tokens and scores from the twelve months before each freeze; this analysis was defined after the registered results were known, so no result in this chapter is a registered result. Rules~A and~B and R1--R3 are applied to the revised scores, with their thresholds and wording, as a re-analysis; the registered analysis reached the same verdicts except on R1, which it did not meet (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}, Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}). Every table is generated from the evaluation summaries of Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}, each Kendall $\tau$ comes with its confidence interval except in the value-slope and sample-definition checks, and each PageRank is read against the raw degree it smooths.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:dataset]{Case study setting: Arbitrum One}

Arbitrum One was chosen because it is a heavily used Ethereum rollup with mature DeFi protocols (Section~\ref{sec:ethereum-l2}), because its logs are in the public BigQuery export that the pipeline reads, and because one of its margin-trading protocols, GMX~V2, records the position closes behind the trader labels. Each score window covers the twelve months before its freeze, 1~July~2025 to 30~June~2026 for the same-window evaluation and W1 and 1~April~2025 to 31~March~2026 for W0. The time decay is anchored at the freeze (Figure~\ref{fig:logistic-decay-methods}), so the most recent six months of a window carry most of the edge weight in AWP and EndorseRank.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:cohorts]{Cohorts}

Four sets of contracts are used. Every address in them holds contract code; externally owned accounts (EOAs) enter the graphs as owners, senders and recipients and are never scored (Section~\ref{sec:data-sources}).

\begin{itemize}
\item Matched cohort ($n=6{,}483$). The contracts that received a non-zero \texttt{Approval} of one of the five tokens from at least three distinct owners in the score window of $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:data-sources}). They are part of the analysis plan's cohort of 27,844 contracts, which applied the same rule to every token from 1~October~2023 and left out as EOAs 1,121 of its 29,022 candidates without code and 57 with an EIP-7702 delegation designator. Membership does not depend on either graph: no allowance edge touches $191$ cohort contracts (by $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ every allowance set in the window by them or to them was zero), no transfer edge touches $1{,}192$, and $30$ are touched by neither; a contract absent from a graph scores zero under that method. Every same-window alignment, difference test and benchmark is computed on this cohort. The W0 data are drawn around a cohort of their own, the $6{,}343$ contracts that meet the same rule in the W0 score window, $5{,}383$ of which are also in the matched cohort.
\item Spender cohorts of W0 and W1 ($n=7{,}914$ and $7{,}960$). In W0 the scores are frozen at $t_1$ (31~March~2026, 23:59:59~UTC), and the cohort is every spender that holds at least one positive latest allowance of one of the five tokens, set inside the W0 score window, at $t_1$ and holds contract code: 7,914 of 7,962 such spenders. In W1 the scores are frozen at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, and the same rule gives 7,960 contract spenders. The spender cohorts are not subsets of the matched cohort, since a spender needs only one owner with a positive allowance. The scores of these contracts are tested against owner--spender approval pairs and transfer senders first seen in the three months after each freeze (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}).
\item Trader cohorts ($n=87$ and $70$). The GMX~V2 accounts that hold contract code, are nodes of the freeze-date graph and close at least three positions in the label window: 87 in W0, 39 of which had received an approval in the score window, and 70 in W1, 37 of which had. Contract accounts closed 133,685 GMX~V2 positions from October~2023 to June~2026, but only 9,809 in the last six months of that span and 6,805 from July to September~2026 (Table~\ref{tab:gmx-quarters} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-gmx}). Every trader result therefore rests on few contracts and has wide intervals.
\item Subsamples of the matched cohort ($n=1{,}000$, $2{,}000$ and $5{,}000$). Deterministic SHA256 subsamples (seed \texttt{contract-benchmark-v1}), followed by the full cohort, used only for runtime against size and for the sensitivity to sample definition (Section~\ref{sub:expanded-pool}). Each stage keeps the extracted edges incident to its sampled contracts, so its edge count grows with $n$.
\end{itemize}

At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the allowance graph has 2,514,846 nodes and 3,808,949 edges, and the transfer graph 3,603,300 nodes and 8,426,124 edges. The solver graphs of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} leave out 864 and 1,573 addresses whose pairs all carry a value weight of zero in floating point. On the same contracts the allowance graph has 45.2\% as many edges as the transfer graph, because an owner--spender pair is an allowance edge only while its latest allowance of one of the five tokens, set in the window, is positive, whereas every pair of addresses that exchanged a non-zero transfer of these tokens in the window is a transfer edge. The sampling is the same for both, so the differences between the scores trace back to their edges and restarts.

The five tokens hold most of the extracted rows: of the ego-network events of the plan's cohort in the score window of $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the revised analysis keeps 85,986,562 approvals (63.3\%) and 442,722,039 transfers (76.2\%), and every kept transfer has a price. Table~\ref{tab:usd-tokens} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-tokens} gives these figures per token.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:implementation]{Implementation}

Every algorithm runs on one Python implementation of PageRank ($d=0.85$, $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$, $I_{\max}=300$). EndorseRank and AWP differ in their edge lists and restart vectors, and C-PR mixes its two edge lists node by node (Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition}). Each evaluation run writes its scores, per-proxy statistics and bootstrap intervals to one summary file, and the benchmark its timings to another. The tables of this chapter come from runs made on 28~October~2026 from 09:16~UTC. At $d=0.85$ every solve converged well inside the iteration cap. Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} lists the runs, the repository revision, the configuration file and the summary files.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:benchmark-results]{Computational benchmark (Claim~C5)}

Claim~C5 concerns what each score costs to compute on the same input. Following the timing procedure of Section~\ref{sec:computational-benchmark}, Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} reports for each algorithm, on the full graphs of the matched cohort, the mean solve time and its standard deviation over five timed runs after one untimed warm-up, the peak memory of the warm-up solve, the number of iterations to convergence and the nodes and edges of the solver graph. Each graph and setting was timed once, so the same measurement reappears as the full-cohort row of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} and as the $d=0.85$ row of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}; Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runs} in Appendix~\ref{sec:benchmark-run-logs} lists every timed run.

\input{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}

EndorseRank solves the allowance graph in $2.66$\,s and AWP the transfer graph in $8.53$\,s, so AWP takes $3.21$ times as long, at an edge ratio of $2.21$. Each power iteration costs a sparse product over the $|E|$ edges and several vector operations over the $|V|$ nodes (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}). Divided by the iterations, assembly included, AWP's solve takes $0.091$\,s and EndorseRank's $0.047$\,s, $1.95$ times as long, between the node ratio of $1.43$ and the edge ratio, and AWP needs $1.65$ times as many iterations ($94$ against $57$); together the two factors give the time ratio. On the same graphs that ratio is $3.00$, $3.21$ and $2.56$ at $d=0.75$, $0.85$ and $0.95$ (Section~\ref{sub:damping}), so it does not follow the edge ratio. Both scores use the same solver, so the difference in speed comes from the size of each graph and from the iterations its walk needs there, which the benchmark does not separate; it is no advantage of the EndorseRank algorithm.

C-PR walks on the union of both edge sets ($10{,}562{,}390$ distinct pairs, $4{,}111{,}490$ nodes), but its solver keeps one matrix per layer and multiplies both in every iteration, up to about $12.2$ million stored entries, $1.45$ times AWP's edges. It converges in fewer iterations ($82$ against $94$) and takes $11.56$\,s, $1.36$ times as long as AWP. S-PR runs on the transfer layer and takes $10.88$\,s. Both its time and its iteration count include the solve of the allowance layer that supplies its restart vector: the $151$ iterations are $56$ for that solve plus $95$ for the seeded transfer walk. Every algorithm that walks the transfer layer, alone or with allowance edges, pays for its size.

Two caveats attach to this result. The time covers only the solver call on edge lists built in advance; a full refresh would add parsing, decoding and edge-list construction, which all algorithms share wholly or in part. And a faster method is not necessarily a better one.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:same-window-alignment]{Same-window alignment (Claim~C4)}

Claim~C4 asks what each score agrees with in the window it is computed from. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} reports the family-mean Kendall $\tau$ of every score on the transfer, allowance and Sybil-stability families, each with a 95\% paired-bootstrap confidence interval. The raw degrees have no row because in this window they are proxies: in-degree belongs to the transfer family and in-approve degree to the allowance family. Apart from the secondary part of rule~A, fixed in the analysis plan (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}) and applied here to the revised scores as a re-analysis, none of the comparisons below was fixed in advance (Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}).

\input{results/tables/alignment-hybrid.tex}

The scores divide by the edges they walk on. The two that walk on transfers alone, AWP and C-PR ($\lambda=0$), reach $0.736$ ($[0.728,0.744]$) and $0.700$ ($[0.691,0.709]$) on the transfer family and $0.226$ ($[0.210,0.245]$) and $0.194$ ($[0.178,0.213]$) on the allowance family; S-PR, whose restarts come from the allowance walk, reaches $0.671$ ($[0.661,0.681]$) and $0.289$ ($[0.272,0.308]$). The two that walk on allowances alone, EndorseRank and C-PR ($\lambda=1$), reach $0.560$ ($[0.548,0.571]$) and $0.561$ ($[0.550,0.572]$) on the allowance family and both $0.196$ ($[0.180,0.216]$) on the transfer family, and the interior mixtures of C-PR lie between the two layers. On the Sybil-stability family the scores that walk on transfers lead, AWP with $0.419$ ($[0.408,0.434]$), while EndorseRank stays at $0.026$ ($[0.012,0.041]$). Part of this agreement is built in. The main proxies of each family come from the same events as one of the graphs, in-degree and in-value from the transfers and in-approve degree and value from the latest allowances, and a PageRank smooths the in-degree of its own graph. Agreement with the proxies of a score's own edge type is therefore an intended-construct check and cannot count as an external test. The holdout of Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results} asks the same question out of window.

Appendix~\ref{sec:proxy-family-detail} breaks the family means of EndorseRank and AWP into their proxies. AWP's largest coefficient is with transfer in-degree ($\tau=0.742$, $[0.732,0.752]$); with in-value it is almost as high ($0.730$, $[0.720,0.740]$). That is what its weights lead one to expect: with the slope set at the median transfer (Section~\ref{sub:awp-graph}), an edge weight grows with the amounts transferred as well as with their number. In the registered analysis, with $b=1$ on raw base units, $V$ counted nearly every transfer about once, and AWP followed the in-degree much more closely than the in-value (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}). It does not collapse to the in-degree either: propagation, time weights and restarts at active senders shift a measurable share of pairwise orderings, although out of window these shifts rank new senders below the in-degree (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}). On the Sybil-stability family AWP follows tenure ($0.588$, $[0.575,0.600]$) and active months ($0.616$, $[0.603,0.629]$), and it is positive on the inbound-counterparty ratio ($0.055$, $[0.033,0.079]$).

EndorseRank follows the in-approve degree ($0.650$, $[0.639,0.661]$) more closely than the in-approve value ($0.469$, $[0.454,0.484]$). Its value weight is $0.8$ at twice the median allowance of 100 dollars, so an allowance of a few hundred dollars and an unlimited one weigh nearly alike, whereas the in-approve value counts each allowance up to the cap of 19,000 dollars. Because every cohort contract received approvals from at least three owners, EndorseRank leaves few ties, so its coefficients rest on an ordering of nearly every contract (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}). C-PR ($\lambda=1$) walks the same allowance graph with capped amounts, also restarts uniformly, and stays within $0.002$ of EndorseRank on every family.

The first half of Claim~C4 is supported in a narrow sense. Every score that walks on one edge type agrees most with the proxies of that edge type, with an interval well clear of zero, and the mixtures of C-PR lie between their two layers on every family. The agreement is partly built in. Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results} locates C-PR within the window.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:rank-divergence]{Rank divergence (H1 / RQ1)}

Hypothesis~H1 and RQ1 concern whether EndorseRank and AWP put the same contracts in different orders. The inter-method Kendall $\tau_b$ between the two score vectors is $0.199$, with interval $[0.181,0.221]$ (last row of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}), so the interval excludes both zero and one. The two rankings are related, yet far from interchangeable. Of the $21{,}011{,}403$ contract pairs, $96.4\%$ are ordered by both scores, and $39.8\%$ of those pairs are discordant.

The marginal score distributions are in Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}, and ties play almost no part in them: EndorseRank, with $5{,}641$ distinct scores, ties $0.20\%$ of the contract pairs, and AWP, with $5{,}292$, $3.38\%$. Under each score the largest tie block is formed by the contracts that score zero: $197$ under EndorseRank and $1{,}192$ under AWP, that is, the $191$ and $1{,}192$ contracts outside each graph and, under EndorseRank, $6$ whose only pairs carry amounts too small for the value weight to differ from zero. Under EndorseRank $63$ further contracts share the score $c=1.93\times10^{-7}$ of a node without in-edges; $62$ of them are in the allowance graph only as owners, and one has received only allowances worth less than a cent, which leave its score at $c$ to nine significant digits; every other cohort contract has received a larger allowance and scores above $c$. A marginal distribution cannot show how two scores order the same contracts, so Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter} plots the two rankings against each other for a random sample of $3{,}000$ contracts. Contracts that both scores rank near the top, or both near the bottom, lie close to the identity line; in between, the points spread over most of the square.

The zero blocks come from one rule of this pipeline: a contract missing from a graph scores zero (Section~\ref{sec:graph-construction}). The analysis plan fixed the alternative, keeping the missing contracts as isolated nodes, as a sensitivity analysis. Under uniform teleportation an isolated node scores like any node without in-edges, so the $191$ contracts outside the allowance graph receive $c$, the lowest score that any node of the graph can have, and join the $63$ contracts at $c$ near the bottom of the order. The $6$ contracts whose only pairs carry zero weight are nodes of the graph that the solver leaves out, so they keep the score zero. No pair changes direction: the pairs between the $191$ and the $63$ become ties, and those between the $191$ and the $6$ become ordered. AWP's $1{,}192$ missing contracts were handled the same way; under AWP's restarts an isolated contract that has sent nothing receives no restart mass, so it scores zero either way. Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} gives the result. No coefficient changes at the third decimal, and the agreement between the two scores stays at $0.199$ ($[0.181,0.221]$). Because the cohort was defined by approvals received, nearly every cohort contract is a spender in the allowance graph, and the rule for missing contracts decides almost nothing.

H1 is supported. The two scores order the cohort differently, and the difference does not come from ties or from the rule for missing contracts. A protocol that relies on transfer-based scores cannot assume that its ordering of contracts survives a switch to allowance-based scores or a blend with them. This divergence, the second half of Claim~C4, is also the premise of the coupled walk: each single-layer walk orders the contracts by one kind of act, and Section~\ref{sec:cpr-results} asks whether one walk over both edge types does better than either.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:robustness-results]{Robustness and sensitivity checks}

Three checks, run for EndorseRank and AWP, ask whether the results hinge on one damping value, one slope of the value weight or one sample definition. The value-slope check takes the place of the plan's token check, which lost its point once every graph keeps five tokens (Section~\ref{sub:top-tokens}). The first two hold the sample fixed; the third varies the sample definition itself, so its coefficients are expected to move, and it is not evidence of robustness. A fourth check asks how much EndorseRank depends on its restart rule.

For EndorseRank no family mean moves by more than $0.0031$ across the three values. For AWP the transfer coefficient falls from $0.740$ ($[0.732,0.748]$) to $0.723$ ($[0.715,0.731]$) as $d$ rises, and the Sybil-stability coefficient from $0.427$ ($[0.416,0.441]$) to $0.406$ ($[0.394,0.420]$). EndorseRank restarts the walk uniformly, while AWP restarts it at addresses in proportion to their recent sending activity (Section~\ref{sub:endorserank-graph}). The analysis plan fixed uniform restarts for EndorseRank and named the variant as a score to report beside it, without fixing any comparison between the two. Table~\ref{tab:endorserank-restarts} scores both versions of EndorseRank, with AWP beside them.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:holdout-results]{Temporal holdout of the single-layer walks (RQ3)}

A same-window check cannot separate a score from its own construct. The holdout instead tests whether centrality measured up to a cut-off predicts behavior after it. Following Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}, scores on the W0 spender cohort are frozen at $t_1$ (31~March~2026), and two labels are then counted for each contract between 1~April and 30~June~2026: new owner--spender approval pairs (the endorsement construct) and new transfer senders (the flow construct). \dissertationSubheading[sec:cpr-results]{The coupled walk C-PR (RQ4, RQ5)}

C-PR is the method of this thesis: one random walk over the US-dollar allowance layer and the US-dollar transfer layer, which at each node mixes the two transition rows with weight $\lambda$ (Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}). The analysis plan fixed one decision rule, and its object is C-PR: rule~A asks the coupled walk to beat each of its layers walked alone (RQ4). The registration of W1 added rule~B, which asks it to improve on its transfer layer on new data (RQ5). Both rules compare C-PR with its own layers, C-PR ($\lambda=1$) and C-PR ($\lambda=0$), because only that contrast changes the coupling and nothing else; C-PR also differs from EndorseRank and AWP in its amount weights and its uniform restarts. Both rules are applied to the revised scores as a re-analysis. This section places C-PR within the window, then reports rule~A in W0 and rule~B in W1, and ends with its contrasts with every other score of the study, which decide nothing.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sec:coupled-results]{The coupled walk within the window}

Two hybrids were defined in Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}. C-PR mixes the US-dollar allowance and transfer layers in each node's transition row with weight $\lambda$, and its ablation S-PR keeps the transfer layer but restarts from the allowance walk. Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} gives their paired contrasts in this window: first the eight of Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests} against the two layers walked alone, then six against EndorseRank and AWP, two of them computed afterwards from the saved scores. RQ4 and RQ5 are decided out of window. Here we only locate the hybrids on the checks that the single-layer scores were built to pass.

\input{results/tables/tau-diff-hybrid.tex}

C-PR lies between its two layers on every family and stays nearer to its allowance layer. At the primary setting it reaches $0.449$ ($[0.437,0.463]$) on the allowance family, $0.112$ below C-PR ($\lambda=1$) ($[-0.124,-0.100]$), and $0.400$ ($[0.385,0.416]$) on the transfer family, $0.300$ below C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($[-0.318,-0.281]$). As $\lambda$ rises from $0.25$ to $0.75$ it moves toward the allowance layer on all three families. AWP ranks the transfer family better than C-PR ($-0.336$, $[-0.354,-0.316]$), and EndorseRank the allowance family ($-0.111$, $[-0.122,-0.099]$). On the family that each single-layer walk follows least, C-PR leads that walk: it ranks the transfer family $0.204$ above EndorseRank ($[0.191,0.214]$) and the allowance family $0.223$ above AWP ($[0.206,0.240]$). A node with out-edges in only one layer follows that layer whatever $\lambda$ is (Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition}); $\lambda$ acts only at the nodes with out-edges in both layers. Almost every node of the allowance layer is an owner with out-edges, so the allowance rows enter the walk wherever an owner also sent a transfer that the extraction kept. On the Sybil-stability family C-PR exceeds its allowance layer ($+0.115$, $[0.104,0.123]$) and lies below its transfer layer ($-0.260$, $[-0.278,-0.241]$).

On every family, S-PR falls below the layer that leads that family or is level with it. Restarting the transfer walk from the allowance walk drops the transfer coefficient to $0.671$ ($[0.661,0.681]$; $\Delta\tau=-0.029$, $[-0.036,-0.023]$, against C-PR ($\lambda=0$)), raises the allowance coefficient only as far as $0.289$ ($[0.272,0.308]$; $\Delta\tau=-0.272$, $[-0.290,-0.253]$, against C-PR ($\lambda=1$)), and leaves Sybil-adjusted stability level with the transfer layer ($-0.004$, $[-0.008,0.000]$). A restart vector changes where the walk lands after each teleport. It carries part of the allowance signal into the transfer walk, but less than walking the allowance edges does. This is the same-window counterpart of the finding that Section~\ref{sub:cpr-rule-a} reaches out of window.

As fixed in the analysis plan, the secondary part of rule~A asks C-PR's point estimate to lie within the 95\% interval of the better layer on the transfer and allowance families, and C-PR to exceed both layers on Sybil-adjusted stability. At $\lambda=0.5$, C-PR fails all three parts, as in the registered analysis (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}). Its transfer coefficient ($0.400$) lies below the interval of C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($[0.691,0.709]$), its allowance coefficient ($0.449$) lies below that of C-PR ($\lambda=1$) ($[0.550,0.572]$), and on Sybil-adjusted stability it exceeds only the allowance layer. The paired contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}, reported alongside the rule as a stricter check, point the same way. In the same-window tables the mixture gives up part of each layer's agreement with its own family and exceeds the better layer on no family. The holdout takes up the remaining question of whether the mixture has any predictive content that the single layers lack.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:cpr-rule-a]{Rule~A in the window W0}

The coupled walk fails rule~A out of window, although in an exploratory comparison it improves on its transfer layer walked alone. That rule, applied here to the revised scores as a re-analysis, asked C-PR to do no worse than C-PR ($\lambda=1$) on new approval pairs and to beat C-PR ($\lambda=0$) on new transfer senders in W0 (first block of Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff}). On the spender holdout, the allowance signal survives when allowance edges carry the walk, fully in the allowance walk and mixed in C-PR, and most of it is lost when they only decide where the walk restarts.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:empirical-claims]{Claims (C1--C6)}

The chapter's results support the claims below, cross-referenced to the hypothesis and research question columns of Table~\ref{tab:hrq-claim-map}. C1--C5 are the primary claims; C2 and C3 rest on contrasts computed after the labels were known, and C6 on the comparison on outcomes built from neither edge type. The verdicts of rules~A and~B in C1 and of R1--R3 in C6 apply the registered rules to the revised scores, as a re-analysis; the registered analysis reached the same verdicts except on R1 (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}).

\begin{enumerate}
\item[C1.] Under its rules, C-PR, a fixed-$\lambda$ mixture of two dollar-weighted layers with uniform restarts, does not beat its two layers walked alone. Rule~A fails: C-PR is no worse than the allowance walk on new approval pairs ($+0.031$, $[0.021,0.042]$) but worse than the transfer walk on new senders ($-0.030$, $[-0.040,-0.020]$), and its same-window part fails as well (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}). Rule~B is not met (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}): the W0 gain over the transfer walk on new approval pairs, found after the labels were known, recurs in W1 (F1, $+0.170$, $[0.149,0.189]$), but C-PR falls below the transfer walk on new senders by more than the margin of $0.01$ (F2, $-0.025$, $[-0.038,-0.015]$), and F3 fails narrowly ($+0.000$, $[-0.010,0.010]$). Against AWP, in the first sensitivity analysis, F2 fails clearly ($-0.075$, $[-0.085,-0.065]$). S-PR ranks new senders slightly above the transfer walk and new approval pairs well below the allowance walk, so allowance edges help mainly when they carry the walk; Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results} reports where the hybrids stand within the window.
\item[C2.] Beside the rules, C-PR ranks both spender labels above EndorseRank in both windows (new approval pairs $+0.026$, $[0.016,0.037]$, and $+0.017$, $[0.006,0.029]$; new senders $+0.064$, $[0.052,0.075]$, and $+0.064$, $[0.051,0.076]$) and leads it within the window on the transfer and Sybil-stability families. It ranks new approval pairs above AWP ($+0.133$, $[0.114,0.153]$; $+0.159$, $[0.139,0.178]$), largely through the spenders that AWP scores zero: among the spenders that had received a transfer, the two are level in W0 and C-PR leads by $0.022$ ($[0.011,0.033]$) in W1. C-PR ranks new senders below AWP and below the transfer in-degree in both windows, new approval pairs below the in-approve degree in both windows ($-0.063$, $[-0.076,-0.052]$; $-0.065$, $[-0.077,-0.054]$) and below EndorseRank with activity restarts in W1 ($-0.019$, $[-0.029,-0.008]$), and the liquidation-free close rate of the W1 traders below the in-approve degree ($-0.112$, $[-0.194,-0.026]$) (Table~\ref{tab:cpr-contrasts}). None of these contrasts was fixed in advance.
\item[C3.] Out of window neither single-layer walk beats the raw degree it smooths. AWP ranks later new transfer senders below the transfer in-degree in both windows (W0 $-0.038$, $[-0.043,-0.032]$; W1 $-0.041$, $[-0.047,-0.034]$), and EndorseRank ranks later new approval pairs below the in-approve degree in both (W0 $-0.090$, $[-0.100,-0.079]$; W1 $-0.082$, $[-0.090,-0.073]$) (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:fresh-posthoc}); in W0 neither comparison is a matter of coverage (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}), and no such restriction of these two contrasts was computed for W1. With activity restarts EndorseRank also stays below its count (Section~\ref{sub:endorserank-restarts}), so on these labels propagation adds nothing to the count of the same edge type, whatever the restart rule. On the profit labels of the contract traders of W0 the paired contrasts separate the two sides only on realized gain, where the transfer layer walked alone ranks above the allowance layer (C-PR ($\lambda=1$) minus C-PR ($\lambda=0$) $-0.257$, $[-0.447,-0.071]$), not on profitable closes (EndorseRank minus AWP $-0.046$, $[-0.205,0.113]$), and no score ranks the share of closes that were profitable (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-traders} and~\ref{tab:neutral-label-grid}). These comparisons were computed after the labels were known.
\item[C4.] In the same window each single-layer walk agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on, EndorseRank with the allowance family ($0.560$, $[0.548,0.571]$) and AWP with the transfer family ($0.736$, $[0.728,0.744]$), and the mixtures of C-PR lie between their two layers (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}). These tests measure internal consistency, and part of the agreement is built in; external validity is outside their scope. The two walks order the cohort differently: their agreement, $\tau_b=0.199$ ($[0.181,0.221]$), has an interval that excludes zero and one; $96.4\%$ of the contract pairs are ordered by both scores, and $39.8\%$ of those are discordant. Neither result comes from ties or from the rule for contracts outside a graph (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}, Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}).
\item[C5.] On identical contract sets, the cost of each score's solver call grows with the size of its graph, since each iteration costs time in $|E|$ and $|V|$, and with the iterations its walk needs. The allowance graph has 45.2\% of the transfer graph's edges, and AWP takes $3.21$ times as long as EndorseRank at an edge ratio of $2.21$ (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}). On the subsamples the time ratio follows the edge ratio at no stage, and the stages do not test edge counts beyond those of the cohort graph (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling}). The low cost is no advantage of the algorithm: C-PR and S-PR walk the transfer layer and take longer than AWP, C-PR $1.36$ times as long.
\item[C6.] On later liquidation avoidance of the GMX~V2 contract traders, the one outcome built from neither edge type that bears on counterparty risk, the approval count at the freeze leads the transfer count in the primary window W1 (P $=+0.141$, 97.5\% interval $[0.026,0.256]$), while in W0 P is positive but not resolved ($+0.095$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.041,0.263]$). The PageRank versions are not resolved (Q $=+0.023$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.071,0.127]$). In W1 the approval count ranks traders lower than the transfer count on the shares of closes that were profitable or not losing, so, in the wording of R3, the signal concerns forced-liquidation avoidance, not trading success; in W0 neither difference is resolved (Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-grid}). The samples are small, 70 and 87 contract traders, and every interval is wide (Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}).
\end{enumerate}

The same-window tables and the rank divergence answer RQ1, the benchmark tables RQ2, the holdouts of the single-layer walks with Table~\ref{tab:cpr-contrasts} RQ3, and Section~\ref{sec:cpr-results} RQ4 and RQ5; C6 adds no research question. The direct comparisons between EndorseRank and AWP are in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp} and Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-contrasts}, and none of them carries a claim of the thesis.
