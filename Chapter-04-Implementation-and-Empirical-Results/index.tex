\chapter{Implementation and Empirical Results}
\label{ch:results}

Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} specified how EndorseRank and the Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP) baseline are built, which construct checks are applied, how uncertainty is quantified and how runtime is timed. This chapter applies that design to Arbitrum One and reports what was observed. It is organised as a single case study: one chain, one observation window, one matched cohort for all same-window comparisons, one spender cohort for the temporal holdout and one expanded address pool for node-count sensitivity. Tables are generated from the evaluation summary in Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}, and confidence intervals accompany every Kendall $\tau$ coefficient.

The chapter proceeds from the case-study setting (Section~\ref{sec:dataset}), through the computational benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-results}), the same-window alignment results with difference tests (Sections~\ref{sec:three-method-alignment}--\ref{sec:proxy-family-detail}), inter-method rank divergence (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}), robustness and sensitivity checks (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-results}) and the temporal holdout (Section~\ref{sec:gf-pr-ceiling}), to a consolidated statement of the empirical claims C1--C4 (Section~\ref{sec:empirical-claims}).

\dissertationSubheading[sec:dataset]{Case study setting: Arbitrum One}

Arbitrum One was selected as the case because it is a high-activity Ethereum rollup with mature DeFi protocols (Section~\ref{sec:ethereum-l2}), its logs are available in the public BigQuery export used by the pipeline, and a margin-trading protocol on it (GMX~V2) supplies the wallet seed set described below. The observation window is 1~December~2025 to 31~May~2026. Six months is long enough to span several market regimes and to give tenure and active-month proxies room to vary, and short enough that a latest-allowance snapshot remains contemporaneous with the transfer history that AWP aggregates.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:cohorts]{Cohorts}

Three address sets are used, each for one purpose.

\begin{itemize}
\item Matched cohort ($n=5{,}521$). Wallets seeded from GMX~V2 margin-trading activity that have non-zero connectivity in both the latest-allowance endorsement graph $G_{\text{approve}}$ and the time-decayed transfer graph $G_{\text{transfer}}$ over the window. All same-window alignment, difference-test and benchmark results refer to this cohort. Requiring connectivity in both graphs ensures that neither method is evaluated on wallets for which it can only return the teleportation floor.
\item Holdout spender cohort ($n=1{,}335$). Wallets that appear as spenders in at least one allowance during the scoring period (events up to 28~February~2026). Their scores are tested against owner--spender approval pairs first observed between 1~March and 31~May~2026 (Section~\ref{sec:gf-pr-graph}).
\item Expanded wallet pool ($N=99{,}080$). The matched cohort unioned with $93{,}559$ supplemental addresses sampled from monthly ERC-20 activity on the chain. The pool is used only for node-count sensitivity of runtime and for the sample-definition sensitivity analysis (Section~\ref{sub:expanded-pool}); its limitation---supplemental addresses contribute nodes but no additional extracted edges---is restated where the results are reported.
\end{itemize}

Graph sizes for the matched cohort are given with the benchmark in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}. The endorsement graph has roughly one ninth as many edges as the transfer graph on the same wallets. That asymmetry is a property of the two representations rather than of the extraction: both graphs are built from the same wallet seed set and window, but EndorseRank keeps only the latest decimal-adjusted allowance per owner--spender pair, so repeated approvals collapse to one directed edge, whereas AWP accumulates every transfer between two wallets into a decayed weight. Differences in scores and alignment are therefore attributable to ranking logic and graph semantics, not to inconsistent sampling.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:implementation]{Implementation}

Both methods run through the same Python PageRank solver with identical damping ($d=0.85$), tolerance ($10^{-8}$) and iteration cap; only the edge lists differ (Section~\ref{sec:implementation-notes}). Scores, per-proxy statistics, bootstrap intervals and timings are written to one summary file per run; the tables below were produced from the run of 12~September~2026. The repository revision and configuration for that run are recorded in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:benchmark-results]{Computational benchmark (Claim~C1)}

Claim~C1 asserts that EndorseRank is computationally cheaper than AWP on identical inputs. Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} reports PageRank solve time, its standard deviation over the five timed repeats, peak memory during the warm-up solve, iterations to convergence and the size of each method's evaluation subgraph on the matched cohort, following the timing protocol of Section~\ref{sec:computational-benchmark}.

\input{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}

Three mechanisms account for the runtime ratio in the table. First, both parts of the timed solve---assembling the sparse transition operator from the edge list and each power-iteration step---are linear in $|E|$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}), and the endorsement graph has about one ninth of the transfer graph's edges. Second, EndorseRank converges in roughly a third of the iterations AWP needs; the transfer operator, with its long-tailed decayed weights, mixes more slowly. Third, peak memory follows $|E|$ as well, since adjacency, weight and residual buffers scale with the edge list. The standard deviations show that run-to-run noise is an order of magnitude smaller than the gap between methods.

Two remarks bound the claim. The benchmark times the solver call on pre-built edge lists; end-to-end refresh cost includes extraction, decoding and edge-list construction, which are shared or partially shared across methods and are not part of C1. And ``faster'' is not ``better'': the runtime result acquires meaning only when paired with the alignment results of Sections~\ref{sec:three-method-alignment}--\ref{sec:proxy-family-detail}, which is why Claim~C4 combines the two.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:runtime-scaling]{Node-count sensitivity on the expanded pool}

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} reports runtime at three staged node counts drawn from the expanded pool by deterministic SHA256 subsampling, together with the per-stage $|V|$ and $|E|$ of each method's subgraph. In-cohort stages ($n\le 5{,}521$) are given in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling-incohort}).

\input{results/tables/benchmark-scaling.tex}

Two features of the table need to be read together with Section~\ref{sub:expanded-pool}. First, the full-pool row is numerically identical to the matched-cohort row of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}. This is not a copy: the supplemental addresses have no extracted approval or transfer logs, so the induced subgraph at $N=99{,}080$ contains exactly the cohort edges, and the timing protocol times each distinct graph once per session and reuses that measurement wherever the same graph is tabulated. The same measurement therefore appears in the $d=0.85$ row of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}. Second, $|E|$ is not monotone in $n$ across stages, because each stage is the subgraph induced by whichever wallets the hash order selects; the $n=10{,}000$ stage happens to select comparatively few cohort wallets and hence few edges. What the table supports is a bounded statement: across stages whose edge counts vary by a factor of five to seven, the AWP/EndorseRank runtime ratio stays within roughly $8$--$10\times$ and EndorseRank remains in the sub-two-second regime. It does not show how either method scales when the edge count itself grows with the address population, since the second-tier extraction that would have supplied those edges did not complete.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:three-method-alignment]{Same-window alignment: EndorseRank vs.\ AWP (Claims~C2--C3)}

Claims~C2 and~C3 ask whether each method recovers its intended construct on contemporaneous local statistics and whether the winners are construct-specific. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci} reports family-mean Kendall $\tau$ with 95\% paired-bootstrap intervals for the transfer, allowance and Sybil-stability families, together with the inter-method agreement between the two score vectors. Figure~\ref{fig:alignment-heatmap} shows the same family means as a heatmap.

\input{results/tables/alignment-family-ci.tex}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{\figpath{alignment-heatmap.pdf}}
\caption{Family-mean Kendall $\tau$ on the matched cohort ($n=5{,}521$) for EndorseRank and AWP; the values are those of Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}.}
\label{fig:alignment-heatmap}
\end{figure}

The pattern is crossed. AWP aligns most strongly with the transfer family and is slightly negative on the allowance family; EndorseRank aligns most strongly with the allowance family and moderately with the transfer family. Whether each contrast exceeds sampling variation is tested in Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}, which reports the six pre-specified paired differences of Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests}.

\input{results/tables/tau-diff.tex}

Four of the six contrasts have intervals that exclude zero: AWP's transfer alignment exceeds EndorseRank's, EndorseRank's allowance alignment exceeds AWP's, and AWP exceeds EndorseRank on the Sybil-stability and (archived) GMX-success families. Two do not. EndorseRank's allowance alignment is not statistically higher than its own transfer alignment (contrast~i), and EndorseRank's in-approve-degree alignment is not statistically higher than its in-degree alignment (contrast~vi). These two null results bound the interpretation of the allowance family. EndorseRank is a global smoothing of spender in-approve degree, so its agreement with allowance proxies is an intended-construct check, not an external test. Transfer centrality (AWP) carries no information about who receives spending authorisation, whereas endorsement centrality (EndorseRank) does, and the reverse holds for transfer volume. The within-method claim that EndorseRank prefers allowance over transfer signal is not supported.

Claim~C2 is supported in the sense that each method achieves non-trivial alignment on its own construct with an interval well away from zero. Claim~C3 is supported by the two between-method contrasts (ii and iii) whose intervals exclude zero in opposite directions: neither method dominates both checks.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:proxy-family-detail]{Proxy family detail}

The family means summarise per-proxy coefficients. This section reports Spearman $\rho$, Kendall $\tau$ and the 95\% interval of $\tau$ for each proxy. Proxies are oriented so that higher values imply higher expected reputation before correlation, and the transfer proxies are those used to validate AWP in its source paper\cend{3}.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:transfer-family]{Transfer family}

\input{results/tables/alignment-transfer.tex}

AWP's agreement with in-degree is the largest coefficient in the chapter, and its agreement with in-value is smaller but still far above EndorseRank's. Both are primary-construct checks for AWP, since it ranks on the time-decayed transfer graph; the informative observation is that AWP does not collapse to in-degree---logistic decay and value weighting move a measurable share of pairwise orderings. EndorseRank's transfer coefficients are moderate and their intervals overlap neither zero nor AWP's intervals. Wallets that receive many transfers are somewhat more likely to be endorsed spenders, but the two behaviours are far from interchangeable. If a protocol's notion of reputation is inbound economic flow, AWP is the appropriate method on this evidence.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:allowance-family]{Allowance family}

\input{results/tables/alignment-allowance.tex}

EndorseRank's coefficients on in-approve degree and in-approve value are nearly equal, which indicates that on this cohort being approved by many owners and being approved for large aggregate value convey similar ranking information under latest-allowance semantics. AWP's coefficients are slightly negative with intervals that exclude zero: transfer centrality does not recover authorisation. That dissociation is the empirical core of the same-window contribution. If transfer-based reputation were a sufficient proxy for endorsement, AWP would show at least moderate allowance alignment; it does not, so protocols that care about which addresses are trusted to move tokens on behalf of others cannot substitute AWP for an allowance-aware method. The caveat of Section~\ref{sec:three-method-alignment} applies: EndorseRank's own allowance coefficients are a construct check, and the out-of-window test of the same construct is the holdout of Section~\ref{sec:gf-pr-ceiling}.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:sybil-family]{Sybil-adjusted stability family}

\input{results/tables/alignment-sybil-stability.tex}

AWP exceeds EndorseRank on tenure and active months, as expected from time-decayed transfer edges that reward sustained activity; the paired difference on the family mean (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}, contrast~iv) excludes zero. The inbound-counterparty ratio is weak for both methods and negative for AWP. This family is a robustness note on how much longitudinal transfer information each method carries, not a headline claim.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:rank-divergence]{Rank divergence (H1 / RQ1)}

Hypothesis~H1 and RQ1 ask whether EndorseRank and AWP order the same wallets differently. The inter-method Kendall $\tau$ between the two score vectors, with its interval, is given in the last row of Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}. The rankings are related but far from interchangeable: agreement exceeds chance because economically active wallets participate in both approval and transfer markets, yet a large fraction of pairwise orderings disagree.

Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions} shows the marginal score distributions. Both are right-skewed, as PageRank on sparse directed graphs typically is, but they differ in shape and in the number of wallets that sit at the teleportation floor. Skewness alone does not establish divergence, so Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter} plots the ranks directly: the cloud is positively sloped but wide, with substantial off-diagonal mass.

\begin{figure}[htbp]

\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{\figpath{rank-scatter.pdf}}

Rank divergence is the structural counterpart of construct-specific alignment. If the two methods produced nearly identical orderings, the family-level winners of Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci} could be noise around a shared ranking; the observed divergence, together with the opposite-signed between-method contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}, supports reading the two as related but distinct instruments. H1 is supported. Operationally, a protocol that currently uses transfer-based scores cannot assume that switching to, or blending in, allowance-based scores will preserve its wallet ordering; the largest disagreements are expected among transfer hubs that are rarely endorsed as spenders and among endorsed spenders with small transfer footprints.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:robustness-results]{Robustness and sensitivity checks}

The results above would be of limited use if they depended on a single damping value, a single token mix or a single sample definition. Three checks follow. The first two are robustness checks in the strict sense: the sample is held fixed and a parameter or the token universe is perturbed. The third is a sensitivity analysis: the sample definition itself is changed, and the coefficients are expected to move.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:damping]{Damping}

\input{results/tables/robustness-damping.tex}

Family means are flat across $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$ to the third decimal for EndorseRank and move by at most a few thousandths for AWP; the crossed pattern is not an artefact of the conventional $d=0.85$. Because allowance edges encode a snapshot rather than a long path structure, changing how much mass teleports does not reorder wallets enough to alter family-level alignment. Runtimes do vary with $d$. AWP is markedly slowest at $d=0.95$, consistent with the slower mixing of a high-damping operator; EndorseRank's variation across the sweep is of the same order as its between-run spread in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} and is not interpreted. The runtime ratio therefore depends on the damping setting even though the alignment conclusion does not. Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity} plots the family means.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{\figpath{damping-sensitivity.pdf}}
\caption{Damping sensitivity of family-mean Kendall $\tau$ for the allowance and transfer families, $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$ (values of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}).}

\end{figure}

Restricting both graphs to the twenty highest-volume ERC-20 tokens changes every family mean by at most a few thousandths. The alignment pattern is therefore not driven by long-tail tokens with sparse or spam-like edges; the economically salient core of ERC-20 activity suffices to reproduce the EndorseRank--AWP contrast.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:sample-size]{Sample-definition sensitivity}

\input{results/tables/robustness-sample-size.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size} recomputes the transfer and allowance family means on the staged pool subsamples used for the runtime scaling. The coefficients are much larger than on the matched cohort, and AWP's allowance coefficient turns positive. Neither change is evidence about the methods. Pool subsamples admit wallets that have no edges in one or both graphs; for such wallets the score and the proxy are both zero after alignment, and tied zeros at the bottom of both rankings raise Kendall $\tau$ mechanically (Section~\ref{sub:sample-size-protocol}). The table is therefore reported as a sensitivity analysis of the sample definition, and it answers a narrower question: does the relative ordering of the two methods on each family survive a change of population? It does---AWP stays ahead on transfer and EndorseRank on allowance at every stage---but the absolute values in the table must not be compared with those of Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}, whose cohort excludes exactly the wallets that inflate them.

The damping and top-token checks support reading Claims~C1--C3 as stable under parameter and token-mix perturbation. The sample-definition check shows that the comparative structure, not the decimal values, is what travels across populations.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:gf-pr-ceiling]{Temporal holdout of future new approvals (RQ4)}

The same-window checks cannot separate a method from its own construct. RQ4 therefore asks whether endorsement centrality measured up to a cut-off predicts endorsement behaviour after it. Scores are frozen at 28~February~2026 and labels are new owner--spender approval pairs first observed from 1~March to 31~May~2026, counted on the spender; the protocol is that of Section~\ref{sec:gf-pr-graph}. Table~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} reports the result.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Temporal holdout on the spender cohort ($n=1{,}335$): Kendall $\tau$ between scores frozen at 28~February~2026 and the number of new owner--spender approval pairs received between 1~March and 31~May~2026. CI = 95\% percentile bootstrap over wallets (200 resamples). $222$ spenders received at least one new pair.}
\label{tab:holdout-spenders}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Method & Spearman $\rho$ & Kendall $\tau$ & 95\% CI of $\tau$ \\
\midrule
EndorseRank & 0.522 & 0.479 & [0.426, 0.529] \\
AWP & 0.053 & 0.044 & [$-0.011$, 0.095] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

EndorseRank's holdout coefficient is larger than its same-window allowance coefficient and its interval excludes AWP's, whose interval includes zero. Because the labels are built from events that post-date every scored event, this is out-of-window evidence for the endorsement construct, and it is the strongest single result in the chapter for the claim that EndorseRank measures something about spenders that transfer centrality does not. Two limits apply. The label is the same construct in the future tense---new approvals, not loss avoidance or repayment---so the result says nothing about credit risk. And the label is sparse: most spenders received no new pair in the outcome window, so the coefficient is driven by the ordering among the minority that did.

Other candidate labels computed for the holdout are not reported as support. Revoke counts and the drain heuristic correlated positively with EndorseRank, which has the wrong sign for an endorsement reading (a heavily endorsed spender is also a frequently revoked one), and Aave liquidation events on this spender set were empty. The trading-success comparison of the research proposal was withdrawn for the reason given in Section~\ref{sub:gf-pr}: the outcome-native reference it relied on is built from the same GMX events as the proxies it would be validated against.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:empirical-claims]{Primary claims (C1--C4)}

The results of this chapter support the following claims, mapped to hypotheses and research questions in Table~\ref{tab:hrq-claim-map}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[C1.] On identical wallet sets EndorseRank solves PageRank in a fraction of AWP's wall-clock time, with proportionally lower memory and fewer iterations, because its latest-allowance graph has about one ninth of the transfer graph's edges (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}). The ratio is stable across in-cohort and pool stages whose edge counts vary by a factor of five to seven (Tables~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} and~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling-incohort}); the pool stages do not test growth in edge count beyond the cohort graph.
\item[C2.] Each method recovers its intended construct on same-window local statistics, with bootstrap intervals well away from zero (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}). These are construct checks, not external validation.
\item[C3.] The winners are construct-specific: AWP exceeds EndorseRank on the transfer family and EndorseRank exceeds AWP on the allowance family, with paired-difference intervals that exclude zero in opposite directions (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff}); the two score vectors agree only moderately (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-family-ci}). EndorseRank's allowance lead over its own transfer alignment is not statistically established.
\item[C4.] The efficiency and construct results combine into a trade-off. EndorseRank is the cheaper method and the only one that aligns with authorisation checks, and on the spender holdout it predicts future new approvals where AWP does not (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders}). AWP remains the stronger method for transfer-hub detection and longitudinal stability.
\end{enumerate}

RQ1 is answered by the inter-method agreement and Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}; RQ2 by the family tables and difference tests; RQ3 by the benchmark tables; RQ4 by the holdout. The claims do not retire AWP. They report a construct-aware comparison with quantified uncertainty: EndorseRank is cheaper, aligns with allowance checks, diverges moderately from AWP and predicts later new approvals on the spender holdout.

Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} interprets these findings against the research questions and objectives of Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}, draws out their theoretical, practical and societal implications, and examines the threats to validity and limitations that bound them.
