\chapter{Implementation and Empirical Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter runs the design of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} on Arbitrum One as a single case study: one chain and one observation window, one matched cohort of contracts for every same-window comparison, spender and trader cohorts for the temporal holdouts W0 and W1, and subsamples of the matched cohort for runtime against size, with the registered window W1 and a comparison on outcomes built from neither edge type at the end of Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}. It reports the revised analysis of Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}, with amounts in US dollars and five tokens; the revision was decided after the registered results were known, so no result in this chapter is a registered result. Rules~A and~B and R1--R3 are applied to the revised scores, with their thresholds and wording, as a re-analysis, and the registered analysis reached the same verdicts (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}, Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}). Every table is generated from the evaluation summaries of Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}, each Kendall $\tau$ comes with its confidence interval except in the value-slope and sample-definition checks, and each PageRank is read against the raw degree it smooths.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:dataset]{Case study setting: Arbitrum One}

Arbitrum One was chosen because it is a heavily used Ethereum rollup with mature DeFi protocols (Section~\ref{sec:ethereum-l2}), because its logs are in the public BigQuery export that the pipeline reads, and because one of its margin-trading protocols, GMX~V2, records the position closes behind the trader labels. The observation window, 1~October~2023 to 30~June~2026 (33 months), spans several market regimes. The time decay is anchored at its end, $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$: the weight of an event falls to $0.5$ at 180~days and is close to zero for events more than two years old (Figure~\ref{fig:logistic-decay-methods}), so the last year carries most of the edge weight in AWP and EndorseRank.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:cohorts]{Cohorts}

Four sets of contracts are used. Every address in them holds contract code; externally owned accounts (EOAs) enter the graphs as owners, senders and recipients and are never scored (Section~\ref{sec:data-sources}).

\begin{itemize}
\item Matched cohort ($n=15{,}107$). The contracts that received a non-zero \texttt{Approval} of one of the five tokens from at least three distinct owners in the window (Section~\ref{sec:data-sources}). They are part of the analysis plan's cohort of 27,844 contracts, which applied the same rule to every token and left out as EOAs 1,121 of its 29,022 candidates without code and 57 with an EIP-7702 delegation designator. Membership does not depend on either graph: no allowance edge touches $523$ cohort contracts (by $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ every allowance granted to them or by them was zero), no transfer edge touches $2{,}670$, and $80$ are touched by neither; a contract absent from a graph scores zero under that method. Every same-window alignment, difference test and benchmark is computed on this cohort.
\item Spender cohorts of W0 and W1 ($n=17{,}050$ and $18{,}152$). In W0 the scores are frozen at $t_1$ (31~March~2026, 23:59:59~UTC), and the cohort is every spender that holds at least one positive latest allowance of one of the five tokens at $t_1$ in the extracted approval data and holds contract code: 17,050 of 17,167 such spenders (104 have no code and 13 an EIP-7702 designator). In W1 the scores are frozen at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, and the same rule gives 18,152 contract spenders. The spender cohorts are not subsets of the matched cohort, since a spender needs only one owner with a positive allowance. The scores of these contracts are tested against owner--spender approval pairs and transfer senders first seen in the three months after each freeze (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}).
\item Trader cohorts ($n=88$ and $70$). The GMX~V2 accounts that hold contract code, are nodes of the freeze-date graph and close at least three positions in the label window: 88 in W0, 40 of which had received an approval by the freeze, and 70 in W1, 37 of which had. Contract accounts closed 133,685 GMX~V2 positions in the observation window, but only 9,809 in its last six months and 6,805 from July to September~2026 (Table~\ref{tab:gmx-quarters} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-gmx}). Every trader result therefore rests on few contracts and has wide intervals.
\item Subsamples of the matched cohort ($n=1{,}000$, $2{,}000$, $5{,}000$ and $10{,}000$). Deterministic SHA256 subsamples (seed \texttt{contract-benchmark-v1}), followed by the full cohort, used only for runtime against size and for the sensitivity to sample definition (Section~\ref{sub:expanded-pool}). Each stage keeps the extracted edges incident to its sampled contracts, so its edge count grows with $n$.
\end{itemize}

At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the allowance graph has 6,221,665 nodes and 11,210,114 edges, and the transfer graph 7,884,602 nodes and 25,001,216 edges. The solver graphs of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} leave out 2,602 and 2,246 addresses whose pairs all carry a value weight of zero in floating point. On the same contracts the allowance graph has 44.8\% as many edges as the transfer graph, because an owner--spender pair is an allowance edge only while its latest allowance of one of the five tokens is positive, whereas every pair of addresses that exchanged a non-zero transfer of these tokens in the window is a transfer edge. The sampling is the same for both, so the differences between the scores trace back to their edges and restarts.

The five tokens hold most of the extracted rows: of the ego-network events of the plan's cohort in the observation window, the revised analysis keeps 224,954,202 approvals (57.7\%) and 1,149,168,538 transfers (74.7\%), and every kept transfer has a price. Table~\ref{tab:usd-tokens} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-tokens} gives these figures per token.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:implementation]{Implementation}

Every algorithm runs on one Python implementation of PageRank ($d=0.85$, $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$, $I_{\max}=300$). EndorseRank and AWP differ in their edge lists and restart vectors, and C-PR mixes its two edge lists node by node (Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition}). Each evaluation run writes its scores, per-proxy statistics and bootstrap intervals to one summary file, and the benchmark its timings to another. The tables of this chapter come from runs made on 10~October~2026 from 11:36~UTC, after the revision had been committed. At $d=0.85$ every solve converged well inside the iteration cap; at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ EndorseRank needed 69 iterations and AWP 92. Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} lists the runs, the repository revision, the configuration file and the summary files.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:benchmark-results]{Computational benchmark (Claim~C1)}

Claim~C1 concerns what each score costs to compute on the same input. Following the timing procedure of Section~\ref{sec:computational-benchmark}, Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} reports for each algorithm, on the full graphs of the matched cohort, the mean solve time and its standard deviation over five timed runs after one untimed warm-up, the peak memory of the warm-up solve, the number of iterations to convergence and the nodes and edges of the solver graph. Each graph and setting was timed once, so the same measurement reappears as the full-cohort row of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} and as the $d=0.85$ row of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}; Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runs} in Appendix~\ref{sec:benchmark-run-logs} lists every timed run.

\input{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}

EndorseRank solves the allowance graph in $10.29$\,s and AWP the transfer graph in $22.37$\,s, so AWP takes $2.17$ times as long, at an edge ratio of $2.23$. Assembly of the sparse matrix and each power iteration are both linear in $|E|$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}), and the measured ratio tracks the edge ratio, slightly below it, although AWP needs more iterations ($92$ against $69$). Peak memory likewise grows with $|E|$ ($879.7$\,MB against $1{,}712.6$\,MB), and the standard deviations over the five timed runs are $0.60$\,s and $1.83$\,s. Both scores use the same solver, so the difference in speed is a consequence of $|E|$, not of the algorithm.

The two hybrid rows show what the speed result covers and where it stops. C-PR walks on the union of both edge sets ($31{,}552{,}359$ edges, $9{,}151{,}010$ nodes) and needs $36.16$\,s, longer than AWP. It converges in fewer iterations ($79$ against $92$), but it has $1.26$ times AWP's edges and takes $1.62$ times as long. S-PR runs on the transfer layer and takes $34.42$\,s. Both its time and its iteration count include the solve of the allowance layer that supplies its restart vector: the $158$ iterations are $63$ for that solve plus $95$ for the seeded transfer walk. The low cost thus belongs to EndorseRank alone: any algorithm that queries the transfer layer, on its own or combined with allowance information, pays the cost of that query.

Two caveats attach to this result. The time covers only the solver call on edge lists built in advance; a full refresh would add parsing, decoding and edge-list construction, which all algorithms share wholly or in part. And a faster method is not necessarily a better one, so the runtime has to be read against the alignment and holdout results that follow.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:same-window-alignment]{Same-window alignment (Claim~C2)}

Claim~C2 asks what each score agrees with in the window it is computed from. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} reports the family-mean Kendall $\tau$ of every score on the transfer, allowance and Sybil-stability families, each with a 95\% paired-bootstrap confidence interval. The raw degrees have no row because in this window they are proxies: in-degree belongs to the transfer family and in-approve degree to the allowance family. Apart from the secondary part of rule~A, fixed in the analysis plan (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}) and applied here to the revised scores as a re-analysis, none of the comparisons below was fixed in advance (Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}).

\input{results/tables/alignment-hybrid.tex}

The scores divide by the edges they walk on. The two that walk on transfers alone, AWP and C-PR ($\lambda=0$), reach $0.572$ ($[0.564,0.580]$) and $0.659$ on the transfer family and $0.193$ and $0.225$ on the allowance family; S-PR, whose restarts come from the allowance walk, reaches $0.629$ and $0.323$. The two that walk on allowances alone, EndorseRank and C-PR ($\lambda=1$), reach $0.585$ ($[0.578,0.592]$) and $0.597$ on the allowance family and $0.158$ and $0.162$ on the transfer family, and the interior mixtures of C-PR lie between the two layers. On the Sybil-stability family the scores that walk on transfers lead, AWP with $0.395$ ($[0.387,0.403]$), while EndorseRank stays at $0.007$ ($[-0.001,0.015]$). Part of this agreement is built in. The main proxies of each family come from the same events as one of the graphs, in-degree and in-value from the transfers and in-approve degree and value from the latest allowances, and a PageRank smooths the in-degree of its own graph. Agreement with the proxies of a score's own edge type is therefore an intended-construct check and cannot count as an external test. The holdout of Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results} asks the same question out of window.

Appendix~\ref{sec:proxy-family-detail} breaks the family means of EndorseRank and AWP into their proxies. AWP's largest coefficient is with transfer in-degree ($\tau=0.578$, $[0.569,0.587]$); with in-value it is almost as high ($0.566$, $[0.558,0.575]$). That is what its weights lead one to expect: with the slope set at the median transfer (Section~\ref{sub:awp-graph}), $V$ is one half for a transfer of 200 dollars, $0.8$ for one of 400 dollars and $0.27$ for one of 100 dollars, so an edge weight grows with the amounts transferred as well as with their number. In the registered analysis, with $b=1$ on raw base units, $V$ counted nearly every transfer about once, and AWP followed the in-degree much more closely than the in-value (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}). It does not collapse to the in-degree either. Propagation, time weights and restarts at active senders shift a measurable share of pairwise orderings, and out of window AWP ranks new senders above the in-degree (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}). On the Sybil-stability family AWP follows tenure ($0.508$, $[0.499,0.517]$) and active months ($0.549$, $[0.540,0.558]$), and it is positive on the inbound-counterparty ratio ($0.127$, $[0.114,0.141]$).

EndorseRank follows the in-approve degree ($0.655$, $[0.648,0.662]$) more closely than the in-approve value ($0.515$, $[0.506,0.524]$). Its value weight is $0.8$ at twice the median allowance of 62 dollars, so an allowance of a few hundred dollars and an unlimited one weigh nearly alike, whereas the in-approve value counts each allowance up to the cap of 20,000 dollars. Because every cohort contract received approvals of the five tokens from at least three owners, EndorseRank leaves few ties here: it gives $13{,}625$ distinct scores to the $15{,}107$ contracts, and only $0.17\%$ of contract pairs are tied, most of them among the $535$ contracts that score zero. Its coefficients therefore rest on an ordering of nearly every contract, and keeping the contracts outside a graph as isolated nodes changes none of them (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}). C-PR ($\lambda=1$) walks the same allowance graph with capped amounts, also restarts uniformly, and stays within $0.02$ of EndorseRank on every family.

Claim~C2 is supported in a narrow sense. Every score that walks on one edge type agrees most with the proxies of that edge type, with an interval well clear of zero, and the mixtures of C-PR lie between their two layers on every family. The agreement is partly built in.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sec:coupled-results]{The coupled operator (RQ5)}

Two hybrids were defined in Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}. The coupled operator C-PR mixes the US-dollar allowance and transfer layers in each node's transition row with weight $\lambda$, and the endorsement-seeded walk S-PR keeps the transfer layer but restarts from the allowance walk. Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} gives their paired contrasts in this window: first the eight of Section~\ref{sec:statistical-tests} against the two layers walked alone, then four against EndorseRank and AWP. RQ5 is decided by the out-of-window tests in Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results}. Here we only locate the hybrids on the checks that the single-layer scores were built to pass.

\input{results/tables/tau-diff-hybrid.tex}

C-PR lies between its two layers on every family and stays nearer to its allowance layer. At the primary setting it reaches $0.477$ ($[0.469,0.485]$) on the allowance family, $0.120$ below C-PR ($\lambda=1$) ($[-0.127,-0.113]$), and $0.365$ ($[0.355,0.375]$) on the transfer family, $0.294$ below C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($[-0.305,-0.284]$). As $\lambda$ rises from $0.25$ to $0.75$ it moves toward the allowance layer on all three families. AWP ranks the transfer family better than C-PR ($-0.207$, $[-0.220,-0.194]$), and EndorseRank the allowance family ($-0.108$, $[-0.115,-0.101]$). A node with out-edges in only one layer follows that layer whatever $\lambda$ is (Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition}); $\lambda$ acts only at the nodes with out-edges in both layers. Almost every node of the allowance layer is an owner with out-edges, so the allowance rows enter the walk wherever an owner also sent a transfer that the extraction kept. On the Sybil-stability family C-PR exceeds its allowance layer ($+0.131$, $[0.126,0.137]$) and lies below its transfer layer ($-0.262$, $[-0.273,-0.253]$).

On every family, S-PR falls below the layer that leads that family. Restarting the transfer walk from the allowance walk drops the transfer coefficient to $0.629$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.031$, $[-0.035,-0.028]$, against C-PR ($\lambda=0$)), raises the allowance coefficient only as far as $0.323$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.274$, $[-0.285,-0.263]$, against C-PR ($\lambda=1$)), and leaves Sybil-adjusted stability slightly below the transfer layer ($-0.007$, $[-0.009,-0.004]$). A restart vector changes where the walk lands after each teleport. It carries part of the allowance signal into the transfer walk, but less than walking the allowance edges does. This is the same-window counterpart of the finding that Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results} reaches out of window.

As fixed in the analysis plan, the secondary part of rule~A asks C-PR's point estimate to lie within the 95\% interval of the better layer on the transfer and allowance families, and C-PR to exceed both layers on Sybil-adjusted stability. At $\lambda=0.5$, C-PR fails all three parts. Its transfer coefficient ($0.365$) lies below the interval of C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($[0.653,0.666]$), its allowance coefficient ($0.477$) lies below that of C-PR ($\lambda=1$) ($[0.590,0.604]$), and on Sybil-adjusted stability it exceeds only the allowance layer, so the secondary part fails. The paired contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}, reported alongside the rule as a stricter check, point the same way. In the same-window tables the mixture gives up part of each layer's agreement with its own family and exceeds the better layer on no family. The holdout takes up the remaining question of whether the mixture has any predictive content that the single layers lack.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:rank-divergence]{Rank divergence (H1 / RQ1)}

Hypothesis~H1 and RQ1 concern whether EndorseRank and AWP put the same contracts in different orders. The inter-method Kendall $\tau_b$ between the two score vectors is $0.174$, with interval $[0.163,0.185]$ (last row of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}), so the interval excludes both zero and one. The two rankings are related, yet far from interchangeable. Of the $114{,}103{,}171$ contract pairs, $96.7\%$ are ordered by both scores, and $41.2\%$ of those pairs are discordant.

The marginal score distributions are in Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}, and ties play almost no part in them: EndorseRank ties $0.17\%$ of the contract pairs, and AWP, with $12{,}435$ distinct scores, $3.13\%$. Under each score the largest tie block is formed by the contracts that score zero: $535$ under EndorseRank and $2{,}672$ under AWP, that is, the $523$ and $2{,}670$ contracts outside each graph and $12$ and $2$ whose only pairs carry amounts too small for the value weight to differ from zero. Under EndorseRank $166$ further contracts share the score $c=7.64\times10^{-8}$ of a node without in-edges; they are in the allowance graph only as owners, and every other cohort contract has received a positive allowance and scores above $c$. A marginal distribution cannot show how two scores order the same contracts, so Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter} plots the two rankings against each other for a random sample of $3{,}000$ contracts, with tied contracts at their mid-rank as in $\tau_b$. The zero blocks appear as a column at the right edge (EndorseRank) and a row at the top (AWP). Contracts that both scores rank near the top, or both near the bottom, lie close to the identity line; in between, the points spread over most of the square.

The zero blocks come from one rule of this pipeline: a contract missing from a graph scores zero (Section~\ref{sec:graph-construction}). Nothing in the graph favors the zero rule over keeping the missing contracts as isolated nodes, and the analysis plan fixed that alternative as a sensitivity analysis. Under uniform teleportation an isolated node scores like any node without in-edges, so the $523$ contracts outside the allowance graph receive $c$, the lowest score that any node of the graph can have, and join the $166$ contracts at $c$ near the bottom of the order. The $12$ contracts whose only pairs carry zero weight are nodes of the graph that the solver leaves out, so they keep the score zero. No pair changes direction: the pairs between the $523$ and the $166$ become ties, and those between the $523$ and the $12$ become ordered. AWP's $2{,}670$ missing contracts were handled the same way; under AWP's restarts an isolated contract that has sent nothing receives no restart mass, so it scores zero either way. Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} gives the result. No coefficient changes at the third decimal, and the agreement between the two scores stays at $0.174$ ($[0.163,0.185]$). Because the cohort was defined by approvals received, nearly every cohort contract is a spender in the allowance graph, and the rule for missing contracts decides almost nothing.

A protocol that relies on transfer-based scores cannot assume that its ordering of contracts survives a switch to allowance-based scores or a blend with them.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:robustness-results]{Robustness and sensitivity checks}

Three checks, run for EndorseRank and AWP, ask whether the results hinge on one damping value, one slope of the value weight or one sample definition. The value-slope check takes the place of the plan's token check, which lost its point once every graph keeps five tokens (Section~\ref{sub:top-tokens}). The first two hold the sample fixed; the third varies the sample definition itself, so its coefficients are expected to move, and it is not evidence of robustness. A fourth check asks how much EndorseRank depends on its restart rule.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:damping]{Damping}

Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping} recomputes the family means at $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$ on the same graphs.

\input{results/tables/robustness-damping.tex}

For EndorseRank no family mean moves by more than $0.003$ across the three values. For AWP the transfer coefficient rises from $0.570$ to $0.577$ as $d$ rises, and the Sybil-stability coefficient falls from $0.397$ to $0.391$. Neither score's pattern is an artifact of the conventional $d=0.85$. EndorseRank's stability is largely structural. For a spender whose owners receive no approvals, EndorseRank equals $c\,(1+d\,\delta(v))$, with $\delta(v)$ the owner-normalized in-strength (Section~\ref{sub:theory-closed-form}), and the order that this formula gives among such spenders does not depend on $d$. At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the formula holds within 1\% for all $12{,}039$ such spenders among the $14{,}406$ cohort contracts with in-edges, so the rank correlations can hardly move. Runtime grows with the number of iterations, which rises with $d$: EndorseRank takes $5.98$, $10.29$ and $31.23$\,s ($41$, $69$ and $217$ iterations) and AWP $15.24$, $22.37$ and $60.96$\,s ($55$, $92$ and $261$), time ratios of $2.55$, $2.17$ and $1.95$ at an edge ratio of $2.23$. Each graph--damping pair was timed once. Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity} plots the family means.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:sample-size]{Sample-definition sensitivity}

Table~\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size} recomputes the family means on the subsamples of the matched cohort from the runtime scaling, each scored on the edges incident to its contracts.

\input{results/tables/robustness-sample-size.tex}

EndorseRank moves most: its allowance coefficient is $0.715$ at $n=1{,}000$ and falls with every stage to $0.585$ on the full cohort, and its transfer coefficient falls from $0.205$ to $0.158$. \dissertationSubsubheading[sub:endorserank-restarts]{Restarts of EndorseRank}

EndorseRank restarts the walk uniformly, while AWP restarts it at addresses in proportion to their recent sending activity (Section~\ref{sub:endorserank-graph}). Carried over to allowances, AWP's rule restarts the walk at owners in proportion to the spenders they approved, each weighted by the age of the latest approval. The analysis plan fixed uniform restarts for EndorseRank and named the variant as a score to report beside it, without fixing any comparison between the two. Table~\ref{tab:endorserank-restarts} scores both versions of EndorseRank, with AWP beside them.

With AWP's restarts, EndorseRank's same-window allowance coefficient falls from $0.585$ to $0.426$, and its transfer coefficient changes little ($0.164$ against $0.158$). The restart mass then goes to the owners that approve most, so the score weights a spender by how active its owners are as well as by how many there are. Out of window the variant ranks both spender labels higher in both windows. In W0 it reaches $0.308$ on new approval pairs and $0.269$ on new senders, $+0.077$ ($[0.071,0.084]$) and $+0.070$ ($[0.064,0.076]$) above the uniform version, and in W1 $0.295$ and $0.242$, $+0.061$ ($[0.054,0.067]$) and $+0.048$ ($[0.042,0.053]$) above it (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-posthoc}). Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} gives the paired contrasts, first the two of rule~A, fixed in the analysis plan before any log of the study was extracted, and then those computed after the labels were known.

The first finding answers the second part of RQ4, and the answer differs between the two edge types. On the transfer side the walk improves on the raw degree: AWP ranks new transfer senders at $\tau=0.346$ ($[0.336,0.356]$), above the transfer in-degree at $t_1$ ($0.303$, $[0.292,0.315]$), a paired difference of $+0.043$ (interval $[0.038,0.048]$). Coverage does not explain the degree comparisons. Of the 17,050 spenders, 2,891 have no transfer edge up to the freeze and score zero under AWP. They gained new approval pairs slightly more often than the rest (13.3\% against 11.9\%) and new senders almost never (0.6\% against 11.9\%), so AWP's zeros lower its coefficient on the first label and raise it on the second. Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving} restricts the holdout to the 14,094 spenders that had received a transfer from another address before the freeze. There AWP still ranks new senders above the transfer in-degree ($0.357$ against $0.301$; $+0.056$, $[0.049,0.064]$), and EndorseRank stays below the in-approve degree on new approval pairs ($0.236$ against $0.249$; $-0.013$, $[-0.020,-0.006]$). The restriction was chosen after the results were known.

The second finding is that the coupled operator fails rule~A, although in an exploratory comparison it improves on its transfer layer walked alone. That rule, applied here to the revised scores as a re-analysis, asked C-PR to do no worse than C-PR ($\lambda=1$) on new approval pairs and to beat C-PR ($\lambda=0$) on new transfer senders. At $\lambda=0.5$, C-PR lies above the allowance walk on new approval pairs ($\Delta\tau=+0.029$, interval $[0.022,0.036]$), so the first condition holds, and below the transfer walk on new transfer senders ($\Delta\tau=-0.034$, interval $[-0.040,-0.029]$), so the second fails, and with it the primary part. Against the transfer walk alone, a comparison the rule did not include, C-PR does better. On new approval pairs C-PR reaches $0.252$ against $0.160$, a paired difference of $+0.092$ (interval $[0.080,0.105]$, on the same resamples). This contrast was computed after the labels were known; in the registered analysis the same contrast became the hypothesis F1 of the registration of W1 (Section~\ref{sub:fresh-results}). Against EndorseRank and AWP, also computed after the labels were known, C-PR lies above both on new approval pairs, $0.021$ above EndorseRank ($[0.013,0.028]$) and $0.056$ above AWP ($[0.042,0.070]$), and it falls well behind AWP on new senders ($-0.085$, $[-0.092,-0.078]$). On the W0 data the analogue of F2 would therefore fail against AWP. Section~\ref{sub:fresh-results} reports that comparison next to the decision of W1. Across the three values of $\lambda$, C-PR is flat on new approval pairs ($0.253$ at $\lambda=0.25$, $0.249$ at $\lambda=0.75$), and its point estimate on new transfer senders falls as $\lambda$ rises, from $0.269$ to $0.253$ (no paired contrast was computed, and $\lambda$ was not tuned).

The seeded ablation shows where the allowance signal has to sit. S-PR matches the transfer walk on the flow label ($\Delta\tau=-0.001$, interval $[-0.004,0.002]$) and falls below the allowance walk on the endorsement label ($\Delta\tau=-0.051$, $[-0.066,-0.038]$), ranking new approval pairs only a little above the transfer walk ($0.172$ against $0.160$). When transfer edges decide where the walk goes, a restart distribution derived from endorsements changes little. On the spender holdout, the allowance signal survives when allowance edges carry the walk, fully in the allowance walk and mixed in C-PR, and most of it is lost when they only decide where the walk restarts.

Two caveats apply to the whole table. Each label is the same construction carried into the future (new approvals, new senders), so none of these spender results bears on credit or loss; the trader labels below are built from neither edge type but are not credit labels either. Both labels are also sparse. Of the 17,050 spenders, 2,070 gained a new approval pair and 1,695 a new sender; every coefficient rests largely on how these subsets are ordered.

Other candidate labels were computed on the holdout, and they are reported here without being offered as support. Revocation counts and the drain heuristic both rise with EndorseRank ($\tau=0.337$, interval $[0.327,0.347]$, and $0.234$, interval $[0.224,0.245]$) and with every other score, and revocations rise most steeply with the raw in-approve degree ($0.388$, $[0.376,0.398]$). For an endorsement reading the sign is wrong, since a trusted spender should lose approvals less often, not more. The likelier reading is volume. A spender with many owners sees more of everything owners do, revocations and drains included, so these labels cannot separate trust from use.

Most of C-PR's loss to AWP on new senders therefore comes from its weights and restarts, not from the coupling.

Superiority on new approval pairs holds in both windows, but non-inferiority within $0.01$ fails on new senders, whose lower bounds are $-0.019$ and $-0.021$, and on the traders' liquidation-free close rate ($+0.021$ $[-0.016,0.074]$ in W0 and $+0.025$ $[-0.023,0.082]$ in W1). C-AWP was defined after the labels were known, from two walks whose rankings of these labels had been seen. It is the coupling to register on a later window, not a finding that a walk over both edge types improves on AWP.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:empirical-claims]{Claims (C1--C6)}

The chapter's results support the claims below, cross-referenced to the hypothesis and research question columns of Table~\ref{tab:hrq-claim-map}; C1--C5 are the primary claims, and C6 rests on the comparison on outcomes built from neither edge type. The verdicts of rules~A and~B in C5 and of R1--R3 in C6 apply the registered rules to the revised scores, as a re-analysis.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[C1.] On identical contract sets, the cost of each score's solver call follows the number of edges in its graph, because both matrix assembly and iteration scale with $|E|$. The allowance graph has 44.8\% of the transfer graph's edges, and AWP takes $2.17$ times as long as EndorseRank at an edge ratio of $2.23$ (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}). On the subsamples the time ratio follows the edge ratio only from 10,000 contracts on, and the stages do not test edge counts beyond those of the cohort graph (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling}). The low cost belongs to the sparser graph, not to the algorithm: C-PR and S-PR walk the transfer layer and take longer than AWP.
\item[C2.] In the same window each score agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on, EndorseRank with the allowance family ($0.585$, $[0.578,0.592]$) and AWP with the transfer family ($0.572$, $[0.564,0.580]$), and the mixtures of C-PR lie between their two layers (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}). These tests measure internal consistency, and part of the agreement is built in; external validity is outside their scope. EndorseRank leaves few ties on the cohort, and the rule for contracts outside a graph changes none of its coefficients (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).
\item[C3.] EndorseRank and AWP order the cohort differently. Their agreement, $\tau_b=0.174$ ($[0.163,0.185]$), has an interval that excludes zero and one; $96.7\%$ of the contract pairs are ordered by both scores, and $41.2\%$ of those are discordant. The divergence does not come from ties or from the rule for contracts outside a graph (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}, Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}).
\item[C4.] Out of window the two walks differ against the raw degrees they smooth. AWP ranks later new transfer senders above the transfer in-degree in both windows (W0 $+0.043$, $[0.038,0.048]$; W1 $+0.022$, $[0.017,0.027]$), while EndorseRank ranks later new approval pairs below the in-approve degree in both (W0 $-0.019$, $[-0.026,-0.011]$; W1 $-0.034$, $[-0.038,-0.029]$) (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:fresh-posthoc}); neither comparison is a matter of coverage (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}). On the contract traders of W0 the walks over transfers rank counts of later profitable closes, and no score ranks the share of closes that were profitable (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-traders}). None of these comparisons was fixed in advance.
\item[C5.] A single random walk over both edge types does not beat its two layers walked alone. Rule~A fails: C-PR is no worse than the allowance walk on new approval pairs ($+0.029$, $[0.022,0.036]$) but worse than the transfer walk on new senders ($-0.034$, $[-0.040,-0.029]$), and its same-window part fails as well (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}). Rule~B is not met (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}): the W0 gain over the transfer walk on new approval pairs, found after the labels were known, recurs in W1 (F1, $+0.110$, $[0.098,0.122]$), but C-PR falls below the transfer walk on new senders by more than the margin of $0.01$ (F2, $-0.028$, $[-0.033,-0.023]$), and F3 fails narrowly ($+0.000$, $[-0.014,0.010]$). Against AWP, in the first sensitivity analysis, F2 fails clearly ($-0.067$, $[-0.072,-0.061]$). S-PR is level with the transfer walk on new senders and below the allowance walk on new approval pairs, so allowance edges help only when they carry the walk; Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results} reports where the hybrids stand within the window.
\item[C6.] On later liquidation avoidance of the GMX~V2 contract traders, the one outcome built from neither edge type that bears on counterparty risk, the approval count at the freeze does not lead the transfer count in the primary window W1 (P $=+0.084$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.026,0.188]$), although P is positive in W0 ($+0.182$, 97.5\% interval $[0.032,0.338]$). The PageRank versions are not resolved (Q $=-0.041$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.148,0.064]$). The approval count ranks traders lower than the transfer count on the shares of closes that were profitable or not losing, so, in the wording of R3, the signal concerns forced-liquidation avoidance, not trading success. The samples are small, 70 and 88 contract traders, and every interval is wide (Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}).
\end{enumerate}

The rank divergence answers RQ1, the same-window tables RQ2, the benchmark tables RQ3, and the holdouts RQ4 and RQ5; C6 adds no research question. The direct comparisons between EndorseRank and AWP are in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp} and Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-contrasts}, and none of them carries a claim of the thesis. Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} reads these findings against the research questions and objectives of Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}.
