\chapter{Implementation and Empirical Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter runs the design of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} on Arbitrum One as a single case study: one chain and one observation window, one matched cohort of contracts for every same-window comparison, spender and trader cohorts for the two holdout windows W0 and W1, and subsamples of the matched cohort for runtime against size. It reports the revised analysis of Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}, in which every amount is valued in US dollars and the cohort, the graphs, the proxies and the spender labels keep the five tokens WETH, USDC, USDT0, ARB and WBTC. The revision was decided after the registered results of the same window, W0 and W1 were known, so no result in this chapter is a registered result. Rule~A was fixed in the analysis plan of 9~October~2026 before any log was extracted; rule~B and the comparison on outcomes built from neither edge type were registered after the W0 results of the registered analysis were known and before the logs of W1 were extracted. This chapter applies these rules, with their thresholds and wording, to the revised scores: rule~A on W0 and in the same window, rule~B on W1, and the comparison on W1 with W0 as its secondary window (Sections~\ref{sub:fresh-results} and~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}). Their verdicts here are a re-analysis, not registered tests; the registered analysis reached the same verdicts (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}, Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}). Every table is generated from the evaluation summaries of Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}, each Kendall $\tau$ comes with its 95\% confidence interval except in the value-slope and sample-definition checks, which were computed without intervals, and each PageRank is read against the raw degree it smooths. Each section states which of its comparisons the plan or the registration named.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:dataset]{Case study setting: Arbitrum One}

Arbitrum One was chosen because it is an Ethereum rollup on which many DeFi protocols run (Section~\ref{sec:ethereum-l2}), because its logs are in the public BigQuery export that the pipeline reads, and because one of its margin-trading protocols, GMX~V2, records the position closes behind the trader labels. The observation window runs from 1~October~2023 to 30~June~2026 (33 months), and the time decay is anchored at its end, $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$. An event at the anchor has time weight $0.858$; the weight falls to $0.5$ at 180~days and is close to zero for events more than two years old (Figure~\ref{fig:logistic-decay-methods}). Events from the early months still create edges, but the last year carries most of the edge weight in AWP and EndorseRank.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:cohorts]{Cohorts}

Four sets of contracts are scored or evaluated. Every address in them holds contract code; externally owned accounts (EOAs) enter the graphs as owners, senders and recipients and are never scored.

\begin{itemize}
\item Matched cohort ($n=15{,}107$), defined in Section~\ref{sec:data-sources}: the contracts that received a non-zero \texttt{Approval} of one of the five tokens from at least three distinct owners. They are part of the plan's cohort of 27,844 contracts, which applied the same rule to the approvals of every token; its other 12,737 contracts do not meet the rule on the five tokens. Of the plan's 29,022 candidate spenders, \texttt{eth\_getCode} at block 513,216,506 (9~October~2026) returned contract code for 27,844, no code for 1,121 and an EIP-7702 delegation designator \parencite{buterin2024eip7702} for 57; the last two groups are EOAs and were left out. Membership does not depend on either graph at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$. Of the cohort contracts, 523 are outside the allowance graph, because by the end of the window the latest \texttt{Approval} of every owner and token that had approved them carried the value zero and they held no positive allowance of the five tokens as owners. The transfer graph leaves out 2,670, because no non-zero transfer of the five tokens in the window names them as sender or recipient (a spender that moves tokens with \texttt{transferFrom} from an owner directly to a third address does not appear in the resulting \texttt{Transfer} event), and 80 contracts are outside both graphs. A contract absent from a graph scores zero under that graph's method. Every same-window alignment, difference test and benchmark is computed on this cohort.
\item Spender cohorts of the two holdout windows. In W0 the scores are frozen at $t_1$ (31~March~2026, 23:59:59~UTC) and the labels are counted from 1~April to 30~June~2026. The cohort is every contract spender that holds at least one positive latest allowance of one of the five tokens at $t_1$ in the extracted approval data. Of the 17,167 such spenders, 17,050 hold contract code, 104 have no code and 13 carry an EIP-7702 designator; the 117 EOAs are left out. In W1 the scores are frozen at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (30~June~2026, 23:59:59~UTC), the labels are counted from 1~July to 30~September~2026, and the same rule gives 18,152 contract spenders. The spender cohorts are not subsets of the matched cohort. The extracted approvals include those that cohort contracts grant as owners, so the contracts they approve enter as spenders, and a spender needs only one owner with a positive allowance.
\item Trader cohorts. The GMX~V2 accounts that hold contract code, are nodes of the freeze-date graph (either layer) and close at least three positions in the label window: 88 in W0, 40 of which had received an approval by the freeze, and 70 in W1, 37 of which had. Contract accounts closed 133,685 GMX~V2 positions in the observation window, 6,473 of them by liquidation, with 88,002 closes in 2025 against 9,809 in the first half of 2026, and 6,805 from July to September~2026, 182 of them by liquidation. Every trader result therefore rests on few contracts and has wide intervals.
\item Subsamples of the matched cohort. Deterministic SHA256 subsamples (seed \texttt{contract-benchmark-v1}) with $n=1{,}000$, $2{,}000$, $5{,}000$ and $10{,}000$ contracts, followed by the full cohort of 15,107 (Section~\ref{sub:expanded-pool}). Each stage keeps the extracted edges incident to its sampled contracts, so its edge count grows with $n$, from 424,888 allowance and 1,692,513 transfer edges at $n=1{,}000$ to the full graphs. The stages serve the runtime against size (Section~\ref{sub:runtime-scaling}) and the sensitivity to the sample definition (Section~\ref{sub:sample-size}).
\end{itemize}

The five tokens hold most of the extracted rows. Of the ego events of the plan's cohort in the observation window, the revised analysis keeps 224,954,202 approvals (57.7\%) and 1,149,168,538 transfers (74.7\%). It leaves out 164,202,859 approvals and 364,468,917 transfers of other tokens, and 709,821 approvals and 24,766,612 transfers of the five tokens in which no contract of the revised cohort takes part; every kept transfer has a price. At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, 5,763,757 of the 12,590,613 positive latest allowances (45.8\%) are unlimited, and 6,776,289 (53.8\%) reach the cap $U$ of the allowance layer of C-PR. On 38 sampled dates the DefiLlama prices differ from the Chainlink feeds read on the chain by a median of 0.01\% to 0.08\% per token and by at most 0.72\%. Table~\ref{tab:usd-tokens} in Appendix~\ref{sec:data-tokens} gives these figures per token, with the addresses and the feeds.

At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the allowance graph has 6,221,665 nodes and 11,210,114 edges, and the transfer graph 7,884,602 nodes and 25,001,216 edges; at $t_1$ the two pair tables hold 10,650,258 allowance and 23,680,692 transfer pairs. The edge counts at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ are those of pairs with a positive EndorseRank or AWP weight. The node counts also include 2,602 and 2,246 addresses whose only pairs carry amounts so small that the value weight is zero in floating-point arithmetic; the solver graphs of EndorseRank and AWP leave them out (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}). On the same contracts the transfer graph thus has 2.23 times as many edges as the allowance graph, which has 44.8\% of the transfer graph's edge count. An owner--spender pair is an allowance edge only while its latest allowance of one of the five tokens is positive, whereas every pair of addresses that exchanged a non-zero transfer of these tokens in the window is a transfer edge. Of the allowance graph's nodes, 6,208,769 are owners with at least one positive allowance, so that graph consists almost entirely of owners pointing at a much smaller set of spenders. Both graphs come from the same extraction, the ego network of the revised cohort, so the differences between the scores trace back to their edges and restarts.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:implementation]{Implementation}

Every algorithm runs on one Python implementation of PageRank ($d=0.85$, $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ in the $L_1$ norm, $I_{\max}=300$). EndorseRank and AWP differ in their edge lists and restart vectors, and C-PR mixes its two edge lists node by node (Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition}). Each evaluation run writes its scores, per-proxy statistics and bootstrap intervals to one summary file, and the benchmark writes its timings to a file of its own. The registered analysis computed its same-window and W0 results after the observation window had been extracted and before W1 was registered, and its W1 results after the July--September logs had been extracted. Every result of the revised analysis was computed on 10~October~2026, from 11:36~UTC, after the revision had been committed and after the registered results of all three windows were known (Section~\ref{sec:amount-revision}). At $d=0.85$ every solve converged well inside the iteration cap: at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ EndorseRank needed 69 iterations, AWP 92, C-PR at $\lambda=0.5$ 79 and C-PR ($\lambda=0$) 92, and the longest single solve, the seeded transfer walk of S-PR, 95 (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-results}). Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} lists the runs, the repository revision, the configuration file, the summary files and the machine on which the benchmark was timed.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:benchmark-results]{Computational benchmark (Claim~C1)}

Claim~C1 concerns what each score costs to compute on the same input. Following the timing procedure of Section~\ref{sec:computational-benchmark}, Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} reports for each algorithm, on the full graphs of the matched cohort at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the mean solve time and its standard deviation over five timed runs after one untimed warm-up, the peak memory of the warm-up solve, the number of iterations, and the nodes and edges of the solver graph. Each graph and setting was timed once. The same measurement reappears as the full-cohort row of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} and as the $d=0.85$ row of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}, and Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runs} in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} lists every timed run.

\IfFileExists{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}{\input{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}}{}

EndorseRank solves the allowance graph (6,219,063 nodes, 11,210,114 edges) in 10.29~s on average and AWP solves the transfer graph (7,882,356 nodes, 25,001,216 edges) in 22.37~s. AWP thus takes 2.17 times as long as EndorseRank, at an edge ratio of 2.23. Assembling the sparse matrix and running each power iteration are both linear in $|E|$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}), and the time ratio stays close to the edge ratio, slightly below it, although AWP needs more iterations (92 against 69, as in the evaluation runs at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$). Peak memory is 879.7~MB for EndorseRank and 1,712.6~MB for AWP, and the standard deviations over the five timed runs are 0.60~s and 1.83~s. The speed is a consequence of $|E|$: on the same contracts the allowance graph has 44.8\% of the transfer graph's edges, and both scores use the same solver.

The two hybrid rows show what the speed result covers and where it stops. C-PR walks on the union of both edge sets (9,151,010 nodes, 31,552,359 edges) and needs 36.16~s, 1.62 times as long as AWP on 1.26 times its edges. S-PR runs on the transfer layer (7,884,602 nodes, 25,018,892 edges) and takes 34.42~s, 1.54 times as long as AWP. Both its time and its iteration count include the solve of the allowance layer that supplies its restart vector; in the evaluation run at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ its 158 iterations are 63 for that solve and 95 for the seeded transfer walk. The lower cost is thus confined to the score that walks the allowance graph alone and comes from that graph's size: any algorithm that walks the transfer layer, on its own or combined with allowance information, pays for the edges of that layer.

Two caveats attach to this result. The time covers only the solver call on edge lists built in advance; a full refresh would add parsing, decoding and edge-list construction, which all algorithms share wholly or in part. And a faster method is not necessarily a better one, so the runtime has to be read against the alignment and holdout results that follow.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:same-window-alignment]{Same-window alignment (Claim~C2)}

Claim~C2 asks what each score agrees with in the window it is computed from. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} reports the family-mean Kendall $\tau$ of every score on the transfer, allowance and Sybil-stability families, each with a 95\% paired-bootstrap interval. The raw degrees have no row because in this window they are proxies: in-degree belongs to the transfer family and in-approve degree to the allowance family. The secondary part of rule~A (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}) is the only same-window comparison that the plan fixed; here it is applied to the revised scores, after the registered results were known, so its verdict is a re-analysis. The paired contrasts below were not named in the plan and were computed together with the same-window results.

\input{results/tables/alignment-hybrid.tex}

The scores divide by the edges they walk on. EndorseRank reaches $0.585$ $[0.578,0.592]$ on the allowance family and $0.158$ $[0.147,0.169]$ on the transfer family; AWP reaches $0.572$ $[0.564,0.580]$ on the transfer family and $0.193$ $[0.182,0.203]$ on the allowance family. Each score is ahead of the other on the family of its own edges, EndorseRank by $+0.392$ $[0.380,0.405]$ on the allowance family and AWP by $+0.414$ $[0.401,0.427]$ on the transfer family (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff} in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp}). The two layers of C-PR walked alone repeat the split: C-PR ($\lambda=1$) reaches $0.597$ $[0.590,0.604]$ on the allowance family and C-PR ($\lambda=0$) $0.659$ $[0.653,0.666]$ on the transfer family. On the Sybil-stability family the scores that walk on transfers lead, AWP with $0.395$ $[0.387,0.403]$ against $0.007$ $[-0.001,0.015]$ for EndorseRank. Part of this agreement is built in. The main proxies of each family come from the same events as one of the graphs, in-degree and US-dollar in-value from the transfers of the five tokens and in-approve degree and capped value from their latest allowances, and a PageRank smooths the in-degree of its own graph. Agreement with the proxies of a score's own edge type is therefore an intended-construct check and cannot count as an external test. The holdouts of Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results} ask the same question out of window.

Appendix~\ref{sec:proxy-family-detail} breaks the family means of EndorseRank and AWP into their proxies. AWP follows the summed US-dollar value of the inbound transfers ($0.566$ $[0.558,0.575]$) almost as closely as their number of senders, the in-degree ($0.578$ $[0.569,0.587]$). That is what its weights lead one to expect. With the slope set at the median transfer (Section~\ref{sub:awp-graph}), a transfer of 200 dollars weighs one half, one of 400 dollars $0.8$ and one of 100 dollars $0.27$, so an edge weight grows with the amounts transferred as well as with their number. In the registered analysis, with $b=1$ on raw base units, $V$ was close to one for nearly every transfer whatever its amount, and AWP followed the in-degree ($0.541$ $[0.534,0.547]$) much more closely than the in-value ($0.353$ $[0.345,0.361]$; registered results digest, Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}). On the Sybil-stability family AWP follows transfer tenure ($0.508$ $[0.499,0.517]$) and active months ($0.549$ $[0.540,0.558]$) and is positive on the inbound-counterparty ratio ($0.127$ $[0.114,0.141]$).

EndorseRank follows the in-approve degree ($0.655$ $[0.648,0.662]$) more closely than the in-approve value ($0.515$ $[0.506,0.524]$). Its value weight is $0.8$ at twice the median allowance of 62 dollars and close to one at a few hundred dollars, so such an allowance and an unlimited one weigh nearly alike, whereas the in-approve value counts each allowance up to the cap of 20,000 dollars. Its transfer coefficients are $0.182$ $[0.169,0.194]$ with in-degree and $0.134$ $[0.123,0.145]$ with in-value. On the Sybil-stability family it is negative on the inbound-counterparty ratio ($-0.156$ $[-0.166,-0.147]$) and reaches $0.080$ $[0.069,0.091]$ on tenure and $0.098$ $[0.087,0.109]$ on active months, so how many owners approve a contract says little about how long it has received transfers. C-PR ($\lambda=1$) walks the same allowance graph with capped US-dollar amounts and also restarts uniformly; it stays close to EndorseRank on every family, and its point estimate on the in-approve value, which sums the same capped amounts as its own layer does, is higher ($0.540$ $[0.531,0.549]$ against $0.515$ $[0.506,0.524]$; no paired contrast was computed).

Because every contract in the cohort received approvals of the five tokens from at least three owners, EndorseRank leaves few ties here. It gives 13,625 distinct scores to the 15,107 contracts, and only 0.17\% of contract pairs are tied, most of them among the 535 contracts that score zero (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}). Its coefficients therefore rest on an ordering of nearly every contract, and keeping the contracts outside a graph as isolated nodes changes none of them (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).

Claim~C2 is supported as an intended-construct check, with agreement that is partly built in. Every score agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on, with intervals far from zero, and the mixtures of C-PR lie between their two layers on every family (Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results}).

\dissertationSubsubheading[sec:coupled-results]{The coupled operator (RQ5)}

Two hybrids were defined in Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}. The coupled operator C-PR mixes the US-dollar allowance and transfer layers in each node's transition row with weight $\lambda$, and the endorsement-seeded walk S-PR keeps the transfer layer but restarts from the allowance walk. Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} gives their paired contrasts in this window: first eight against the two layers walked alone, then four against EndorseRank and AWP. RQ5 is decided out of window (Sections~\ref{sec:holdout-results} and~\ref{sub:fresh-results}). Here we only locate the hybrids on the checks that the single-layer scores were built to pass.

\input{results/tables/tau-diff-hybrid.tex}

C-PR lies between its two layers on every family. At $\lambda=0.5$ it reaches $0.365$ $[0.355,0.375]$ on the transfer family, $0.477$ $[0.469,0.485]$ on the allowance family and $0.142$ $[0.134,0.151]$ on the Sybil-stability family, and as $\lambda$ rises from $0.25$ to $0.75$ it moves toward the allowance layer on all three (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}). It stays nearer to its allowance layer than to its transfer layer: on the allowance family it falls $0.120$ below C-PR ($\lambda=1$) ($[-0.127,-0.113]$), and on the transfer family $0.294$ below C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($[-0.305,-0.284]$). The mixing rule of Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition} acts at every node with out-edges in both layers. Almost every node of the allowance layer is an owner with out-edges, so the allowance rows enter the walk wherever an owner also sent a transfer that the extraction kept. Against the single-layer scores C-PR is below AWP on the transfer family ($-0.207$, $[-0.220,-0.194]$) and below EndorseRank on the allowance family ($-0.108$, $[-0.115,-0.101]$). On the Sybil-stability family it lies above the allowance walk ($+0.131$, $[0.126,0.137]$) and EndorseRank ($+0.135$, $[0.129,0.141]$) and below the transfer walk ($-0.262$, $[-0.273,-0.253]$) and AWP ($-0.253$, $[-0.263,-0.242]$).

On every family S-PR falls below the single layer that leads that family. Restarting the transfer walk from the allowance walk lowers the transfer coefficient to $0.629$ $[0.621,0.636]$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.031$, $[-0.035,-0.028]$, against C-PR ($\lambda=0$)) and the Sybil-stability coefficient to $0.398$ $[0.390,0.406]$ ($-0.007$, $[-0.009,-0.004]$). It raises the allowance coefficient to $0.323$ $[0.313,0.333]$, against $0.225$ $[0.214,0.235]$ for the transfer walk with uniform restarts (no paired contrast was computed for this pair), yet leaves it $0.274$ below the allowance walk ($[-0.285,-0.263]$). A restart vector changes where the walk lands after each teleport. It carries part of the allowance signal into the transfer walk, but less than walking the allowance edges does.

As fixed in the analysis plan, the secondary part of rule~A asks C-PR's point estimate at $\lambda=0.5$ to lie within the 95\% interval of the better layer on the transfer and allowance families, and C-PR to exceed both layers on the Sybil-stability family. Applied to the revised scores, all three parts fail, as they did in the registered analysis (Table~\ref{tab:registered-vs-revised}). The transfer coefficient ($0.365$) lies below the interval of C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($[0.653,0.666]$), the allowance coefficient ($0.477$) lies below that of C-PR ($\lambda=1$) ($[0.590,0.604]$), and on the Sybil-stability family C-PR exceeds only the allowance layer. The paired contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}, a stricter check that is not part of the rule, point the same way. In the same window the mixture gives up part of each layer's agreement with its own family and exceeds the better layer on no family. The holdout takes up the remaining question of whether it has predictive content that the single layers lack.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:rank-divergence]{Rank divergence (H1 / RQ1)}

Hypothesis~H1 and RQ1 concern whether EndorseRank and AWP put the same contracts in different orders. The inter-method Kendall $\tau_b$ between the two score vectors is $0.174$, with interval $[0.163,0.185]$ (last row of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}), so the interval excludes both zero and one. The two rankings are related, yet far from interchangeable. Of the 114,103,171 contract pairs, 96.7\% are ordered by both scores, and 41.2\% of these are ordered in opposite directions.

Ties play almost no part in this result. The marginal score distributions are in Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}. EndorseRank gives 13,625 distinct scores to the 15,107 contracts and ties 0.17\% of the pairs; AWP gives 12,435 distinct scores and ties 3.13\%. Under each score the largest tie block is formed by the contracts that score zero: 535 under EndorseRank and 2,672 under AWP. They are the 523 and 2,670 contracts outside each graph, 80 of them outside both, and 12 and 2 contracts whose only pairs carry amounts too small for the value weight to differ from zero. Under EndorseRank 166 further contracts share one score, $c=7.64\times10^{-8}$, the score of a node without in-edges; they are in the allowance graph only as owners. Every other cohort contract has received at least one positive allowance and scores above $c$. A marginal distribution cannot show how two scores order the same contracts, so Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter} plots the two rankings against each other for a random sample of 3,000 contracts, with tied contracts at their mid-rank as in $\tau_b$. The zero blocks appear as a column at the right edge (EndorseRank) and a row at the top (AWP). Contracts that both scores rank near the top, or both near the bottom, lie close to the identity line; in between, the points spread over most of the square.

The zero blocks come from one rule of the pipeline: a contract missing from a graph scores zero (Section~\ref{sec:graph-construction}). The analysis plan fixed the alternative, keeping missing contracts as isolated nodes, as a sensitivity analysis, and Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} gives its result on the revised scores. No coefficient changes at the third decimal, and the agreement between the two scores stays at $0.174$ $[0.163,0.185]$. The reason is where the missing contracts land. Under EndorseRank's uniform restarts an isolated node receives $c$, the lowest score that any node of the graph can have, so the 523 contracts outside the allowance graph stay near the bottom of the order and join the 166 contracts at $c$. The 12 contracts whose only pairs carry zero weight are nodes of the allowance graph, so the rule does not add them, and the solver, which keeps only edges of positive weight, leaves them out; they keep the score zero. No pair changes direction: the pairs between the 523 and the 166 become ties, and the pairs between the 523 and the 12, tied at zero under the main rule, become ordered. Under AWP's restarts an isolated contract that sent nothing receives no restart mass and scores zero under both rules. Because the cohort was defined by approvals received, nearly every cohort contract is a spender in the allowance graph, and the rule for missing contracts decides almost nothing.

A protocol that relies on transfer-based scores cannot assume that its ordering of contracts survives a switch to allowance-based scores or a blend with them.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:robustness-results]{Robustness and sensitivity checks}

Three checks, run for EndorseRank and AWP, vary the damping factor, the slope of the value weight and the sample definition. The analysis plan fixed the damping values; the value-slope check takes the place of the plan's token check, which lost its point once every graph keeps five tokens (Section~\ref{sub:top-tokens}), and the sample-definition check was not in the plan. All three were run on the revised scores after the registered results were known. The first two checks hold the sample fixed and ask whether the results hinge on one setting. The third changes the sample itself, so its coefficients are expected to move, and it is not evidence of robustness. A fourth check asks how much EndorseRank depends on its restart rule.

Table~\ref{tab:holdout-spenders} sets every score computed at $t_1$, the two raw-degree baselines included, against both labels. Table~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} gives the paired contrasts: in its first block the two of rule~A, fixed in the analysis plan before any score existed and applied here to the revised scores, and in its second block those computed after the labels were known.

On the transfer side, AWP ranks new transfer senders at $\tau=0.346$ $[0.336,0.356]$, above the transfer in-degree at $t_1$ ($0.303$ $[0.292,0.315]$), with a paired difference of $+0.043$ $[0.038,0.048]$. The second finding is that the coupled operator fails rule~A on the revised scores, although in an exploratory comparison it improves on its transfer layer walked alone. Rule~A, fixed in the analysis plan before any score existed, asked C-PR at $\lambda=0.5$ to do no worse than C-PR ($\lambda=1$) on new approval pairs and better than C-PR ($\lambda=0$) on new transfer senders. Against the transfer walk alone on new approval pairs, a comparison that rule~A did not include, C-PR does better: it reaches $0.252$ $[0.241,0.262]$ against $0.160$ $[0.144,0.174]$, a paired difference of $+0.092$ $[0.080,0.105]$ on the same resamples. The seeded ablation shows where the allowance signal has to sit. S-PR is level with the transfer walk on new senders ($-0.001$, $[-0.004,0.002]$) and falls below the allowance walk on new approval pairs ($-0.051$, $[-0.066,-0.038]$). The S-PR contrasts and the increment of C-PR ($\lambda=1$) over its degree were computed after the labels were known; only the two contrasts of rule~A were named in the plan.

Two caveats apply to the whole table. Each label is the same construction carried into the future (new approvals, new senders), so none of these spender results bears on credit or loss; the trader labels below are built from neither edge type but are not credit labels either. Both labels are also sparse. Of the 17,050 spenders, 2,070 (12.1\%) gained a new approval pair and 1,695 (9.9\%) a new sender, and every coefficient rests largely on how these subsets are ordered.

Two other candidate labels, which the plan did not name, were computed on the holdout, and they are reported here without being offered as support. Revocation counts rise with every score, most with the raw in-approve degree ($0.388$ $[0.376,0.398]$), then with EndorseRank ($0.337$ $[0.327,0.347]$), and least with the walks over transfers, AWP ($0.191$ $[0.175,0.205]$) and C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($0.200$ $[0.185,0.214]$). The drain heuristic rises with every score as well, from $0.112$ $[0.096,0.126]$ for the transfer in-degree to $0.328$ $[0.318,0.338]$ for EndorseRank with activity restarts, with EndorseRank at $0.234$ $[0.224,0.245]$ and the in-approve degree at $0.214$ $[0.200,0.227]$. For an endorsement reading the sign is wrong, since a trusted spender should lose approvals less often, not more. The likelier reading is volume. A spender with many owners sees more of everything owners do, revocations and drains included, so these labels cannot separate trust from use.

In W1 the in-approve degree at the freeze reaches $\tau=0.394$ $[0.265,0.518]$ on the liquidation-free close rate and the transfer in-degree $0.310$ $[0.184,0.425]$, so P, their difference, is $+0.084$ $[-0.013,0.176]$, with a 97.5\% interval of $[-0.026,0.188]$ that includes zero. R3 applies. In W1, P lies below zero on the profitable share ($-0.196$, $[-0.364,-0.046]$) and on the non-loss share ($-0.252$, $[-0.391,-0.120]$), so, in the registered wording, the signal concerns forced-liquidation avoidance, not trading success. Part of the non-loss gap is built in. The pipeline counts a liquidated close as a close that was not a loss, so a trader with liquidations gains non-loss share, whereas the profitable share counts a liquidated close as not profitable; R3 therefore rests mainly on the profitable share. The other labels do not enter the decision (Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-grid}). On the no-liquidation flag, P is $+0.078$ $[-0.012,0.166]$ in W1 and $+0.197$ $[0.059,0.333]$ in W0. On profitable closes and realized gain, which grow with activity, the PageRank pairs favor the transfer side in W1, with intervals below zero for Q ($-0.231$, $[-0.429,-0.027]$, and $-0.350$, $[-0.536,-0.157]$), for EndorseRank against AWP ($-0.214$, $[-0.423,-0.012]$, and $-0.346$, $[-0.547,-0.144]$) and for C-PR's layers on realized gain ($-0.404$, $[-0.624,-0.182]$); in W1 the same three pairs also favor the transfer side on both shares (Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-grid}). P points the same way on the two count labels without being resolved, and in W0, among the pairs of an allowance-side and a transfer-side score, only C-PR's layers on realized gain are resolved ($-0.248$, $[-0.433,-0.067]$).

\dissertationSubheading[sec:empirical-claims]{Claims (C1--C6)}

The chapter's results support the claims below, cross-referenced to the hypothesis and research question columns of Table~\ref{tab:hrq-claim-map}. All of them rest on the revised analysis. The verdicts of rules~A and~B in C5 and of R1--R3 in C6 apply the registered rules to the revised scores, after the registered results were known, so they are a re-analysis; the registered analysis reached the same verdicts (Appendix~\ref{sec:map-registered}).

\begin{enumerate}
\item[C1.] Cost. On the full graphs of the same contracts the solver's time follows the edge count of its graph, and on the subsamples it does so from the stage of 10{,}000 contracts on. The allowance graph has 44.8\% of the transfer graph's edges (11,210,114 against 25,001,216 at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$), and EndorseRank's call takes correspondingly less time than AWP's: AWP takes 2.17 times as long (22.37~s on 7,882,356 nodes against 10.29~s on 6,219,063 nodes), at an edge ratio of 2.23 (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}). C-PR (36.16~s on the union of 9,151,010 nodes and 31,552,359 edges) and S-PR (34.42~s on 7,884,602 nodes and 25,018,892 edges) walk the transfer layer and take longer than AWP. The speed belongs to the sparser graph, not to the algorithm.
\item[C2.] Same-window agreement. Each score agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on, EndorseRank with the allowance family ($0.585$, $[0.578,0.592]$) and AWP with the transfer family ($0.572$, $[0.564,0.580]$), with intervals far from zero (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}). This is an intended-construct check, and the agreement is partly built in. With US-dollar amounts AWP follows the summed value of the inbound transfers almost as closely as their number of senders (in-value $0.566$, $[0.558,0.575]$; in-degree $0.578$, $[0.569,0.587]$). Every contract in the cohort received approvals of the five tokens, so EndorseRank leaves few ties (13,625 distinct scores, 0.17\% of pairs tied), and the isolated-node rule changes no coefficient (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}). The C-PR mixtures lie between their two layers on every family.
\item[C3.] Divergence. EndorseRank and AWP order the contracts differently, with $\tau_b=0.174$ $[0.163,0.185]$; 96.7\% of the pairs are ordered by both scores, and 41.2\% of those are discordant (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}, Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}).
\item[C4.] Degree baselines out of window. The two walks behave differently against the counts they smooth. AWP ranks later new transfer senders better than the raw transfer in-degree in both windows (W0 $+0.043$, $[0.038,0.048]$; W1 $+0.022$, $[0.017,0.027]$), so on the transfer side the walk, with its time and value weights and its activity restarts, added information to the count. EndorseRank, with the uniform restarts fixed in the plan, ranks new approval pairs below the raw in-approve degree in both windows (W0 $-0.019$, $[-0.026,-0.011]$; W1 $-0.034$, $[-0.038,-0.029]$). The variant with AWP's activity restarts ranks them above the degree in both windows (W0 $+0.059$, $[0.050,0.067]$; W1 $+0.027$, $[0.021,0.034]$), but that comparison was made after the labels were known and is a hypothesis, not a finding (Section~\ref{sub:endorserank-restarts}, Table~\ref{tab:fresh-posthoc}). All degree contrasts were computed after the labels were known; none was named in the plan or the registration. The increment of C-PR ($\lambda=1$) over the in-approve degree in W0 ($-0.027$, $[-0.034,-0.019]$) and the two S-PR contrasts were also computed after the labels. Restricting W0 to the 14,094 spenders that had received a transfer leaves both degree comparisons in place (EndorseRank $-0.013$, $[-0.020,-0.006]$; AWP $+0.056$, $[0.049,0.064]$; Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}).
\item[C5.] Coupling. On the revised scores, one walk over both edge types does not beat its layers walked alone. Rule~A fails: C-PR is no worse than the allowance walk on new approval pairs ($+0.029$, $[0.022,0.036]$) but worse than the transfer walk on new senders ($-0.034$, $[-0.040,-0.029]$), and its same-window part fails as well (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}). Rule~B is not met (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}). F1 holds ($+0.110$, $[0.098,0.122]$, over the transfer walk on new approval pairs), so the W0 gain of $+0.092$ ($[0.080,0.105]$), computed after the labels were known, recurs; F2 fails ($-0.028$, $[-0.033,-0.023]$, beyond the margin of 0.01); and F3 fails narrowly ($+0.000$, $[-0.014,0.010]$, on the traders' liquidation-free close rate, with equal point estimates of $0.350$ but a lower end below $-0.01$). With AWP as the comparator, in the first sensitivity analysis, F2 fails clearly ($-0.067$, $[-0.072,-0.061]$). In two W0 contrasts computed after the labels were known, S-PR is level with the transfer walk on new senders ($-0.001$, $[-0.004,0.002]$) and below the allowance walk on new approval pairs ($-0.051$, $[-0.066,-0.038]$): allowance edges help only when they carry the walk. Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results} reports where the hybrids stand within the window.
\item[C6.] Outcomes built from neither edge type. On the liquidation-free close rate of GMX~V2 contract traders, the count-level lead of the approval count over the transfer count (R1) is not supported in the primary window W1 (P $=+0.084$, Bonferroni 97.5\% interval $[-0.026,0.188]$; stratified $+0.101$, $[-0.024,0.231]$), although P is positive in W0 ($+0.182$, 97.5\% interval $[0.032,0.338]$). The PageRank lead (R2) is not supported (Q $=-0.041$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.148,0.064]$; in W0 $-0.014$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.161,0.151]$). R3 applies: the approval count ranks traders lower than the transfer count on the profitable share ($-0.196$, $[-0.364,-0.046]$) and the non-loss share ($-0.252$, $[-0.391,-0.120]$). Every trader that had received an approval avoided liquidation in both windows (37 of 37 and 40 of 40), against 72.7\% and 75.0\% of the others, but the samples are small (70 and 88 contract traders), and the transfer count also ranks this label ($0.310$, $[0.184,0.425]$, in W1) (Tables~\ref{tab:neutral-label-contrasts} and~\ref{tab:neutral-label-recipients}).
\end{enumerate}

The rank divergence answers RQ1, the same-window tables RQ2, the benchmark tables RQ3, and the holdouts RQ4 and RQ5; C6 adds no research question. The direct comparisons between EndorseRank and AWP are in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp} and Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-contrasts}, and none of them carries a claim of the thesis. Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} reads these findings against the main research question, what ERC-20 allowance edges add to PageRank as a reputation score for smart contracts on Arbitrum One compared with the transfer walk and with the raw degrees that both walks smooth, and against the objectives of Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}.
